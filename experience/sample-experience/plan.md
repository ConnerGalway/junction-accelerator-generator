## Goal
In 90 days, Foothills Quarter Horses will launch a half-day horsemanship experience for up to six guests at $145 per person. The experience will run every Saturday morning from late May through September, with the first paying departure on May 24, 2025. By the end of June, the ranch will have welcomed at least 12 paying guests and established a repeatable booking and operations workflow.

## The Experience
A half-day at the ranch where guests learn to read a horse, saddle up properly, and ride the same trails our family has worked for four generations.

## Strategic Positioning
This experience is for visitors who want more than a trail ride. They want to understand horses, not just sit on one. Foothills Quarter Horses is the only working ranch in the Crowsnest Pass offering hands-on horsemanship instruction from a third-generation rancher. This is not for guests looking for a quick photo opportunity or a passive scenic tour. It is for people who want to leave knowing something they did not know before.

## Welcome Message
You have been sharing your ranch with friends and neighbours for years. This plan turns that into a bookable experience that pays for itself. In 90 days, you will have your first paying guests in the saddle.

## Track 1: Define the Experience
Phase: Design
Short Label: Define It
Subtitle: Clarify exactly what guests will do, who they are, and why this experience is worth the drive.

### What We Heard From You
You mentioned that people always ask if they can "come see the horses" and you have been saying yes informally for years. You know the land and the animals better than anyone, but you have never had to explain the experience in a way that makes someone book online. You said you want guests to leave feeling like they learned something real, not just ticked a box.

### What This Is
This track is about defining the experience clearly enough that you can describe it in one sentence, price it confidently, and know exactly what happens from the moment guests arrive to the moment they leave. You will create a guest persona, map out the experience arc, and write a description that sells without overselling.

### Why It Matters
Everything else depends on this. You cannot price what you have not defined. You cannot market to someone you have not imagined. Getting the design right means operations and launch go smoothly. Skipping this step leads to confusion, underpricing, and guests who expected something different.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Define Your Guest

**1. Write your ideal guest persona**
- Describe who this experience is for: age range, where they are visiting from, what they are hoping to get out of the day
- Write down what they already know about horses (assume little to none)
- Note what would make them tell a friend about this experience afterward

**2. Identify who this is not for**
- List the types of guests who would be disappointed (e.g., thrill-seekers, those expecting a gallop)
- Write a sentence you could use to gently redirect the wrong guest before they book

#### Part B: Map the Experience Arc

**3. Outline the beginning, middle, and end**
- Write what happens in the first 15 minutes (arrival, welcome, safety brief)
- Write the core activity block (1.5–2 hours of instruction and riding)
- Write the closing (debrief, photos, departure)

**4. Define what is included and what is not**
- List inclusions: instruction, helmet, use of ranch horse, water, snack
- List exclusions: lunch, transportation to ranch, riding boots (guests bring their own or borrow)

#### Part C: Write the One-Sentence Description

**5. Draft your experience sentence**
- Write 3 versions of a one-sentence description
- Test each by asking: would this make someone click "Book Now"?
- Choose the strongest one and save it for your listing

### Success Metrics

| Metric | 30 Days | 60 Days | 90 Days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guest persona document complete | Yes | — | — |
| Experience arc mapped | Yes | — | — |
| One-sentence description finalised | — | Yes | — |

### Tips

- Your guest persona does not need to be fancy. A half-page is plenty.
- The experience arc should fit on a single sheet of paper. If it is longer, simplify.
- Test your one-sentence description on someone who has never visited the ranch. If they get it, you are done.

## Track 2: Make It Operable
Phase: Operations
Short Label: Make It Work
Subtitle: Set your price, plan for safety, and build the operational backbone so every departure runs smoothly.

### What We Heard From You
You said you have never charged for this before and are not sure what the right price is. You also mentioned that insurance is something you have been meaning to sort out but have not gotten around to. You want to make sure nothing goes wrong on a guest's first day, and you want the experience to be sustainable, not a favour you resent doing.

### What This Is
This track covers the operational side: calculating your costs, setting a price that works, confirming insurance and permits, writing a safety protocol, and creating a cancellation policy. By the end, you will have a clear picture of what each departure costs you and what you need to have in place before guests arrive.

### Why It Matters
Operations is where goodwill turns into a real business. If you underprice, you will burn out. If you skip the safety work, you are exposed. Getting this right means you can run the experience confidently, knowing you are covered and compensated fairly.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Calculate Your Costs

**1. List your fixed costs per departure**
- Fuel for truck and trailer (if transporting horses)
- Snacks and water for guests
- Wear and tear on tack and equipment

**2. Calculate your time cost**
- Estimate total hours per departure (including setup and cleanup)
- Assign an hourly rate you would be comfortable earning
- Multiply to get your labour cost per departure

**3. Add a margin for unexpected costs**
- Add 15–20% buffer for repairs, weather delays, or no-shows

#### Part B: Set Your Price

**4. Calculate your break-even point**
- Divide total cost by minimum number of guests (e.g., 2 guests)
- This is the lowest price you could charge without losing money

**5. Set your final per-guest price**
- Choose a price that covers costs, pays you fairly, and feels right for the market
- Confirm $145 per guest meets your targets

**6. Decide on a minimum booking policy**
- Set a minimum of 2 guests per departure, or offer a private option at a premium

#### Part C: Safety and Compliance

**7. Confirm your insurance coverage**
- Contact your broker and confirm you have commercial liability for guest activities
- Ask specifically about coverage for horseback riding instruction
- Get a copy of the certificate to show booking platforms

**8. Check permit and licence requirements**
- Contact the MD of Pincher Creek to confirm whether you need a business licence
- Confirm whether Alberta Tourism requires any registration for paid experiences

**9. Write your safety protocol**
- Document the safety briefing you give at the start
- List emergency procedures (nearest hospital, who to call, first aid location)
- Write a guest waiver and have it reviewed by your insurance broker

**10. Draft your cancellation and weather policy**
- Decide how far in advance guests can cancel for a full refund
- Write what happens if you cancel due to weather (full refund or reschedule)

### Tips

- Do not underprice to be "nice." Guests who value the experience will pay a fair rate.
- Your insurance broker is your friend. Call them before you launch, not after something goes wrong.
- A simple cancellation policy is better than a complicated one. Make it easy for guests to understand.

## Track 3: Make It Bookable
Phase: Launch
Short Label: Go Live
Subtitle: Get your listing live, your photos uploaded, and your first booking confirmed.

### What We Heard From You
You said you are not sure where to list the experience or how to get the word out. You have great photos of the ranch but nothing specifically shot for a booking page. You want this to be real, not just an idea you talk about at the coffee shop.

### What This Is
This track covers everything needed to go live: choosing a booking platform, writing listing copy that converts, getting photos taken, and launching to your first guests. By the end, you will have a live listing, a way to take bookings, and a plan for filling your first departures.

### Why It Matters
A great experience means nothing if no one can find it or book it. This track turns your designed, costed experience into something people can actually pay for. Launch is where the plan becomes real.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Choose Your Booking Platform

**1. Evaluate booking platform options**
- Compare Airbnb Experiences, FareHarbor, Peek, and direct booking via Square
- Consider fees, ease of use, and where your guests are already looking

**2. Create your platform account**
- Sign up for your chosen platform
- Complete your host profile with a photo and bio

#### Part B: Write Your Listing

**3. Write your listing headline**
- Use your one-sentence description as a starting point
- Keep it under 60 characters so it displays fully on mobile

**4. Write the full listing description**
- Open with what makes this experience unique
- Describe what guests will do (use the experience arc from Track 1)
- Close with practical details: duration, what to bring, meeting point

**5. Set your availability and pricing**
- Enter your $145 per-guest price
- Set your Saturday morning availability through September
- Configure your minimum booking (2 guests) and maximum (6 guests)

#### Part C: Photos and Launch

**6. Plan and shoot your listing photos**
- Schedule a morning shoot with good light
- Capture: the horses, the landscape, a guest learning to saddle, a riding shot, the ranch welcome area
- Aim for 8–12 photos minimum

**7. Upload photos and finalise listing**
- Add photos in the order that tells a story (arrival → activity → departure)
- Write alt text for accessibility
- Preview the listing on mobile before publishing

**8. Publish and share your listing**
- Hit publish and confirm the listing is live
- Share the link with your email list, social media, and local tourism contacts
- Ask three friends to share it with their networks

**9. Confirm your first booking**
- Follow up with anyone who shows interest
- Offer a friends-and-family rate for the first departure if you want a soft launch
- Confirm at least one booking before May 24

## Bookability Checklist

### Insurance Coverage Confirmed
No reputable booking platform will list you without proof of commercial liability coverage for guest activities.

### Business Licence Verified
The MD of Pincher Creek may require a home-based business licence for paid experiences. Confirm before you launch.

### Guest Waiver Ready
A signed waiver protects you and sets clear expectations. Have it reviewed by your insurance broker before your first guest arrives.

### Safety Protocol Documented
Your safety briefing and emergency procedures should be written down, not just in your head. Staff and helpers need to know the plan.

### Cancellation Policy Published
Guests need to know what happens if they cancel or if you cancel due to weather. Make it clear before they book.

### First Photos Uploaded
Photos sell experiences. A listing without photos will not convert. Aim for 8–12 images that show the horses, the land, and the activity.

### Availability Set Through September
Do not launch with just one date. Show guests that this is a real, ongoing experience with multiple opportunities to book.

### First Booking Confirmed
Your first paying guest is the milestone that matters. Do whatever it takes to get that first booking on the calendar before launch day.
