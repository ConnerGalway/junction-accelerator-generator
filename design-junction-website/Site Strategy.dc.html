Junction — Site strategy · Aug 2026

The website's one job: generate qualified leads.

Junction already generates attention — stages, The Brief, referrals, cold outreach. The site's one job is to convert that attention into qualified enquiries, and to turn your content volume into compounding lead capture.

1 · Who the site serves, in its first 10 seconds

Four visitors matter. Each arrives warm and needs a different first screen-full:

Post-talk DMO execNeeds instant confirmation this is the person from the stage, and one obvious "work with us" path. Homepage hero + proof, fast.
Referral validatorNeeds trust signals: named clients, real numbers, a credible team. Work page carries this visit.
Brief reader with budgetAlready sold on the thinking. Needs the bridge from content to services — every article ends in a relevant next step.
Cold searcherFound you via search. The content library is the front door — this is where "lead magnet" happens at scale.

2 · The conversion model

One primary conversion, four secondary ones — mapped straight to your 12-month targets:

Primary — the qualified enquiry

A structured "Start a conversation" form that asks who you are, what kind of organization, and what you're trying to achieve. Deliberate friction filters tire-kickers; routing (DMO / operator / AI training / speaking) means every enquiry lands pre-qualified. Target: 3 DMO + 1 AI-training enquiries/week, 1 speaking enquiry/week.

JunctionU free account
Product-led warm pool. Target: daily signups.
The Brief signup
The nurture engine. Target: 5/day. Present on every page, every article, every tool result.
Assessment completion
Tool leads (below) — email required for full results.
Speaking enquiry
Its own short form on the Speaking page — event, date, audience.

3 · The lead engines

You chose "2–3 tools, operated well." My recommendation on which, and why:

① The Digital Snapshot — free instant assessmentOperators → Accelerator

Enter your business name; get an instant mini-score across the checks the Accelerator audits (Google profile, reviews, site basics), plus one free fix. Full report requires email. It's the Accelerator's own methodology as a taster — the tool literally demonstrates the product. Highest-leverage build; this is the flagship.

② The Program Builder — DMO ROI calculatorDMOs → Partnership

A DMO enters region size, operator count and budget; gets a modelled program — seats funded, projected completion, economic upside (grounded in Yukon/Okotoks numbers) — and a shareable one-pager for their board. The output is the business case they need internally. Ends in the qualified enquiry form, pre-filled.

③ Interactive case studiesValidators → Trust

Explorable engagements: flip through an actual (anonymized) three-page plan, scrub a program timeline, watch the before/after numbers move. Cheap to build on your real deliverables, and no competitor shows their work this way.

Deliberately deferred: the free mini-course lives inside JunctionU as the free tier (it strengthens signups rather than competing with them); the client portal becomes phase 2 — show a read-only "what working with us looks like" screen inside the interactive case studies for now.

5 · Brand direction

"Trusted senior advisor with a contrarian edge" — calm surfaces, certain type, opinions in the copy. Synthesized from what you kept:

Colour
Deep green #114B3C becomes the Junction colour — panels, links, emphasis. Warm paper #F5F1E8 as surface, near-black ink, and gold #f2a900 kept from the design system strictly for action (CTAs, "ACTION" moments). Orange retires.
Type
Massive hero type stays — but split personality: heavy (800–900) for the big declarative moments, light (300) for everything advisory, per the design system's "confidence through restraint." Mono for wayfinding labels only.
Wayfinding, quieted
The junction/routes idea survives as structure and language: "pick your route," numbered services, the converging-routes motion graphic. The sign-panel costume retires. One signature motif per page, maximum.
Voice
Casual, direct, confident, friendly. Short sentences, specific numbers, named clients. Opinions stated plainly ("Most tourism marketing budgets leak at the same three spots"). Banned: contrast-reframe constructions, adjective triplets, puffery, and the standard AI vocabulary list — written into the project rules so it holds across every page.
