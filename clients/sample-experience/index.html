Track 1 · Design

Define the Experience

Clarify exactly what guests will do, who they are, and why this experience is worth the drive.

What We Heard From You

You mentioned that people always ask if they can "come see the horses" and you have been saying yes informally for years. You know the land and the animals better than anyone, but you have never had to explain the experience in a way that makes someone book online. You said you want guests to leave feeling like they learned something real, not just ticked a box.

What This Is

This track is about defining the experience clearly enough that you can describe it in one sentence, price it confidently, and know exactly what happens from the moment guests arrive to the moment they leave. You will create a guest persona, map out the experience arc, and write a description that sells without overselling.

Why It Matters

Everything else depends on this. You cannot price what you have not defined. You cannot market to someone you have not imagined. Getting the design right means operations and launch go smoothly. Skipping this step leads to confusion, underpricing, and guests who expected something different.

Part A Define Your Guest
  1. 1

    Write your ideal guest persona

    • Describe who this experience is for: age range, where they are visiting from, what they are hoping to get out of the day
    • Write down what they already know about horses (assume little to none)
    • Note what would make them tell a friend about this experience afterward
  2. 2

    Identify who this is not for

    • List the types of guests who would be disappointed (e.g., thrill-seekers, those expecting a gallop)
    • Write a sentence you could use to gently redirect the wrong guest before they book
Part B Map the Experience Arc
  1. 3

    Outline the beginning, middle, and end

    • Write what happens in the first 15 minutes (arrival, welcome, safety brief)
    • Write the core activity block (1.5-2 hours of instruction and riding)
    • Write the closing (debrief, photos, departure)
  2. 4

    Define what is included and what is not

    • List inclusions: instruction, helmet, use of ranch horse, water, snack
    • List exclusions: lunch, transportation to ranch, riding boots (guests bring their own or borrow)
Part C Write the One-Sentence Description
  1. 5

    Draft your experience sentence

    • Write 3 versions of a one-sentence description
    • Test each by asking: would this make someone click "Book Now"?
    • Choose the strongest one and save it for your listing

Tips
🎯
The guest persona is not a marketing exercise. It is a filter. When you are unsure whether to include something in the experience, ask: would my ideal guest want this?
✍️
Your one-sentence description should pass the "would I click on this?" test. If it sounds like every other horse experience, rewrite it until it does not.
⏱️
Time everything. The experience arc is not real until you have walked through it with a stopwatch. Guests notice when things run long or feel rushed.

Track 2 · Operations

Make It Operable

Set your price, plan for safety, and build the operational backbone so every departure runs smoothly.

What We Heard From You

You said you are comfortable with the horses but have never had to think about liability waivers, emergency procedures, or how to handle a guest who shows up unprepared. You have a rough sense of what the experience should cost but have not done the math to know if $145 covers your time and leaves a margin.

What This Is

This track turns your experience into something that can run repeatedly without you reinventing it each time. You will set your price, draft your waiver, write your safety briefing, and build a checklist for pre-departure setup. By the end, you will have an operations manual you can hand to a helper.

Why It Matters

Operations make the difference between a one-off event and a repeatable business. A clear price removes hesitation at checkout. A waiver protects you legally. A safety brief protects guests physically. A checklist ensures nothing gets forgotten on a busy Saturday morning. Without these, every departure is stressful. With them, you can relax and focus on the guest.

Part ASet Your Price
  1. 1

    Calculate your costs per departure

    • List fixed costs: insurance, equipment wear, snacks, water
    • List variable costs: anything that scales with guest count
    • Add your time at a fair hourly rate (e.g., $50/hour for 3 hours = $150)
  2. 2

    Set your per-guest price

    • Divide total cost by minimum guest count to find break-even
    • Add 30-50% margin to cover cancellations and slow weeks
    • Round to a clean number ($145, $150, $175)
Part BDraft Safety and Legal Documents
  1. 3

    Draft your liability waiver

    • Use a template from Travel Alberta or your insurance provider as a starting point
    • Include assumption of risk, release of liability, and photo release
    • Have a lawyer review it once before you launch
  2. 4

    Write your safety briefing script

    • Cover horse behaviour basics (approach from the side, no sudden movements)
    • Explain what to do if a horse spooks
    • State the emergency plan: who to call, where the first aid kit is
Part CBuild Your Operations Checklist
  1. 5

    Create a pre-departure checklist

    • Items to prepare: helmets, waivers, water, snacks, first aid kit
    • Horse prep: grooming, tack check, arena setup
    • Guest communication: confirmation email, directions, what to wear
  2. 6

    Create a post-departure checklist

    • Untack and cool down horses
    • Send thank-you email with photo and review request
    • Log any incidents or feedback for future improvement

Tips
💰
Do not underprice to fill spots. A higher price attracts guests who value the experience. A lower price attracts bargain hunters who may not be your ideal guest.
📋
The waiver is not optional. Even if you have been doing informal visits for years, the moment you charge money, the liability changes. Get it reviewed.
🗣️
Practice the safety briefing out loud. It should sound natural, not read from a script. Guests respond better to confidence than to perfection.

Track 3 · Launch

Make It Bookable

Get your listing live, your photos uploaded, and your first booking confirmed.

What We Heard From You

You said you have never listed anything on a booking platform before and are not sure where to start. You do not have professional photos of the ranch or the horses. You mentioned that you would rather take bookings directly than through a big platform, but you are open to whatever works.

What This Is

This track gets your experience visible and bookable. You will choose a booking platform, write your listing, take or gather photos, and publish. By the end, someone searching for "horse experience Crowsnest Pass" should be able to find you, read what you offer, and book a spot.

Why It Matters

You cannot get bookings if people cannot find you. The listing is how strangers discover what you offer. The photos are how they decide whether to click. The booking flow is how they commit. Without this track, you have an experience that only friends and word-of-mouth can access. With it, you have a business.

Part AChoose Your Booking Platform
  1. 1

    Evaluate booking platform options

    • Airbnb Experiences: largest audience, 20% commission, strong search visibility
    • FareHarbor/Peek: lower commission, more control, requires your own marketing
    • Direct booking (Google Form + e-transfer): zero commission, maximum effort
  2. 2

    Select one platform and create your account

    • For first-time operators, Airbnb Experiences is recommended for discoverability
    • Complete your host profile with a photo and bio
    • Verify your identity and payment information
Part BBuild Your Listing
  1. 3

    Write your listing copy

    • Use your one-sentence description as the headline
    • Write what guests will do, learn, and take away
    • Include logistics: duration, meeting point, what to bring, what is provided
  2. 4

    Gather or take photos

    • Minimum 5 photos: the ranch, the horses, you working with a horse, guests (or stand-ins) doing the activity, the setting/landscape
    • Natural light, no heavy filters, show real moments
    • If you do not have photos, schedule a shoot before you publish
Part CPublish and Get Your First Booking
  1. 5

    Set your availability and publish

    • Start with one day per week (Saturday mornings)
    • Block dates you cannot host
    • Publish the listing and confirm it appears in search
  2. 6

    Invite friends or family to be your first guests

    • Run the full experience as if they were paying strangers
    • Ask for honest feedback on timing, clarity, and enjoyment
    • Request a review if the platform allows it
  3. 7

    Confirm your first paying booking

    • Respond to inquiries within 24 hours
    • Send a confirmation message with directions and what to bring
    • Celebrate: you are officially in business

Tips
📸
Photos matter more than words. A listing with five mediocre photos will get fewer clicks than one with three great photos. Quality over quantity.
⏱️
Respond to inquiries within an hour if you can. Platforms reward fast response times with better search placement, and guests book whoever responds first.
⭐
Your first few reviews set the tone. Ask your test guests for honest, detailed feedback. A thoughtful 5-star review is worth more than ten generic ones.
