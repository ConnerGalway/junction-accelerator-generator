Digital Audit and Listings Cleanup

Get every platform accurate, consistent, and showing the full picture of what Karen's Cafe actually offers, before anything else goes on top of it.

What This Is

A one-time review and cleanup of every platform where Karen's Cafe appears online, followed by a simple monthly habit that keeps those listings accurate as the business changes. The primary platform is the Google Business Profile, which feeds directly into Google Maps, AI Overviews, and local search results. TripAdvisor and Yelp are secondary but meaningful, particularly for the US visitor segment Harrison Hot Springs attracts. This tactic is the precondition for Tactic 2 and Tactic 3: social content and website updates only compound if the listings they point to are complete and consistent.

What This Breaks Into
Google Business Profile
Claim access, complete all fields, add attributes, upload photos, respond to reviews
TripAdvisor & Yelp
Audit and update listings with matching descriptions and photos
Directory Consistency
Ensure NAP (Name, Address, Phone) is identical across all platforms
What We Heard From You

"The cafe has profiles on Google, TripAdvisor, and Yelp, but they have not been audited recently for accuracy or completeness. Previous paid marketing and hired website help did not produce clear results. None of the platforms currently mention the playroom, the crafting events, the event space, or the garden."

Why It Matters

When a visitor from Vancouver searches for things to do in Harrison Hot Springs, Google builds its recommendation from listing information, reviews, and website content. A business with an incomplete Google Business Profile does not appear for the category searches that would bring in new guests. Karen's Cafe is currently findable by people who already know its name. This tactic makes it findable by people who do not know it exists yet, but would absolutely come if they did.

Time Commitment
2-3 hrs one-time audit, then 15 min/month
  1. 1
    Claim and access the Google Business Profile
    • Go to business.google.com and sign in with a Google account. Search for Karen's Cafe & Gifts Harrison Hot Springs.
    • If the listing shows an "Own this business?" link, click it and follow the verification steps. Verification usually takes five to seven business days by postcard, or can be done by phone.
    • If access already exists, confirm you are signed in as the primary owner, not just a manager. The primary owner account controls everything. Do not lose access to it.
    • If a duplicate listing exists, do not create a new one. Use "Suggest an edit" to flag it for removal, or contact Google Business support.
    Go to Google Business Profile Manager
  2. 2
    Complete every section of the profile
    • Business name: Karen's Cafe & Gifts. Confirm it matches exactly what appears on the front door and the website.
    • Address and phone number: verify both are current. Even a single character difference from what appears on the website creates a NAP inconsistency that suppresses local search ranking.
    • Website URL: confirm it links to the current homepage, not a redirect or old URL.
    • Hours: update all regular hours and add any consistent seasonal changes. Include the days the cafe is closed (Mondays and Thursdays) so visitors are not misled.
    • Business description (750 characters): write a new description that names every major offering. Suggested: "Karen's Cafe & Gifts is Harrison Hot Springs' destination for high tea, breakfast, and lunch. Beyond the menu, the cafe offers a children's playroom, a private event and meeting room, hands-on crafting events including candle making, floral arranging, and felting, and a garden for weddings and gatherings of up to 100 guests. A cozy, unhurried space where visitors and locals stay longer than they planned."
    • Business category: primary category should be "Cafe." Add secondary categories: "Tea house," "Event venue," "Gift shop."
  3. 3
    Add business attributes
    • Under "Edit profile" and then "More," find the Attributes section. Enable every attribute that applies: "Good for kids" for the playroom, "Good for groups," "Private dining room available," "Outdoor seating" for the garden, "Family-friendly."
    • If Google offers a "High tea" or "Afternoon tea" attribute for the category, enable it. These feed directly into filtered Google Maps searches like "family-friendly cafes near me."
    • Enable the "From the business" section to add a short owner description. This is a good place for Karen's voice: "We've been welcoming guests to our little corner of Harrison for years. Whether it's a quiet cup of tea, a crafting afternoon, or a garden wedding, there's always something happening here."
  4. 4
    Upload photos to cover every major offering
    • The goal is a minimum of 10 photos before the audit is considered complete: tea service setup, food plating, the children's playroom, the garden, the event room set for a gathering, and a crafting event in progress.
    • Photo quality matters but does not need to be professional. Good natural light and a steady phone is enough. Google Business Profiles without photos receive significantly fewer clicks than profiles with even moderate-quality images.
    • Upload photos from the business Google account, not as a customer. Owner-uploaded photos appear higher in the photo section.
    • Remove any outdated or low-quality photos uploaded by previous account holders or through Google's automatic suggestions.
  5. 5
    Write review response templates and respond to existing reviews
    • In a Google Doc, write three template responses: one for an enthusiastic positive review, one for a general positive review, and one for a mixed or negative review. These are starting points to edit, not scripts to copy verbatim.
    • Positive template (enthusiastic): "Thank you so much for this. It genuinely made our day to read. We are so glad you discovered [specific thing they mentioned]. We hope to see you again soon, and please do bring someone next time who hasn't been."
    • Positive template (general): "Thank you for taking the time to leave a review. We are glad you enjoyed your visit, and we look forward to welcoming you back."
    • Negative or mixed template: "Thank you for sharing this with us. We are sorry [specific thing] was not what you were hoping for. We always want guests to leave feeling good about the time they spent here. Please reach out to us directly and we would like to make it right."
    • Respond to every review posted in the last six months that has not yet received a response. After that, aim to respond within five business days of any new review appearing.
  1. 6
    Audit and update TripAdvisor and Yelp
    • Go to tripadvisor.com and search for Karen's Cafe & Gifts. If an unclaimed listing exists, click "Is this your business?" Claiming is free.
    • On TripAdvisor, update: business name, address, phone, website, hours, description, and photos. The description should mirror the Google Business Profile description, adjusted for TripAdvisor's restaurant and attraction categories.
    • Go to biz.yelp.com and repeat the same process. Yelp is particularly relevant for the US visitor segment Harrison attracts. American visitors often check Yelp before Google.
    • After updating both platforms, do a side-by-side check: business name, phone number, and address should be character-for-character identical across Google, TripAdvisor, Yelp, and the website contact page. Any inconsistency suppresses local search ranking.

    • Search "Karen's Cafe & Gifts Harrison Hot Springs" in Google and scroll through the first two pages of results. Note any directory listings that appear: Yellow Pages, Foursquare, Facebook, Canada 411.

Social Media Content System

Build the content pillars, the asset library, and the scheduling routine that let a social media helper post consistently without starting from scratch every week.

What This Is

A social media content system for Karen's Cafe has three components: a set of defined content pillars that tell the social media helper what to post, a content capture workflow that builds a library of usable material during normal business hours, and a simple scheduling routine in Meta Business Suite that batches posting in advance rather than requiring real-time decisions every day. The system is designed specifically to be handed to someone who is not Karen and executed without her involvement once the pillars and the library are established.

What This Breaks Into
Content Pillars
Five defined pillars: Tea and Table, Playroom, Events and Crafting, Garden, Gifts
Content Library
Google Drive folder with organised photos and videos from weekly capture sessions
Platform Setup & Scheduling
Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram; TikTok for the 25-40 demographic
What We Heard From You

"Social media posting has been inconsistent. Karen manages almost everything herself and does not feel comfortable with the platforms. One staff member knows Instagram and can post when asked, and a young local artisan may be willing to take on a part-time social media role. The challenge is giving that person a system specific enough that the content reflects the business well."

Why It Matters

Karen's Cafe has five distinct experiences to sell, and each one appeals to a slightly different visitor or local. A content system with defined pillars ensures each of those experiences gets regular attention rather than only the most photogenic. The 20-to-40 demographic Karen wants to attract is discovering cafes through Instagram saves, TikTok recommendations, and friend shares. The only way to be present in that discovery path is to post consistently in the formats those platforms reward.

Time Commitment
2-3 hrs content capture & scheduling, per week
  1. 1
    Define the five content pillars
    • Print the table below and give a copy to whoever manages posting. Every time a post needs to go out and there is no obvious idea, pick a pillar, find a moment happening in the cafe this week, and match them.
    • Tea and the Table (2x per week): High tea setup, scone and baking process, table styling, seasonal menus, today's tea posts. Example: Short video of scones coming out of the oven with caption "Fresh this morning. Come in today and they'll still be warm."
    • The Playroom (1x per week): Children playing, the playroom space, the concept of tea for mum and play for kids, parents relaxing. Example: Photo or short video with caption "Bring the kids. We'll look after them. You focus on the tea."
    • Events and Crafting (1-2x per week): Candle making sessions in progress, floral arrangements, felting closeups, completed projects, upcoming booking announcements. Example: Reel of a candle-making session with caption "Our next candle making evening is [date]. DM to reserve a spot."
    • The Garden (1x per week in season): Garden space, outdoor tea settings, wedding setups, seasonal flowers, garden is open announcements. Example: Garden photo with caption "Garden season is here. Tea outside is always better."
    • Gifts and the Shop (2x per month): Jewellery from the artisan, gift items, seasonal displays, perfect gift for prompts. Example: Flat-lay of jewellery with caption "A local maker, sold right here. Come meet the collection."
    • The Playroom pillar is the most strategically important one for reaching the 20-to-40 demographic Karen wants to attract. It should appear in content every single week.
  1. 2
    Set up a shared content library
    • Create a Google Drive folder named "Karen's Social Content" with subfolders for each pillar: Tea and Table, Playroom, Events and Crafting, Garden, Gifts.
    • Add a "Ready to Post" folder where approved content moves once it is captioned and ready.
    • Grant edit access to whoever will be posting (the staff member or the young local artisan).
    • All content capture from this point goes into the Drive folder, not to personal phone camera rolls.
  2. 3
    Build a weekly capture routine
    • Pick two 30-minute windows each week during operating hours. The best moments are not staged: tea service going out to a table, a child showing their parent something in the playroom, a candle-making participant finishing their piece.
    • Video matters more than stills. A 15-second Reel of scones coming out of the oven performs better on Instagram than a perfect photograph. Shoot vertical (9:16) for Reels and TikTok.
    • When an event is scheduled, add 15 minutes to the setup and breakdown time for content capture.
    • Target 10 photos and 3 short clips per week as a sustainable capture rate. Anything beyond that is a bonus.
    • Guest consent: for any content showing identifiable people, get verbal consent before posting. A simple "Do you mind if we share this on our social media?" is enough.
  3. 4
    Do an initial content capture day
    • Before the posting routine begins, spend one full operating day capturing a foundation library: every space, every offering, every setup. This gives the social media helper something to draw from even in weeks where live capture is thin.
    • Prioritize the playroom, which currently has zero photos anywhere online.
    • Capture the garden while it is still in season.
    • Aim for 50 photos and 10 clips from this single session. This creates a two-month buffer.
  1. 5
    Audit the current Instagram and Facebook accounts
    • Log in to the existing Instagram account. Confirm Karen still has access and the email on the account is current. If access has been lost, start the recovery process immediately.
    • Check whether the Instagram account is a personal account or a business account. If personal, convert it to a business account to unlock scheduling and analytics.
    • Log in to the existing Facebook page. Confirm the page is claimed, active, and linked to the Instagram account via Meta Business Suite.
    • Update bio on both platforms to mention: high tea, the playroom, crafting events, the garden for weddings, and Harrison Hot Springs as the location.
    Meta Business Suite
  2. 6
    Create a TikTok account for the cafe
    • TikTok is the single best platform for reaching the 25-to-40 demographic discovering local experiences. The playroom content, in particular, will resonate with parents on TikTok.
    • Register the account with a business email Karen owns, not a personal phone number.
    • Use the same handle as Instagram if it is available, or a close variant.
    • The account does not need to be active immediately, but it needs to exist so that content can begin flowing to it in Month 2.
    TikTok for Business
  3. 7
    Establish a weekly posting cadence
    • The minimum sustainable cadence is 4 posts per week across platforms: 3 on Instagram/Facebook (mirrored) and 1 on TikTok.
    • Set a recurring schedule: post Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday on Instagram/Facebook, and Friday on TikTok.
    • Use Meta Business Suite to schedule the Instagram and Facebook posts up to a week in advance. This decouples posting from the day it needs to appear.
    • Every post should be tagged by pillar in the caption (e.g. "#KarensPlayroom", "#KarensCrafting") to help track which pillars are getting attention over time.
    Scheduling posts in Meta Business Suite
  4. 8
    Handoff to the social media helper
    • Once the content pillars document, the Google Drive folder, and the platform access are set up, the helper has everything they need to post without Karen making decisions each time.
    • Brief the helper in one session: walk through the five pillars, show them the Drive folder structure, demonstrate how to schedule a post in Meta Business Suite, and explain the weekly cadence.
    • For the first two weeks, review posts before they go live. After that, give the helper autonomy and review only if something seems off.
    • If the helper is the young local artisan (on a part-time basis), confirm compensation and expectations in writing before the handoff.

    • If the login credentials are unknown, use the "Lost your password?" link to send a reset email to the email address that was used when the site was created.
    • If neither works, contact the hosting provider. The cafe's hosting provider can be identified by running a WHOIS lookup on the domain. The hosting provider can reset WordPress admin credentials with proof of account ownership.

    • Once logged in, go to Pages and try to edit an existing page. Confirm you can change text and save the changes.
    • Check whether the site uses a page builder (Elementor, Divi, WPBakery) or the default WordPress block editor. The editing process differs significantly depending on which one.

  1. 3
    Write the FAQ content
    • The FAQ needs to answer the questions that humans ask Google and that AI tools answer when someone types "where can I get afternoon tea near Harrison Hot Springs" into ChatGPT.
    • Core questions to answer: Do you need a reservation for high tea? Is the playroom supervised? What crafting events do you offer? Can you host private events? Can you host weddings? Where are you located? What are your hours? Are you family-friendly?
    • Write each answer in a single paragraph that fully answers the question. AI tools and Google's featured snippets pull content that directly answers the query in one block.
    • Use the question as the heading and the answer as a paragraph beneath it. This is the schema format that Google understands as FAQ content.
  2. 4
    Publish the FAQ page
    • Create a new page in WordPress titled "Frequently Asked Questions" or "FAQ."
    • Add the questions and answers. Use H2 or H3 headings for the questions, and regular paragraph text for the answers.
    • Link the FAQ page from the main navigation menu. An FAQ hidden in the footer will not be found by visitors or AI tools.
    • If the WordPress theme or page builder has an accordion or toggle block, use it. This is the standard visual format for FAQ pages and makes long lists easier to scan.
  1. 5
    Create the Playroom page
    • The playroom is Karen's most distinctive offering and currently does not exist anywhere online. This is the single highest-priority page.
    • Content to include: what the playroom is, what ages it is designed for, whether it is supervised or parent-present, what toys and activities are available, and that parents can enjoy tea while children play.
    • Add at least 2 photos from the content capture day in Tactic 2.
    • Title the page "Children's Playroom" and include "Harrison Hot Springs" in the page title or first paragraph so it can be found in location-based searches.
  2. 6
    Create the Crafting Events page
    • Content to include: what crafting events are offered (candle making, floral arranging, felting), how often they run, how to book a spot, pricing if consistent, and what is included.
    • Include at least one photo of a crafting session in progress from the Tactic 2 content library.
    • If there is a recurring schedule, include it. If events are announced ad hoc, say "Follow us on Instagram for upcoming dates."
    • Title the page "Crafting Events at Karen's Cafe" or similar, and include Harrison Hot Springs in the first paragraph.
  3. 7
    Create the Private Events and Meeting Room page
    • Content to include: what the private space can be used for (meetings, birthday parties, showers, small corporate events), capacity, what catering is available, and how to enquire or book.
    • Include a photo of the event room set up for a gathering from the Tactic 2 content library.
    • If pricing is standardised, include a starting price or a "from $X for a two-hour booking" reference. If pricing varies, say "Contact us for a quote."
    • Title the page "Private Events and Meeting Space" with Harrison Hot Springs in the content.
  4. 8
    Create the Garden Weddings page
    • Content to include: the garden space, capacity (up to 100 guests), what is included in a rental, catering options, and how to enquire.
    • Include at least 2 photos of the garden. If any past weddings have been hosted, ask the couples for permission to use a photo.
    • This page will compete for "Harrison Hot Springs wedding venue" searches. Use that phrase naturally in the content.
    • Include a clear call to action: "Contact us to schedule a site visit" or "Email us to discuss your date."
  5. 9
    Add all new pages to the navigation menu
    • In WordPress, go to Appearance and then Menus. Find the main navigation menu.
    • Add the new pages: Playroom, Crafting Events, Private Events, Garden Weddings, and FAQ.
    • Group them logically. One approach: a "Things to Do" dropdown containing Playroom, Crafting Events, and Private Events, and a separate "Garden Weddings" link for that audience.
    • Pages that are not in the navigation are effectively invisible to most visitors. Do not skip this step.

Tips
  • The playroom page is the single most important page in this tactic. It is Karen's most distinctive offering, it is currently invisible online, and parents are the exact demographic the cafe wants to attract. Prioritise it over everything else.
  • Write the FAQ in the voice of someone answering a real question from a real customer. AI tools prefer direct, helpful language over marketing copy. "Yes, we can host private events" is better than "Our elegant private event space is available for discerning guests."
  • If WordPress access cannot be recovered quickly, do not stall the other tactics waiting. The GBP description in Tactic 1 and the social content in Tactic 2 can carry the message until the site is updatable.
  • Test AI visibility yourself. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google "where can I get afternoon tea in Harrison Hot Springs" before and after publishing the new pages. This is the simplest way to see whether the content is being picked up.
  • Pages that are not in the navigation might as well not exist. The single most common mistake is publishing a page and forgetting to add it to the menu.
