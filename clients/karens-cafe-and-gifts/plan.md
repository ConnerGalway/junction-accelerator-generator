## Goal

Karen's Cafe & Gifts in Harrison Hot Springs is already beloved. Guests describe it as going to a grandmother's house, word of mouth brings people in from Vancouver, Chilliwack, Mission and beyond, and the experience earns real loyalty every time someone walks through the door. The problem is that almost none of that warmth is visible before someone arrives. The full offering -- a children's playroom, a private event room, crafting evenings, a garden that holds up to 100 guests, a gift shop -- is invisible online. The current WordPress website does not mention the playroom. There is no dedicated bar or event section. Past attempts at Google Ads and hired website help did not produce clear results because the foundation they needed was not in place. The next ninety days fix that. The goal is to make Karen's Cafe as discoverable online as it is well-regarded in person: accurate and consistent listings across every platform, a social media content system that runs without Karen, and a website that answers the questions visitors are already asking AI tools and search engines before they plan a trip to Harrison.

Harrison Hot Springs draws visitors from the Fraser Valley and beyond for weekends and day trips, but the cafe and restaurant options in town are limited. Karen's Cafe has five distinct experiences that no competitor in Harrison offers in the same building: high tea from scratch, a children's playroom that gives parents a real break, hands-on crafting evenings, a private event room, and a garden that seats a wedding. The visitors the cafe wants more of -- younger families, groups planning celebrations, couples looking for a date night -- are discovering businesses through Instagram saves, TikTok recommendations, and AI-generated travel suggestions, not Yellow Pages. The positioning opportunity is to be the place every Harrison visitor hears about before they arrive, not just after.

## Tactic 1: Digital Audit and Listings Cleanup
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Listings & Reviews
Subtitle: Get every platform accurate, consistent, and showing the full picture of what Karen's Cafe actually offers -- before anything else goes on top of it.

### What We Heard From You

The cafe has profiles on Google, TripAdvisor, and Yelp, but they have not been audited recently for accuracy or completeness. Previous paid marketing and hired website help did not produce clear results, which is consistent with a missing foundation: driving traffic to an incomplete or inaccurate digital presence does not convert. None of the platforms currently mention the playroom, the crafting events, the event space, or the garden, because those offerings have never been systematically communicated anywhere online. The Google Business Profile is the most important place to fix first, and it is completely free to do.

### What This Is

A one-time review and cleanup of every platform where Karen's Cafe appears online, followed by a simple monthly habit that keeps those listings accurate as the business changes. The primary platform is the Google Business Profile, which feeds directly into Google Maps, AI Overviews, and local search results. TripAdvisor and Yelp are secondary but meaningful, particularly for the US visitor segment Harrison Hot Springs attracts. This tactic is the precondition for Tactic 2 and Tactic 3: social content and website updates only compound if the listings they point to are complete and consistent.

### Why It Matters

When a visitor from Vancouver searches for things to do in Harrison Hot Springs, Google builds its recommendation from listing information, reviews, and website content. A business with an incomplete Google Business Profile -- missing attributes, no photos of the playroom or garden, a description that only says "cafe" -- does not appear for the category searches that would bring in new guests. Karen's Cafe is currently findable by people who already know its name. This tactic makes it findable by people who do not know it exists yet, but would absolutely come if they did.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Google Business Profile

1. **Claim and access the Google Business Profile.**
   - Go to business.google.com and sign in with a Google account. Search for Karen's Cafe & Gifts Harrison Hot Springs.
   - If the listing shows an "Own this business?" link, click it and follow the verification steps. Verification usually takes five to seven business days by postcard, or can be done by phone.
   - If access already exists, confirm you are signed in as the primary owner, not just a manager. The primary owner account controls everything. Do not lose access to it.
   - If a duplicate listing exists, do not create a new one. Use "Suggest an edit" to flag it for removal, or contact Google Business support.

2. **Complete every section of the profile.**
   - Business name: Karen's Cafe & Gifts. Confirm it matches exactly what appears on the front door and the website.
   - Address and phone number: verify both are current. Even a single character difference from what appears on the website creates a NAP (Name, Address, Phone) inconsistency that suppresses local search ranking.
   - Website URL: confirm it links to the current homepage, not a redirect or old URL.
   - Hours: update all regular hours and add any consistent seasonal changes. Include the days the cafe is closed -- Karen closes Mondays and Thursdays -- so visitors are not misled.
   - Business description (750 characters): write a new description that names every major offering. A suggested version: "Karen's Cafe & Gifts is Harrison Hot Springs' destination for high tea, breakfast, and lunch. Beyond the menu, the cafe offers a children's playroom, a private event and meeting room, hands-on crafting events including candle making, floral arranging, and felting, and a garden for weddings and gatherings of up to 100 guests. A cozy, unhurried space where visitors and locals stay longer than they planned." Adjust to 750 characters or fewer.
   - Business category: primary category should be "Cafe." Add secondary categories: "Tea house," "Event venue," "Gift shop." Multiple categories make the listing appear in more filtered searches.

3. **Add business attributes.**
   - Under "Edit profile" and then "More," find the Attributes section. Enable every attribute that applies: "Good for kids" for the playroom, "Good for groups," "Private dining room available," "Outdoor seating" for the garden, "Family-friendly."
   - If Google offers a "High tea" or "Afternoon tea" attribute for the category, enable it. These feed directly into filtered Google Maps searches like "family-friendly cafes near me."
   - Enable the "From the business" section to add a short owner description. This is a good place for Karen's voice: "We've been welcoming guests to our little corner of Harrison for years. Whether it's a quiet cup of tea, a crafting afternoon, or a garden wedding, there's always something happening here."

4. **Upload photos to cover every major offering.**
   - The goal is a minimum of 10 photos before the audit is considered complete: tea service setup, food plating, the children's playroom, the garden, the event room set for a gathering, and a crafting event in progress.
   - Photo quality matters but does not need to be professional. Good natural light and a steady phone is enough. Google Business Profiles without photos receive significantly fewer clicks than profiles with even moderate-quality images.
   - Upload photos from the business Google account, not as a customer. Owner-uploaded photos appear higher in the photo section.
   - Remove any outdated or low-quality photos uploaded by previous account holders or through Google's automatic suggestions.

5. **Write review response templates and respond to existing reviews.**
   - In a Google Doc, write three template responses: one for an enthusiastic positive review, one for a general positive review, and one for a mixed or negative review. These are starting points to edit, not scripts to copy verbatim.
   - Positive template (enthusiastic): "Thank you so much for this. It genuinely made our day to read. We are so glad you discovered [specific thing they mentioned]. We hope to see you again soon, and please do bring someone next time who hasn't been."
   - Positive template (general): "Thank you for taking the time to leave a review. We are glad you enjoyed your visit, and we look forward to welcoming you back."
   - Negative or mixed template: "Thank you for sharing this with us. We are sorry [specific thing] was not what you were hoping for. We always want guests to leave feeling good about the time they spent here. Please reach out to us directly at [email or phone] and we would like to make it right."
   - Respond to every review posted in the last six months that has not yet received a response. After that, aim to respond within five business days of any new review appearing.

#### Part B: TripAdvisor, Yelp, and Directory Consistency

6. **Audit and update TripAdvisor and Yelp.**
   - Go to tripadvisor.com and search for Karen's Cafe & Gifts. If an unclaimed listing exists, click "Is this your business?" Claiming is free.
   - On TripAdvisor, update: business name, address, phone, website, hours, description, and photos. The description should mirror the Google Business Profile description, adjusted for TripAdvisor's restaurant and attraction categories.
   - Go to biz.yelp.com and repeat the same process. Yelp is particularly relevant for the US visitor segment Harrison attracts. American visitors often check Yelp before Google.
   - After updating both platforms, do a side-by-side check: business name, phone number, and address should be character-for-character identical across Google, TripAdvisor, Yelp, and the website contact page. Any inconsistency -- even a missing abbreviation or different street format -- suppresses local search ranking.

7. **Check and correct any other directory listings.**
   - Search "Karen's Cafe & Gifts Harrison Hot Springs" in Google and scroll through the first two pages of results. Note any directory listings that appear: Yellow Pages, Foursquare, Facebook, Canada 411.
   - For any directory where information is incorrect or outdated, click through and update it or flag it for correction.
   - The Harrison Tourism website is worth checking specifically: confirm Karen's Cafe appears in the local dining or attractions listings and that the link points to the current website URL.

### Tips
- The Google Business Profile is the single most important platform to get right. Spend the majority of the audit time there. TripAdvisor and Yelp matter, but at a fraction of the impact.
- The children's playroom is Karen's most distinctive and most searchable feature. A cafe with a supervised children's play space in a day-trip destination town is genuinely unusual. It needs to appear prominently in the GBP description, in the attributes, and in a dedicated photo. Currently it appears nowhere online.
- Do not create a new Google Business Profile if one already exists, even if it is incomplete. Duplicate listings are a common local SEO problem and they suppress both entries. Claim and fix what is there.
- Review responses are indexed by search engines and read by AI tools. Responses that naturally mention "high tea in Harrison," "the playroom," or "crafting events" add to the listing's relevance for those searches, without needing to stuff keywords artificially.
- Set a calendar reminder for the first of each month to spend 15 minutes checking the GBP: are the hours still accurate, have new reviews appeared, are there any public questions that need answering?

## Tactic 2: Social Media Content System
Phase: Content and Audience
Short Label: Content & Social
Subtitle: Build the content pillars, the asset library, and the scheduling routine that let a social media helper post consistently without starting from scratch every week.

### What We Heard From You

Social media posting has been inconsistent. Karen manages almost everything in the business herself, does not feel comfortable with the platforms, and there has been no plan for what to post beyond what feels relevant on a given day. One staff member knows Instagram and can post when asked, and a young local artisan who sells jewellery in the space may be willing to take on a part-time social media role. The challenge is not finding someone to post. It is giving that person a system specific enough that the content reflects the business well, rather than producing generic food photos that look like every other cafe's account. The goal of attracting a younger adult demographic -- 20 to 40 -- is well-matched to Instagram and TikTok, but the content has to show the experiences that demographic would actually save and share: crafting nights, the playroom concept, tea service aesthetics.

### What This Is

A social media content system for Karen's Cafe has three components: a set of defined content pillars that tell the social media helper what to post, a content capture workflow that builds a library of usable material during normal business hours, and a simple scheduling routine in Meta Business Suite that batches posting in advance rather than requiring real-time decisions every day. The system is designed specifically to be handed to someone who is not Karen and executed without her involvement once the pillars and the library are established.

### Why It Matters

Karen's Cafe has five distinct experiences to sell -- tea, the playroom, events and crafting, the garden, and the gift shop -- and each one appeals to a slightly different visitor or local. A content system with defined pillars ensures each of those experiences gets regular attention rather than only the most photogenic, which left to chance is usually just the food. The 20-to-40 demographic Karen wants to attract is not primarily discovering cafes through Google search. They are discovering them through Instagram saves, TikTok recommendations, and friend shares. The only way to be present in that discovery path is to post consistently in the formats those platforms reward.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Content Pillars

1. **Define the five content pillars.**
   - Print the table below and give a copy to whoever manages posting. Every time a post needs to go out and there is no obvious idea, pick a pillar, find a moment happening in the cafe this week, and match them. The post is that simple.

   | Pillar | What It Covers | Frequency | Example Post |
   |--------|----------------|-----------|--------------|
   | Tea and the Table | High tea setup, scone and baking process, table styling, seasonal menus, today's tea posts | 2x per week | Short video of scones coming out of the oven. Caption: "Fresh this morning. Come in today and they'll still be warm." |
   | The Playroom | Children playing, the playroom space, the concept of tea for mum and play for kids, parents relaxing | 1x per week | Photo or short video of the playroom. Caption: "Bring the kids. We'll look after them. You focus on the tea." |
   | Events and Crafting | Candle making sessions in progress, floral arrangements, felting closeups, completed projects, upcoming booking announcements | 1-2x per week | Reel of a candle-making session from start to finish. Caption: "Our next candle making evening is [date]. DM to reserve a spot." |
   | The Garden | Garden space, outdoor tea settings, wedding setups, seasonal flowers, garden is open announcements | 1x per week in season | Garden photo on a sunny day. Caption: "Garden season is here. Tea outside is always better." |
   | Gifts and the Shop | Jewellery from the artisan, gift items, seasonal displays, perfect gift for prompts | 2x per month | Flat-lay of jewellery pieces. Caption: "A local maker, sold right here. Come meet the collection." |

   - The Playroom pillar is the most strategically important one for reaching the 20-to-40 demographic Karen wants to attract. Young parents with small children are specifically looking for places that accommodate both them and their kids gracefully. This is a genuinely unusual offering in a day-trip destination town and it should appear in content every single week.
   - The Events and Crafting pillar serves a dual purpose: it promotes specific bookable events and it positions the cafe as a place where things happen, not just a place to eat. Crafting event content consistently outperforms standard food content on saves and shares among the target demographic.

#### Part B: Content Capture Workflow

2. **Set up the content library.**
   - Create a Google Drive folder called "Karen's Cafe Content Library." Inside it, create five sub-folders matching the pillars: Tea and Table, Playroom, Events and Crafting, Garden, Gifts and Shop.
   - The social media helper's first task, before a single post goes live, is to spend one or two hours during a normal service day capturing assets: phone photos and short videos in each pillar category. The goal is a minimum of 20 photos and 5 short video clips before posting begins.
   - File naming convention: [pillar]-[subject]-[date]. Example: tea-scones-20260601.jpg or playroom-kids-20260605.mov. Consistent naming makes the library searchable and prevents the disorganised photo dump that makes most content libraries unusable within a month.
   - Capture priority for the first two weeks: playroom content first (currently zero photos of this exist online), crafting session content second, tea service third. These three categories have the largest gap between their actual appeal and their current online visibility.

3. **Build a weekly capture habit.**
   - Ask the social media helper to spend 10 to 15 minutes during each shift capturing two to three photos or a short video clip. Not editing, not posting. Just capturing and uploading to the correct Google Drive sub-folder before they leave.
   - During crafting events, process video is the priority. A 15-to-30 second clip of candle wicks being placed, wax being poured, or a finished arrangement being held up generates significantly more engagement and saves than a static photo of the final product.
   - During tea service, the moment to capture is the plating or the pour, not the finished table after guests have started. The aesthetic of a freshly poured pot of tea next to a three-tier stand of scones and sandwiches is exactly what the target demographic saves for future reference.
   - For the playroom, capture during natural moments when children are engaged and parents are relaxed. Do not stage it. A genuine moment of a toddler at the play table while a parent sips tea in the adjacent room is more compelling than any arranged photo.

#### Part C: Platform Setup and Scheduling

4. **Set up Meta Business Suite for scheduling.**
   - Go to business.facebook.com and confirm that both the Karen's Cafe Facebook Page and Instagram account are connected under the same Business Suite account. If they are not linked, go to Settings, then Accounts, then Instagram, and connect the Instagram account.
   - Meta Business Suite's Planner tool allows posts to be scheduled for both Facebook and Instagram simultaneously. The social media helper should batch-create and schedule one week of posts at a time, ideally on Monday or Tuesday morning for the week ahead.
   - A sustainable weekly posting schedule: Tuesday for Tea and Table, Thursday for Playroom or Events and Crafting, Saturday for Garden, Gifts, or an event reminder. This three-post-per-week cadence gives the algorithm enough regularity to build reach without burning out the helper.
   - Stories on Instagram require no planning. They are the spontaneous layer. Encourage the helper to post one or two Stories per day during their shift: a plate going out, a view of the garden, a crafting table being set up. Stories disappear after 24 hours and carry no production pressure.

5. **Launch TikTok.**
   - Create a TikTok Business account at business.tiktok.com using the same email address associated with Karen's Cafe. Confirm the username matches the Instagram handle for brand consistency.
   - TikTok does not require separate content. The same Reels produced for Instagram can be downloaded without the Instagram watermark using CapCut or by saving before uploading to Instagram, and then posted directly to TikTok.
   - The crafting event content and the playroom concept are both well-suited to TikTok's current recommendation patterns for the 25-to-40 demographic. A 20-to-30-second clip of a candle making session with a simple text overlay saying "we run these evenings every month in Harrison Hot Springs" is exactly the format that surfaces in travel planning and things-to-do searches on TikTok.
   - TikTok posting can start at one to two times per week and stay there for the first 90 days. The goal is establishing the account and testing what content resonates, not volume.

6. **Brief the social media helper.**
   - Brand voice in two sentences: "Karen's Cafe is warm, unhurried, and a little bit like your grandmother's house -- but in the best possible way. We write the way Karen talks: friendly, genuine, and never salesy."
   - What to post: any moment from the five content pillars that feels authentic. The playroom, the crafting sessions, the tea service, the garden, the gifts.
   - What not to post: stock images, heavily filtered or over-edited photos, promotional graphics that look like advertising, posts that apologise for being quiet or explain the absence of posts.
   - How to write captions: short, specific, direct. Name what is in the photo or video. Add one sentence that invites action or creates a reason to visit. End with a practical detail when relevant, such as hours, event date, or a booking prompt.
   - Who to tag: the local artisan when posting jewellery content, Harrison Hot Springs Tourism when posting garden or destination content, local suppliers or makers if any ingredients or products are locally sourced.

### Tips
- Karen's concern about over-posting is understandable, but it no longer matches how social media algorithms work in 2026. The platforms now show content based on interest and behaviour, not reverse-chronological order. A post does not land on every follower's feed simultaneously the way it did in 2015. Posting three times per week is not bombardment. It is the minimum needed to maintain visibility.
- The playroom is the most shareable thing about Karen's Cafe by a significant margin. A parent who discovers a cafe where their children can play independently while the adults have a proper tea experience will tell every other parent they know. Social content showing this clearly -- not described, but demonstrated -- is what drives that word-of-mouth into the digital space.
- Do not brief the social media helper with "just post whatever looks good." That instruction produces content that reflects the helper's taste, not the business's identity. The content pillars table and the brand voice description are the brief. Give them both in writing.
- CapCut on mobile is sufficient for all video editing needed at this stage. It handles Reels and TikTok formats, auto-captions, trimming, and basic transitions.
- Instagram Insights (free, in the app under "Professional dashboard") shows which posts reached the most non-followers and which got the most saves. Saves are the signal that matters most for understanding what content is resonating with people who are planning a future visit. Check it monthly and do more of whatever is earning saves.

## Tactic 3: Website FAQ and Content Update
Phase: Search and AI Visibility
Short Label: Website & FAQ
Subtitle: Add the content that makes Karen's Cafe findable by visitors who do not know it exists yet -- including the AI tools that are increasingly deciding where people eat.

### What We Heard From You

The website is on WordPress and was built with outside help that is no longer available. Karen is not familiar with the technical details of the site and is uncertain about the login credentials. The site is functional but has not been updated to reflect the playroom, the crafting events, or the garden as distinct offerings visitors can seek out and book. Past paid Google Ads did not produce clear results. That is consistent with sending traffic to a site that does not represent the full business or answer the questions a first-time visitor would have. The coaching call identified the FAQ addition as the highest-priority content change, because it directly affects how AI search tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews describe and recommend the cafe.

### What This Is

This tactic has two components. The first is recovering WordPress access and confirming the login credentials, then making targeted content additions: new pages or sections for the playroom, events and crafting, and the garden, plus a FAQ section on the homepage. The second is structuring that FAQ content in the format that AI search tools are designed to extract -- questions as headings, direct answers in the first sentence -- so the cafe begins appearing in AI-generated travel recommendations for Harrison Hot Springs.

### Why It Matters

When a visitor asks Google AI Overview or ChatGPT "what are some good cafes in Harrison Hot Springs" or "things to do in Harrison Hot Springs with kids," the answer is assembled from website content, business listing descriptions, and review text. A cafe with a children's playroom that does not mention the word "playroom" anywhere on its website will never appear in that response, regardless of how many people love it in person. Adding structured FAQ content is the lowest-cost, highest-impact change available to improve how AI tools describe Karen's Cafe to potential visitors. It is also where this tactic and Tactic 1 compound each other: accurate listings plus FAQ-structured website content together are what produce an AI citation.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Recover WordPress Access

1. **Retrieve WordPress login credentials.**
   - Contact the previous website developer and ask specifically for: the WordPress admin username and password, the hosting provider name (examples: SiteGround, Bluehost, GoDaddy, WPEngine), and the domain registrar information, which may be a separate account from hosting.
   - If the developer is not responsive, go to the hosting provider directly. If you know which hosting company was used, log in at their website with the email address associated with the account. Most hosts allow password resets via email verification.
   - If neither is reachable, a WordPress developer can recover access through the database. This typically costs one to two hours of developer time. The Accelerator program can recommend resources if needed.
   - Once logged in to WordPress admin at your-domain.com/wp-admin, immediately change the password to something you control and save it in a password manager or a secure note. Record the hosting provider, the registrar, and the login credentials in a single secure document so this situation cannot repeat itself.

2. **Learn the basic WordPress update workflow.**
   - WordPress' built-in editor, called Gutenberg or the block editor, does not require coding knowledge. Pages are built from stacked content blocks -- text blocks, image blocks, heading blocks -- that can be clicked and edited directly.
   - To edit any existing page: go to Pages, then All Pages in the WordPress dashboard, click on the page name, and the editor opens. Click on any text block to edit the text. Click the blue Update button in the top right when done.
   - To add a new page: go to Pages, then Add New. Give it a title, add content blocks, and click Publish when ready.
   - A step-by-step WordPress playbook specific to Karen's site was offered during the coaching call. If this has not been received, request it through the Accelerator program at support@elearningu.com. It will be provided.

#### Part B: Add the FAQ Section to the Homepage

3. **Write and add the FAQ content.**
   - The FAQ section is the single most impactful content addition available on the WordPress site. Add it to the homepage, not buried on a separate page, so it is indexed alongside the business name and location. That is how AI tools connect the answers to the business.
   - In WordPress, add the FAQ section near the bottom of the homepage, above the footer. Use a Heading block set to H2 for the section title: "Frequently Asked Questions." Then, for each question, use an H3 heading block for the question itself, followed immediately by a paragraph block with the answer.
   - The question must be phrased as a real question a visitor would type into Google or ask an AI tool. "Does Karen's Cafe have a children's play area?" not "About our playroom." The heading format signals to search engines and AI systems that this is question-and-answer content.
   - Write the answer so the first sentence is a complete, direct response to the question. No preamble. Example: "Does Karen's Cafe have a children's play area? Yes -- there is a dedicated children's playroom at the cafe, designed so that kids can play independently while parents enjoy tea or a meal without interruption."
   - Use the following questions and opening answer sentences as the first version of the section:
     - "What does Karen's Cafe serve?" -- "We serve breakfast, lunch, and afternoon high tea, made from scratch each day. Our tea service is the most popular, but the full menu covers a wide range of options for both visitors and locals."
     - "Does Karen's Cafe have a children's play area?" -- "Yes -- there is a dedicated children's playroom at the cafe, designed so that kids can play independently while parents enjoy tea or a meal without interruption."
     - "Can I book Karen's Cafe for a private event?" -- "Yes -- we have a private event and meeting room available for bookings, suitable for birthday celebrations, baby showers, small group meetings, and other gatherings. Contact us to discuss availability."
     - "Does Karen's Cafe have a garden?" -- "Yes -- the cafe garden can accommodate up to 100 guests and is available for weddings, private gatherings, and outdoor events. It is one of the only garden event spaces in Harrison Hot Springs."
     - "What crafting events does Karen's Cafe offer?" -- "We run regular crafting evenings including candle making, floral arranging, and felting workshops. Events are bookable in advance and are popular for groups. Check our current schedule or contact us to find out what is coming up."
     - "Is Karen's Cafe open year-round?" -- "Yes -- we are open year-round, including through the quieter winter months. Rainy days are particularly popular for our afternoon tea service."
     - "How do I get to Karen's Cafe in Harrison Hot Springs?" -- "We are located in Harrison Hot Springs, BC, easily reachable from Vancouver, Chilliwack, and Mission. [Insert full address and any parking notes]."

#### Part C: Create or Update Pages for Each Offering

4. **Add or update the playroom, events, and garden pages.**
   - Each of the five offerings should have its own page or a clearly labelled section on an existing page. The minimum viable version is a descriptive paragraph, a photo, and a contact or booking call to action.
   - Playroom page: two to three sentences describing the playroom, who it is for, and what parents can do while their children are there. Include a photo. Add: "One of the only cafes in the Fraser Valley with a dedicated children's play space."
   - Events and crafting page: list the types of events offered, note that events are bookable in advance, and include a contact form or email address for enquiries. If events run on a set schedule, list the upcoming dates.
   - Garden page: describe the garden space including capacity and what it is suited for, include a photo, and add a contact prompt for wedding and event enquiries. State explicitly that this is one of the only private garden event spaces in Harrison outside the resort. That is a highly searchable differentiator.
   - Private dining and event room: a single paragraph is sufficient. Name the capacity, the types of gatherings it suits, and how to enquire.

5. **Test the site in AI search tools.**
   - Once the FAQ section and offering pages are live, go to chat.openai.com and ask "What are some good cafes in Harrison Hot Springs for afternoon tea?" Note whether Karen's Cafe is mentioned and what information is cited.
   - Do the same test in Perplexity at perplexity.ai, which shows its sources. If Karen's Cafe appears as a cited source, note which page was cited. If it does not appear, note what businesses do appear and what their websites have in common.
   - Run these tests again at 60 days and 90 days. FAQ content typically takes four to eight weeks to be indexed and begin influencing AI-generated responses. Changes should be visible by the end of the 90-day program.
   - Test in Google as well with specific queries: "cafe with children's play area Harrison Hot Springs," "afternoon tea Harrison Hot Springs," "private event space Harrison Hot Springs." Note which result position Karen's Cafe appears in, if at all.

6. **Connect Google Search Console to the website.**
   - Go to search.google.com/search-console and add the website. Verification takes about 20 minutes and requires a small piece of code added to the site, or a DNS record added at the domain registrar.
   - Once connected, Search Console shows exactly which search queries are bringing people to the site, which pages are appearing in results, and whether Google has found any errors. This is how you know the content updates are working.
   - Check it once a month. There is no need to look daily. The data compounds over time and the monthly view is what tells the story.

### Tips
- The FAQ section is more important than any other content update on the site. If time is limited and only one thing gets done in this tactic, the FAQ section is the thing. It has a disproportionate effect on AI search visibility relative to the time it takes to write.
- Do not attempt to learn WordPress from scratch before starting the updates. The block editor is genuinely approachable for non-technical users, and a 30-minute YouTube tutorial (search "WordPress Gutenberg beginner 2026") is enough to make basic edits.
- If finding a new WordPress developer feels urgent, ask the Accelerator program for a referral, post in the local Harrison or Agassiz business community on Facebook, or check Upwork for a WordPress specialist rated highly for content updates. Budget for two to four hours of developer time for the content additions. This is not a full rebuild.
- The garden is one of the most searchable differentiators Karen's Cafe has. "Wedding garden Harrison Hot Springs" is a low-competition search query with high conversion intent. A single well-written page dedicated to the garden will likely rank for this query within 90 days.
- Once the FAQ and offering pages are live, check Search Console monthly. It tells you which queries are working and which pages need strengthening. That data is the brief for everything you write next.

## GBP Quick Wins

These are small jobs that pay back quickly and do not need a developer, a budget, or anything on the WordPress site. Most take under an hour. They should not wait for anything else in the plan.

### On the Google Business Profile

- **Reply to all existing Google reviews.** Go through every review on the profile and give each one a personal response. Mention something specific the reviewer said, and invite them back. Google treats owner responses as an activity signal, and future guests read them when deciding whether to visit.
- **Add every service and amenity to the profile.** Under "Edit profile," go to the Services and Products sections and add everything Karen's Cafe offers: high tea, breakfast, lunch, candle making workshops, floral arranging workshops, felting workshops, children's playroom, private event room, garden wedding space, gift shop. The more specific the listing, the more category searches the profile appears in.
- **Start posting to Google Posts.** There is a Posts section in the Google Business Profile dashboard. One post a fortnight tied to whatever is happening -- an upcoming crafting evening, the garden opening for the season, a seasonal tea menu -- keeps the listing active and gives Google recent content to reference.
- **Set up a Google Review link and share it.** In the GBP dashboard, find "Get more reviews" and copy the short link Google generates. Save it in a note on the front counter, add it to the bottom of the menu, and give it to the social media helper to use in Instagram Stories. The easier the ask, the more reviews come in.
- **Enable messaging.** Under the GBP dashboard, turn on the messaging feature so visitors can send a question directly from Google Maps. Set a notification so messages do not sit unanswered for days.

### On the Website (Before the Full WordPress Work Begins)

- **Confirm the website is mobile-friendly.** Open the current site on a phone and check whether the menu works, the hours are visible without zooming, and the phone number is tappable. If any of those are broken, note them for the WordPress work in Weeks 3-4.
- **Check that all social media links in the website footer actually work.** Click every icon. A dead link to an old or renamed account is worse than no link at all.
- **Confirm the online ordering or booking link is current.** If the cafe takes reservations or deposits for events through any online tool, make sure the link on the site points to the right place and does not produce an error.

### One Thing Worth Watching

AI tools are showing up as a traffic source in Google Analytics for more businesses every month. The FAQ content and offering pages being built in Tactic 3 are exactly the pages that get cited in AI-generated responses. It is worth checking, at the 90-day review, whether any traffic is appearing from AI referral sources in Google Analytics or Search Console. That number will likely be small in 90 days and meaningful in 18 months, and the work being done now is what builds it.
