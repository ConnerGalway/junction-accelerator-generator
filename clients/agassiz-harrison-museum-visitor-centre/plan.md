# Agassiz-Harrison Museum & Visitor Centre: Accelerator Implementation Plan

## Meta
Client Slug: agassiz-harrison-museum
Grant Budget: $1,500
Has GBP Quick Wins: yes

## Goal

The Agassiz-Harrison Museum & Visitor Centre sits on 13,000 photographs, military stories that have never been told online, agricultural history that connects directly to the Fraser Valley identity, and a local personality archive that most regional communities would trade a lot to have. The goal of the next ninety days is not to get more followers. It is to convert online discovery into in-person visits by getting that material in front of the families, young couples, local residents, and school groups who do not yet know the museum exists or what is inside it. The digital assessment confirmed the foundation is solid: a 4.6-star Google rating from 125 reviews, strong technical performance, and number-one rankings for every branded search term. What is missing is volume and structure. The content goes out at a reasonable pace but lacks the pillar organisation that makes a small team's effort compound over time, the video formats that platforms now reward with reach, and the FAQ-style content that AI search tools use to answer questions about what to do in Agassiz and Harrison Hot Springs. This plan fixes all three in sequence, using what the museum already has: the archives, the Canva setup, and Maria's commitment to doing this properly.

## Strategic Positioning

The Agassiz-Harrison Museum is not competing with the Royal BC Museum. It is the only place where someone can learn that a First World War veteran became the first chairman of Harrison Hot Springs, find a photograph of the 1893 railway station that became the building they are standing in, or hold a conversation about the farming families who built the District of Kent. That specificity is the competitive advantage, and it is also exactly what AI search tools are designed to surface when someone asks a specific question. The museum's positioning is: the authoritative local record of Harrison Hot Springs and the District of Kent, alive online through daily storytelling, and worth the drive on a rainy Fraser Valley afternoon.

> **Your Strategic Positioning**
> The Agassiz-Harrison Museum is where the Fraser Valley's history is kept. Thirteen thousand photographs, stories that did not make the textbooks, and a community that earned its place in BC history. The building itself is a 133-year-old railway station. That is the starting point.

## Tactic 1: Social Content System and Video Shift
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Content & Video
Subtitle: Build the content pillar framework and backlog that lets a small team post consistently at scale, then shift the default format to short-form video to unlock the reach the current posting rate has not been able to produce.

### What We Heard From You

The museum already posts four to six times per week and has a four-month content backlog, which puts it ahead of almost every organisation at this stage. The challenge Maria named is not discipline -- it is structure. Seasonal staff turnover means the voice drifts, the topics repeat without intention, and the content does not compound the way it should when a team has 13,000 photographs to draw from. The coaching call also surfaced a specific gap: almost no Reels. The digital assessment confirmed it -- only one of the last twelve posts was a Reel, despite Reels generating two to ten times the reach of static posts on Instagram's algorithm. The archive exists. The Canva setup exists. What is missing is the framework that makes the content system repeatable for whoever is running it.

### What This Is

A five-pillar content framework built around the museum's existing archives, combined with a shift in default format from static posts to short-form video using Canva's animation tools. The framework gives each content bucket a name, a theme, a posting rhythm, and a backlog target, so that any staff member can open the system and know exactly what to post next. The video shift does not require new equipment or skills -- it requires animating the static Canva posts already being produced and adding music or voiceover before publishing.

### Why It Matters

The museum's Instagram engagement rate is 0.35 percent against a tourism industry benchmark of one to three percent. That gap is almost entirely explained by format: static images have been losing algorithmic reach since 2022, and Reels are now the primary distribution lever on Instagram and Facebook. Posting the same number of times in the same format will not change that number. Shifting to animated or video content, even simple Canva animations with music, will. The platform rewards the format regardless of production quality, and the museum's archive material -- historic photographs, artifact close-ups, Fraser Valley landscapes -- is exactly what performs well as short-form video.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Build the Content Pillar Framework

1. **Define the five content pillars and assign each one a posting cadence.**
   - The five pillars recommended for the museum are: Agriculture and Farming Heritage, Military Stories and Veterans, Local Personalities and Community Builders, Scenery and Place (Fraser Valley landscapes, the railway station, Harrison Hot Springs geography), and Museum Life (exhibits, artifacts, events, the "Guess That Artifact" series, volunteer spotlights).
   - Print this as a one-page reference and post it wherever content is created. Every post gets assigned to a pillar before it is made. This is the document that keeps seasonal staff on voice.
   - Set a weekly posting rhythm across pillars: two posts from Agriculture or Military, two from Local Personalities or Scenery, one from Museum Life, and one wildcard from any pillar. That is six posts per week, matching the current cadence with intentional distribution.
   - Review the pillar assignments quarterly. If one pillar is consistently underperforming or the archive is running thin on a category, adjust the cadence rather than abandoning it.

2. **Build a backlog of ten posts per pillar before changing anything about the current schedule.**
   - The museum already has a four-month backlog, which is excellent. The task here is not to create more content before starting -- it is to tag and organise the existing backlog by pillar so the framework is working from day one.
   - Go through the current content queue and assign each piece to one of the five pillars. Pieces that do not fit any pillar clearly are either wildcard posts or candidates for deletion.
   - Work with the archives assistant to identify at least ten additional archive photographs or artifacts per pillar that have not yet been posted. These become the next layer of the backlog.
   - File each pillar's backlog in its own Canva folder or Google Drive sub-folder so content is retrievable by pillar, not just by date.

3. **Create a master content calendar in a shared Google Sheet.**
   - Columns: date, platform, pillar, content description, status (draft, ready, scheduled, published), and the person responsible.
   - Map out four weeks at a time, not twelve. Twelve-week calendars go stale because exhibitions change, local events happen, and seasonal staff rotate. Four weeks is enough to batch-schedule in advance without locking in content that will feel outdated.
   - Use the calendar as the handoff document between Maria and seasonal staff. A new staff member should be able to open it, read the pillar definitions, and know exactly what to create next without asking.
   - Schedule a monthly 30-minute calendar review to fill the next month and flag any gaps.

#### Part B: Shift the Default Format to Short-Form Video

4. **Convert every static Canva post into an animated version before publishing.**
   - Canva has built-in animation tools on every design. Before hitting download, click Animate and choose a simple movement -- fade, slide, or breathe. Add royalty-free music from Canva's audio library (free with Canva Pro). This takes approximately two minutes per post and produces a video file ready for Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts.
   - The goal is not cinematic production. It is moving content. A historic photograph that slides in with a music bed and a text overlay performs two to four times better than the same photograph as a static JPEG, using the same sixty seconds of effort.
   - Download in MP4 format for video platforms (Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) and PNG for Facebook feed posts where static still performs reasonably. Do not post static images to Instagram feed anymore unless the content cannot be animated.

5. **Set a target of three to five Reels per week and build templates for the two formats that will generate most of the volume.**
   - Format one: the Archive Reveal. A single historic photograph, animated with a slow zoom, text overlay naming the subject and year, and a question in the caption inviting engagement. Example: a 1943 photograph of a Agassiz farmer, animated with a zoom, captioned "Do you recognise this face? He farmed the Kent flats for forty years. Tell us what you know." Thirty seconds. Done.
   - Format two: the Artifact Close-Up. A short video panning across an artifact, with text overlay naming what it is and a one-line fact. Example: a slow pan across a WWI service medal with text overlay reading "He earned this at Passchendaele. He came home. He became your town's first mayor." Fifteen to thirty seconds. High-performing, low-effort.
   - Build these two templates in Canva once, save them, and use them repeatedly. The template is the scalable system. The content changes every time, but the format does not.

6. **Expand to TikTok and YouTube Shorts using the same content produced for Instagram Reels.**
   - Every Reel produced for Instagram is also a TikTok and a YouTube Short. Download the MP4 from Canva, upload to TikTok and YouTube separately. This is ten minutes of additional distribution per week for the same content.
   - Create TikTok and YouTube accounts in the museum's name if they do not already exist. Keep the bio and description consistent with the Instagram and Facebook profiles.
   - TikTok's algorithm does not require followers to generate reach. A new account with no history can reach thousands of people if the content is specific and answers a searchable question. The museum's archive material is exactly the kind of niche, specific content that performs on TikTok without paid promotion.
   - YouTube Shorts are indexed by Google search. A YouTube Short titled "The First World War veteran who became Harrison Hot Springs' first chairman" will appear in Google search results for queries about Harrison Hot Springs history. This is free search visibility that takes five minutes to publish.

#### Part C: Activate Stories and Platform Distribution

7. **Post to Instagram and Facebook Stories daily, separate from the main feed schedule.**
   - Stories are not a polished content format. They are the spontaneous layer: a quick photo of a new artifact being catalogued, a behind-the-scenes look at an exhibit being set up, a poll asking followers which historical story they want to hear next week. This does not require Canva. It requires a phone.
   - Daily Stories signal to the Instagram algorithm that the account is active, which improves the reach of main feed posts. An account that posts Reels three times per week and Stories every day will outperform an account that posts Reels three times per week and nothing else.
   - Assign Stories responsibility to whoever is on-site each day. The instruction is simple: post one Story before noon showing something happening at the museum. That is the entire brief.

8. **Set up cross-platform scheduling using Meta Business Suite for Facebook and Instagram.**
   - Meta Business Suite at business.facebook.com allows posts and Reels to be scheduled simultaneously to Facebook and Instagram, including Stories. This is free and removes the need to post to both platforms separately.
   - Schedule one full week of content in one sitting, ideally on Monday morning. This is the batch-scheduling habit that keeps the calendar running when weeks get busy.
   - For TikTok, use TikTok's own scheduler (available in the app for Business accounts) or schedule through a third-party tool like Later or Buffer if the museum's workflow benefits from a single dashboard.
   - YouTube Shorts can be scheduled through YouTube Studio. Set a default publishing time based on when the museum's audience is most active, typically Tuesday to Thursday between 10am and noon.

### Tips
- The pillar framework only works if it is physically present where content is created. A one-page printed reference next to the computer where Canva runs is worth more than a document saved somewhere it has to be searched for. Print it.
- Do not wait for perfect archive selections to start posting Reels. The first ten Reels should be experiments. The goal is to learn which pillar and format the museum's current audience responds to, not to produce the ideal video on the first attempt.
- The "Guess That Artifact" format the museum already uses is among the best-performing interactive formats available to a cultural organisation. Expand it to two per month minimum and treat it as a tent-pole post rather than an occasional feature.
- When a Reel performs significantly above average, note the pillar, the format, the time of day, and the caption style. That combination is the template for the next ten posts in that pillar. This is how a data-driven content calendar is built from practice rather than guesswork.
- Seasonal staff turnover is the biggest threat to content consistency. The pillar framework, the Canva templates, and the content calendar are the solution -- but only if they are documented in a one-page onboarding document that every new staff member reads on day one. Build that document in Month 2.

## Tactic 2: GEO Content and FAQ Architecture
Phase: Search and AI Visibility
Short Label: GEO & FAQ
Subtitle: Build the question-and-answer content structure that gets the museum cited by AI search tools when visitors ask what to do in Agassiz and Harrison Hot Springs.

### What We Heard From You

The coaching call covered a significant shift in how visitors discover places like the museum. Search is no longer primarily keyword-based. When someone asks ChatGPT or Google AI Overview "what should I do in Harrison Hot Springs with kids" or "are there any museums near Agassiz BC," the answer is assembled from websites that have structured their content as clear questions with direct answers -- the format that AI systems are designed to extract. The museum's website has strong technical performance and good local search rankings, but the content is not yet structured for this kind of AI discovery. Damon suggested starting with the top 100 questions visitors actually ask, because those questions are the architecture of the entire GEO strategy.

### What This Is

A two-part content build. First, a systematic FAQ section on the museum's website built around the questions visitors and locals actually ask -- formatted with each question as an H2 or H3 heading and the answer in the first sentence of the paragraph immediately below it. Second, a set of deeper answer pages targeting the specific historical and cultural questions the museum's archive can answer authoritatively: the history of the Agassiz railway station, the military figures connected to the District of Kent, the agricultural story of the Fraser Valley. Both of these feed AI search tools, improve Google rankings for long-tail queries, and make the museum the cited source when Harrison Hot Springs travel planning happens through AI.

### Why It Matters

The museum's website currently generates approximately 1,131 monthly organic search visits and ranks number five for "Agassiz BC." That is a strong starting point. The gap is that the content does not answer the questions AI tools are using to build travel recommendations. A visitor who asks an AI tool "what is the history of Harrison Hot Springs" and gets a response citing the museum's website is more likely to visit than a visitor who has to independently find the website and read it. GEO is also cumulative: each FAQ answer and each deep content page strengthens the museum's authority as the source for Fraser Valley history, which makes every subsequent citation more likely. The work done in the next 90 days compounds for years.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Build the FAQ Architecture

1. **Run the top 100 questions session with the full team.**
   - Block two hours with Maria, the archives assistant, and any other regular staff or volunteers. The prompt is simple: what do visitors ask at the front desk? What do people ask on social media? What do tourists ask that the team already knows the answer to but has never written down?
   - Categories to cover: general visitor questions (hours, admission, parking, accessibility), historical questions about Agassiz and Harrison Hot Springs, questions about the archive and photograph collection, questions about specific exhibits, questions about the 1893 railway station building, questions about things to do nearby, questions about school group visits.
   - Write every question down as it is asked, word for word. The exact phrasing matters because AI tools match on natural language, not on keywords. "Is the Agassiz Museum free?" is a different question from "what does it cost to visit the Agassiz Museum?" and both need an answer.
   - After the session, sort the questions into clusters of five to ten related questions. Each cluster becomes one FAQ page or one section of the website FAQ.

2. **Add a FAQ section to the museum's homepage.**
   - Add this section near the bottom of the homepage, above the footer. Use an H2 heading: "Frequently Asked Questions."
   - For each question, use an H3 heading for the question itself, followed immediately by a paragraph block with the answer. The first sentence of every answer must be a complete, direct response. No preamble.
   - Start with the ten questions visitors ask most at the front desk. These are the highest-traffic search queries for this type of museum. Suggested first ten:
     - "What are the Agassiz-Harrison Museum's hours?" -- "The museum is open [days and hours]. We recommend calling ahead during shoulder season as hours may vary."
     - "How much does it cost to visit the Agassiz-Harrison Museum?" -- "Admission is [price]. Children under [age] are free. Group rates are available for school trips and bus tours."
     - "Where is the Agassiz-Harrison Museum located?" -- "We are at 7011 Pioneer Avenue, Agassiz, BC, in the historic 1893 Canadian Pacific Railway station. Parking is available on-site."
     - "Is the Agassiz-Harrison Museum accessible?" -- "[Answer about accessibility based on the actual building situation]."
     - "How old is the museum building?" -- "The building is the original 1893 Canadian Pacific Railway station, making it one of the oldest standing railway structures in the Fraser Valley."
     - "What does the Agassiz-Harrison Museum have on display?" -- "The museum holds over 13,000 historical photographs alongside artifacts covering agricultural history, military service, and the stories of the families who built the District of Kent and Harrison Hot Springs."
     - "Are school trips welcome at the Agassiz-Harrison Museum?" -- "[Answer based on actual school visit program]."
     - "How far is the museum from Harrison Hot Springs?" -- "The museum is approximately [X] minutes from Harrison Hot Springs, located in central Agassiz."
     - "Does the museum have a gift shop?" -- "Yes -- the gift shop carries locally produced items and regional books. Purchases support the museum's operations."
     - "Can I access historic photographs of Agassiz or Harrison Hot Springs?" -- "The museum holds over 13,000 historical photographs. Contact us about archive access and research requests."

3. **Build the FAQ page into a dedicated resource on the website, separate from the homepage FAQ.**
   - Once the homepage FAQ is live with ten questions, move the full 100-question library onto a dedicated FAQ page. Link to it from the homepage FAQ with "See all frequently asked questions."
   - Organise the full FAQ page by section, using H2 headings for each category: Visit Planning, History, Collections and Archives, School Groups, and Getting Here.
   - This page becomes the most valuable single asset for AI search visibility because it answers dozens of specific questions on a single indexed URL, which AI tools prefer to content spread across many pages.

#### Part B: Deep Content Pages for Historical Authority

4. **Identify the five historical topics the museum can answer most authoritatively and write one deep content page per topic.**
   - A deep content page is 500 to 800 words, written in plain language, structured with H2 subheadings, and formatted so each subheading and the paragraph immediately below it answers a specific question. It is not a blog post. It is a reference document.
   - Recommended first five topics, based on the coaching call and archive inventory:
     - The History of the Agassiz Railway Station (building history, CPR connection, how it became the museum)
     - Agriculture in the Fraser Valley: A History of the District of Kent (the families, the crops, the land)
     - Harrison Hot Springs: From Hot Springs to Resort Town (the history visitors ask about when they come from the resort)
     - Military Connections: Agassiz and Harrison Residents Who Served (the First World War chairman story, the Billy Bishop-trained pilot story, any confirmed veterans in the archive)
     - Pioneers of the District of Kent: The Families Who Built Agassiz (local personalities pillar translated into long-form content)
   - Write each page as if answering a curious visitor's questions directly, not as a formal exhibit label. The Harrison Hot Springs history page, for example, should answer: when did people first visit the hot springs, what did the resort look like historically, who were the notable early visitors, how did the town develop. Every one of those is a question someone is currently asking an AI tool.

5. **Format every deep content page for AI citation.**
   - Every H2 subheading on a deep content page should be phrased as a question or as a direct statement that completes the question "What is...?" Example: instead of "Early Settlement," use "Who Were the First Settlers in the District of Kent?" This signals to AI tools that the section answers a specific question.
   - The first sentence of every paragraph must be a complete, standalone answer to the subheading question. AI tools extract the first coherent sentence after a heading when building a cited response. If the first sentence is a preamble, the citation is lost.
   - Add internal links from each deep content page to the FAQ page and to at least two other related deep content pages. Internal linking tells search engines and AI tools that this content belongs to a cluster of authority on the same topic.
   - Add each deep content page to the website navigation or a "History" section visible from the homepage. Content that cannot be found from the homepage takes months longer to be indexed and cited.

6. **Set up and activate Google Business Profile Posts.**
   - Google Business Profile Posts are free content slots that appear directly in Google search results and Maps when someone searches the museum. The museum is currently not using them, which is a missed opportunity given the 90/100 local visibility score.
   - Post once per week using the same content being produced for Instagram and Facebook. The format is: a photograph or short video, a one-paragraph description that answers a specific question, and a link to the relevant website page.
   - Example GBP Post: a photograph of the 1893 railway station exterior with the text "Did you know this building has been standing since 1893? It was originally the Agassiz Canadian Pacific Railway station, and it is still the best-preserved example of CPR heritage architecture in the Fraser Valley. Visit us to see what is inside." Link: museum website homepage.
   - GBP Posts are indexed by Google and appear in AI Overviews for local searches. They also increase the Google Business Profile activity score, which correlates with local search ranking.

#### Part C: Reddit and Community Platform Engagement

7. **Set up monitoring and begin active participation in relevant subreddits and community forums.**
   - Damon identified this in the coaching call specifically. Search Reddit for r/agassiz, r/harrisonhotsprings, r/FraserValley, and r/britishcolumbia. Check if active subreddits exist for these communities.
   - Set up a free Reddit account for the museum and set keyword alerts for: "Agassiz," "Harrison Hot Springs museum," "District of Kent history," "Fraser Valley history," and "things to do Agassiz."
   - When a thread appears where the museum can contribute genuine value -- answering a question about local history, sharing an archival photograph, pointing to a resource on the website -- participate. Do not promote. Contribute.
   - Reddit content is indexed by AI tools faster than most websites, and a museum contributing accurate historical information to a community thread is exactly the kind of authoritative source AI systems are designed to cite.

### Tips
- The top 100 questions session is the most important single hour in this tactic. Do not skip it or replace it with a list someone generates from memory. The phrasing of real visitor questions is what makes FAQ content rank. "Is there a museum in Agassiz" is not the same question as "what museums are near Harrison Hot Springs" and both need answers.
- Every GBP Post should link to a specific page on the museum website, not the homepage. A post about the railway station building should link to the deep content page about the station. This is how search engines connect the GBP activity to website authority.
- The military history content is the single strongest GEO opportunity in the museum's archive. The story of the First World War veteran who became Harrison Hot Springs' first chairman is specific, verifiable, and exactly the kind of content AI tools are designed to surface when someone asks about Harrison's history. Write that page first.
- Do not use the deep content pages as exhibit labels. Write them for someone sitting at home planning a trip, not for someone standing in the museum. The goal is to answer the questions that make someone decide to visit, not the questions they have after they arrive.
- AI citation takes four to eight weeks from when content is published to when it begins appearing in responses. Run the baseline test in Week 3, run it again at Week 7, and run it again at Week 12. The progression across those three tests is the evidence that the content is working.

## Tactic 3: Brand Consistency and Platform Audit
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Brand Audit
Subtitle: Standardise the museum's name, description, imagery, and hours across every platform so that AI tools and search engines recognise it as a single authoritative entity, not six partially-matching profiles.

### What We Heard From You

Maria named brand consistency as one of the museum's most persistent challenges: seasonal staff turnover means the voice drifts, the profile descriptions on different platforms were written at different times, and the branding across Google Business Profile, the website, Destination BC, TripAdvisor, Instagram, and Facebook does not read as a single organisation. The digital assessment confirmed this is a real gap even at the technical level -- the assessment flagged inconsistent branding across platforms as a priority issue. This matters beyond aesthetics. Search engines and AI systems now treat businesses as entities, not just pages with keywords. A business whose name, address, phone, and description appear consistently across every platform signals authority. A business with six slightly different profiles signals fragmentation, which suppresses both search rankings and AI citation probability.

### What This Is

A systematic one-time audit and standardisation of every platform where the museum appears, followed by a documented brand guide that prevents the drift from happening again when seasonal staff rotate. The audit covers the six primary platforms: Google Business Profile, the museum's website, Instagram, Facebook, Destination BC, and TripAdvisor. The brand guide covers the four elements that must be consistent: the museum's exact legal name, the standard description (in three lengths: one sentence, one paragraph, three paragraphs), the current hours formatted consistently, and the hero images used as profile and cover photos.

### Why It Matters

This tactic is the infrastructure that makes Tactics 1 and 2 work at full strength. A museum that produces excellent video content and well-structured FAQ pages but has six slightly different names across platforms will see that content's authority diluted because search engines cannot confidently associate it all with a single entity. The assessment found the museum ranking 90/100 for local visibility -- that score can be pushed higher, and the entity consistency work in this tactic is what does it. It also directly affects AI citation: AI tools build their understanding of a business from the pattern of information that appears across the internet. A consistent pattern produces confident citations. An inconsistent one produces hedged or missing citations.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: The Platform Audit

1. **Run the full platform audit across all six platforms in a single two-hour session.**
   - Open each platform in a separate browser tab and record the current information in a shared Google Sheet: platform name, current business name (exactly as it appears), current description (copy and paste the full text), hours as listed, phone number, website URL, and the profile photo and cover image currently in use.
   - The six platforms to audit: Google Business Profile (business.google.com), the museum website homepage, Instagram (the bio and the About section), Facebook (the Page About section), Destination BC (login to the partner portal), and TripAdvisor (login to the owner account or claim it if unclaimed).
   - As each platform is checked, note every field that differs from the website as the source of truth. Common differences: a shorter name on Instagram, an older phone number on TripAdvisor, hours that have changed since the Destination BC listing was set up, a description written in a different voice on Facebook.
   - This spreadsheet becomes the master record. Keep it. Update it every time anything changes on any platform.

2. **Claim or correct any platform where the listing is unverified or inaccurate.**
   - The digital assessment flagged no verified TripAdvisor presence. If the listing is unclaimed, go to tripadvisor.com, search for the museum, and click "Is this your business?" Claiming is free and takes approximately 15 minutes.
   - For Destination BC, log in at the partner portal and check that the listing reflects current hours, current contact information, and the current website URL. Destination BC listings appear in regional tourism search results and are cited in AI-generated "things to do" responses.
   - Confirm that the Facebook Page is set to "Page type: Museum" rather than a generic business category, and that the Instagram account is set to a Creator or Business account with "Museum/Art Gallery" as the category. These category tags feed into platform search and AI classification.

3. **Write the three standard descriptions and distribute them to every platform.**
   - One sentence (for Instagram bio, TikTok bio, Google Business Profile short description): "The Agassiz-Harrison Museum preserves the history of the District of Kent and Harrison Hot Springs in a 133-year-old railway station -- 13,000 photographs, local artifacts, and stories worth knowing."
   - One paragraph (for Facebook About, Destination BC short field, TripAdvisor description): Expand the above to cover the five content pillars in plain language. Name agriculture, military history, local personalities, the railway heritage, and the archive collection specifically.
   - Three paragraphs (for the website About page and any long-form platform bio): Add the visitor experience (what a 20-to-45-minute visit involves), the gift shop, the school group program, and the contact information for archive research requests.
   - Once written, Maria reviews and approves all three versions. They are then copied exactly to each platform. No platform gets a custom version. The discipline of using identical descriptions is what builds entity consistency.

#### Part B: Visual and Hours Standardisation

4. **Standardise the profile photo and cover image across all platforms.**
   - Choose one profile photo that represents the museum clearly: either the exterior of the 1893 railway station or a strong interior shot showing the exhibit space. This image should be recognisable at small size (profile photos on mobile display at 40 to 50 pixels square) and visually consistent across every platform.
   - Choose one cover image for platforms that use them (Facebook, YouTube, TripAdvisor banner): a wide-format photograph of either the building exterior or a compelling archive photograph. The same image across all platforms.
   - Export the profile photo at 400x400 pixels minimum and the cover image at 1200x630 pixels (Facebook standard, which works for all platforms). Upload these to every platform in a single session.
   - Save both source files in a shared Google Drive folder called "Museum Brand Assets -- Current." Include a note with the date they were uploaded. When these images change, update them across all platforms in the same session.

5. **Confirm hours are identical on all six platforms and set a quarterly update reminder.**
   - The digital assessment flagged an incomplete hours display on the website (Monday, Saturday, Sunday shown but Tuesday to Friday missing). Fix this on the website first, then copy the complete hours to Google Business Profile, TripAdvisor, Facebook, and Destination BC.
   - Hours must match exactly: same day abbreviations, same time format (9am not 9:00 AM), same language for any exceptions (e.g., "Closed statutory holidays"). Even formatting differences can create inconsistency signals.
   - Set a recurring calendar reminder for the first week of each quarter to review hours on all platforms. Museum hours often change seasonally, and a mismatch between Google and the website is one of the most common reasons visitors arrive at closed doors -- which generates negative reviews.
   - Add a note to the GBP listing for any temporary closures or special holiday hours. Google allows special hours to be added in advance, and using this feature signals active management to both the algorithm and to visitors.

#### Part C: Build the Brand Guide for Seasonal Staff

6. **Create a one-page brand guide for seasonal staff onboarding.**
   - This document does not need to be elaborate. It is a single printed page that every seasonal staff member reads on day one before being given access to any social media account.
   - Contents: the exact museum name (Agassiz-Harrison Museum & Visitor Centre, not "Agassiz Museum" or "the Museum"), the one-sentence description, the brand voice in three adjectives (suggested: specific, warm, and unhurried -- the museum does not rush through history), the content pillars from Tactic 1, and a short list of things not to post (personal opinions, unverified historical claims, anything that changes the tone significantly from the established voice).
   - Include the login credentials sheet location and the content calendar location. Staff should never need to ask where to find these.
   - Review and update the brand guide at the start of each hiring season. A guide that reflects last year's content strategy becomes a liability as the strategy evolves.

7. **Add the museum's full address to the website footer.**
   - The digital assessment specifically recommended adding the full address (7011 Pioneer Avenue, Agassiz, BC V0M 1A0) to the website footer to strengthen NAP consistency signals.
   - This is a five-minute website edit that directly improves local search authority. Every page of the website should show the full address, phone number, and email address in the footer. This is what search engines use to confirm the museum's physical location when building local search results.
   - While in the footer, confirm the phone number is formatted as a clickable tel: link so mobile visitors can tap to call. The assessment identified the phone number as not clickable, which is a friction point for the 60-plus percent of traffic arriving from mobile devices.

### Tips
- The platform audit spreadsheet is a document worth maintaining permanently, not just running once. Every time the museum's hours change, a new platform is added, or a new staff member updates a profile, the spreadsheet should be updated. A current master record prevents drift before it starts.
- Destination BC and TripAdvisor are where tourists specifically research cultural attractions in BC before visiting. These two platforms are not optional additions to the Google-and-social-media world -- they are the platforms where the museum's target audience of regional and international tourists researches the decision to visit. Treat them as primary, not secondary.
- The one-sentence description is the most important of the three. It appears on the smallest platforms, at the smallest text sizes, and in the most competitive attention environments. Write it, test it by reading it aloud to someone unfamiliar with the museum, and if they do not immediately understand what the museum is and why it is interesting, rewrite it.
- When seasonal staff are hired, give them the brand guide and the content pillar framework before giving them any social media account access. The sequence matters. Access without context produces content that undermines the consistency work done in this tactic.
- Do not chase platform completeness at the expense of platform quality. Six strong, consistent, accurate profiles are worth more than ten partially-completed ones. Get the six primary platforms right first, and add new platforms only when a dedicated posting schedule is ready to follow them.

## GBP Quick Wins

These are small jobs that pay back quickly and do not require a developer or a large time commitment. Most take under an hour. None of them should wait for anything else in the plan.

### On the Google Business Profile

- **Start posting to Google Business Profile Posts weekly.** The museum has a 90/100 local visibility score but is not using GBP Posts, which are free content slots that appear directly in Google search results when someone searches the museum or related terms. One post per week tied to a current exhibit, an upcoming event, or a historical story from the archives. This takes ten minutes and directly improves the GBP activity score, which correlates with local search ranking.
- **Reply to every Google review, starting with the most recent.** The assessment found 125 reviews with a 4.6-star rating and no confirmed response pattern. Responding to every review -- especially ones that mention the caboose exhibit, the volunteer staff, or specific artifacts -- signals active management and builds the relationship with past visitors that generates repeat visits. Responses are also indexed by search engines.
- **Add the museum's standard opening hours for all seven days of the week.** The digital assessment flagged that Tuesday to Friday hours are missing from the website and likely from the GBP as well. An incomplete hours listing creates uncertainty for mid-week visitors and is one of the most common reasons people do not show up.
- **Add every service and amenity to the GBP attributes section.** Under "Edit Profile" in the GBP dashboard, there is an Attributes section. Enable every applicable attribute: museum, visitor centre, gift shop, wheelchair accessible, free parking, guided tours available (if applicable), school programs available. Each attribute adds the listing to a new category of filtered search results.
- **Upload ten new photographs covering the areas the assessment identified as gaps:** the caboose exhibit (mentioned frequently in reviews), the archive photograph collection, the 1893 railway station exterior in good daylight, and the gift shop. Owner-uploaded photos appear higher in the photo section than visitor photos.

### On the Website

- **Fix the six duplicate H1 header tags on the homepage.** The digital assessment identified six H1 tags where there should be one. The primary H1 should be "Welcome to Agassiz-Harrison Museum & Visitor Centre" or equivalent. All other headings should be H2 or H3. This is a 20-minute fix that directly improves how search engines read the page hierarchy.
- **Add alt text to the 15 images missing descriptions.** The assessment identified 15 of 21 homepage images as missing alt text. Alt text helps visually impaired visitors and improves image search visibility for terms like "historic railway station Agassiz" and "Fraser Valley agricultural artifacts." Write descriptive alt text for each image, naming what is in the photograph and including a natural location or historical reference where relevant.
- **Make the phone number a clickable tel: link.** The assessment flagged that (604) 796-3545 is not clickable on mobile, which is a friction point for the 60-plus percent of traffic arriving from mobile devices. A developer or the website's CMS can fix this in under 15 minutes.
- **Reduce the meta description from 245 to 155 characters.** The current meta description is getting cut off in Google search results, which means the second half of the description -- the part that might prompt a click -- is invisible. Rewrite it to under 155 characters, leading with the most compelling statement about what the museum offers.

The GEO content work in Tactic 2 is specifically designed to push this further by answering the questions that AI tools use when visitors ask about things to do in the area.
