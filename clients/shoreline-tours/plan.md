## Goal

Summer is the season that decides your year, and summer 2026 was down 20 percent on 2025. Your fall river season is ahead of last year and your spring waterfall cruises pay their way, but as you put it, if every boat is not sold out in June, July, August and September, the business takes a hit for the whole year. The empty seats were mostly on weekend public sailings. Every business in Harrison felt the dip, and fewer local visitors drove much of it. The goal of this plan is to make sure summer 2027 is back to, or above, the level you were at before this year, and to get there by making the money you already spend on advertising work harder.

You are not starting from nothing. You spend roughly $1,000 a month on Meta in summer and about $25 a day now, plus around $10 a day on Google. Your Google campaign drew 622 clicks from 14,000 impressions last month for $212, at 34 cents a click, which is unusually cheap. GA4 is installed and recording conversions. Your Meta Pixel has been on the site for two years. You have 4.8 stars from 263 Google reviews, 10,430 followers across Instagram and Facebook, and guests who come back every season. What is missing is not the tools. It is knowing what the tools are telling you. You said it yourself: "I'm just guessing right now."

That is why the work runs in this order. Weeks 1 to 4 make the numbers trustworthy and clean up Google Ads, because there is a real question about whether GA4's figures count bookings or something else, and every later decision depends on the answer. The same weeks fix the homepage, which stays the destination for your ads for now. Weeks 5 to 8 rebuild Meta around four audiences: families for summer, retirees and intentional travellers for spring and fall, Washington State visitors, and private groups. A retargeting campaign runs underneath all four. Weeks 9 to 12 build and test one landing page per cruise and start switching ads over to them, one campaign at a time. The content habit in Tactic 3 runs through all twelve weeks, because it is what feeds the ads.

This plan does not add spend. It works inside your current budget of about $1,000 a month on Meta in peak season, scaled down when you are quieter, plus about $10 a day on Google. We are not putting a revenue projection on it, because the starting figure that matters, summer 2025 public tour bookings by weekday and weekend, is not in our notes yet. Step 5 of Tactic 1 records that baseline so the 90-day review measures against a real number rather than a feeling. What the plan will tell you by Week 12 is something you cannot see today: what a booking costs you from Google and from each Meta audience, and which audience deserves the summer budget.

The plan dates are not fixed yet. Your season runs waterfall cruises in spring, lake cruises from May to September and river cruises in October and November, and you close from December to February. Where a step depends on what is bookable in a given week, it tells you how to decide. ## Strategic Positioning

There is almost no direct competition on the water in Harrison. The only comparable option is a 12-person jet boat eco tour. Shoreline runs a 62-foot yacht that carries up to 35 guests, with indoor and outdoor decks, food and drinks on board, and a two-hour lake cruise that asks nothing of anyone physically. That makes you the easy yes for groups that cannot all do the same thing: grandparents and grandchildren, a staff party, a bachelorette group, a couple celebrating an anniversary. Your reviews say the same thing in guests' own words. They mention staff by name, birthday celebrations, organized operations and the views.

The competitor that matters is not another boat. It is the assumption that Harrison Hot Springs means the resort pool and nothing else. As you put it, people look at the resort, see they are not staying there, and move on. Your ads and pages have to answer that first. Show the lake, the falls, the eagles and the river from the deck, and make it plain that anyone visiting Harrison can book, wherever they stay.

You also have three seasons, which is both an asset and a source of confusion. Many guests do not know you run a different cruise each season. The homepage's "Choose Your Adventure" block already solves that well, which is why it is the right ad destination today. Over the next year, each season should get its own page and its own ads, so a summer family, a fall eagle watcher and a Washington couple each land on something written for them.

Washington visitors are worth naming separately. They drive up to three hours, stay two or more nights, and respond when you tell them on the phone that the prices are in Canadian dollars. That point belongs in the ads before they ever call.

## Tactic 1: Run Meta and Google Ads You Can Read and Trust
Phase: Foundation and Paid
Short Label: Meta & Google Ads
Subtitle: Confirm what your tracking is counting, clean up Google Ads, and rebuild Meta around four audiences and a retargeting campaign that never switches off.

### What We Heard From You

You said the thing you most want from this program is "making sure I'm doing Google ads and Facebook ads properly." You hired help once. She set things up and it was still confusing, and now you are "just guessing" on Meta and "just winging it" on Google. You have stopped using Boost because you heard you should, but you are not sure what you are doing instead is any better. When you opened Google Ads on the call, you found it was still advertising a lake cruise in the middle of river season, that some of its search terms included the resort and restaurants, and that every ad sends people to the homepage. You also found, to your surprise, that GA4 is recording conversions. You target the Lower Mainland and Washington at 25 and up (35 and up for river tours), and you have never used your Meta Pixel for retargeting.

### What This Is

Four pieces of work. First, confirm that GA4, FareHarbor and the Meta Pixel are counting real bookings and nothing else, and write down where you are starting from. Second, clean up the Google campaign so it spends on people looking for a boat tour, says what is actually sailing, and reports bookings honestly. Third, move Meta from boosted posts to a simple structure in Ads Manager: four audience campaigns that take turns at your budget, plus a retargeting campaign that runs all the time. Fourth, use the creator visits you already host as ad content, with permission in writing.

### Why It Matters

Last month GA4 showed 89 conversions from paid search against $212 of Google spend. At an average booking of about $300, that would be a return of more than 100 to 1, which almost never happens. One of two things is likely going on. Either something other than a booking is being counted as a conversion, or the Google campaign is mostly catching people who searched "Shoreline Tours" by name and would have booked anyway. Both are fixable, and both change how you should spend. On Meta, the people who already follow you, watch your Reels and visit your site are your cheapest bookings, and today no campaign is aimed at them. With a budget of around $1,000 a month, the structure matters more than the amount. Spread thin across five campaigns at once, it learns nothing. Run in turns with retargeting underneath, it tells you within a season which audience earns the summer money.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Make the Numbers Tell the Truth

1. **Check what GA4 is counting as a conversion.**
   - Go to analytics.google.com and sign in with the same Google account you use for Google Ads and your Business Profile.
   - Open Admin (the gear icon, bottom left) → Data display → Events. Every event with a filled star in the "Mark as key event" column is being counted as a conversion.
   - The only events that should be starred are `purchase` (a FareHarbor booking) and, after Step 4, your private event inquiry. If `page_view`, `session_start`, `first_visit`, `scroll`, `click` or `form_start` is starred, click the star to turn it off.
   - Take a screenshot of the list before and after, and save both. If anything looks unfamiliar, send the screenshot to Junction before changing it.
   - Turning a star off stops it counting from that day forward. It does not rewrite past reports, so treat everything before the change date as unreliable.

2. **Confirm FareHarbor is reporting real bookings to GA4.**
   - In your FareHarbor Dashboard, go to Settings → Analytics & Tracking. Check that a GA4 measurement ID starting with `G-` is listed, and that it matches the one in GA4 under Admin → Data streams → your web stream.
   - On the same screen, look for the checkbox "Calendar, item grid, or book button is added to multiple websites." FareHarbor's guidance is that leaving it unticked can pull in bookings made through other websites that carry your booking flow, which inflates your numbers. If your booking calendar appears anywhere other than your own site, tick it. (When we checked, a third-party booking listing for Shoreline Tours appeared online, so this is worth confirming with FareHarbor Support.)
   - In GA4, go to Admin → Data streams → your web stream → Configure tag settings → Show all → List unwanted referrals. Add `fareharbor.com` and your own domain (without www). FareHarbor lists this as mandatory. Without it, bookings get credited to FareHarbor as a referral instead of to the ad or search that brought the guest.
   - Then do a reality check. In GA4, go to Reports → Monetization → Ecommerce purchases (or Reports → Engagement → Events → purchase) for the last 28 days and note the number of purchases. Run the bookings report in FareHarbor for the same 28 days and compare. They will never match exactly, but they should be in the same range. If GA4 shows far more, something other than bookings is being counted.

3. **Connect the Meta Pixel to the FareHarbor booking flow.**
   - Your pixel (ID 514399410953650) is on your Squarespace site, but the booking itself happens inside FareHarbor. Unless FareHarbor is also sending events to that pixel, Meta can see who visited but not who booked.
   - FareHarbor connects the Meta Pixel to its booking flow on request. Contact FareHarbor Support, give them the pixel ID, and ask them to confirm that the Purchase event will fire when a booking completes.
   - Once they confirm, open business.facebook.com → Events Manager → select the pixel → Overview. Within a day or two of bookings coming in, you should see `Purchase` listed with a recent "Last received" time.
   - Install the free Meta Pixel Helper extension in Chrome. Visit your homepage and click the extension icon. It should show your pixel firing a PageView. This confirms the base code is live.

4. **Track private event inquiries as their own conversion.**
   - Private events come through the inquiry form on your website, not FareHarbor, so today they are invisible to both GA4 and Meta.
   - Create a simple thank-you page in Squarespace: Pages → the "+" button → Blank page. Name it "Thank You" and set its URL to `/thank-you-inquiry`. Add one line ("Thanks, we will be in touch within one business day") and your phone number. Leave it in the Not Linked section so it does not appear in your menu.
   - Edit the inquiry form block (click the form → Edit). Under Post-Submit, choose Redirect and enter `/thank-you-inquiry`. [CONFIRM: URL of the private events inquiry page]
   - In GA4, go to Admin → Data display → Events → Create event. Name it `private_inquiry`, with the condition `event_name equals page_view` and `page_location contains thank-you-inquiry`. Save, then star it as a key event once it appears (usually within 24 hours of the first test).
   - In Meta Events Manager, go to Custom conversions → Create custom conversion. Choose your pixel, set the rule to "URL contains thank-you-inquiry", and name it "Private Inquiry."
   - Submit a test inquiry yourself and check that both appear.

5. **Write down your starting numbers.**
   - In FareHarbor, pull public tour bookings for June to September 2024 and June to September 2025, split into weekdays and weekends. This is the summer baseline the whole plan is measured against. [CONFIRM: summer 2024 and 2025 public tour bookings and revenue]
   - Record, in one sheet: monthly Meta spend, monthly Google spend, Google clicks and cost per click (currently 622 clicks, $212, $0.34), Instagram and Facebook followers (2,530 and 7,900), and posts per week (1.1).
   - Add the GA4 purchase count from Step 2 and the FareHarbor booking count for the same period.
   - Date the sheet and keep it. In Week 12 you will fill in the same numbers again.

#### Part B: Clean Up Google Ads

6. **Make bookings the only goal Google optimizes for.**
   - Your campaign is Performance Max, which means Google decides where to show the ads (Search, YouTube, Maps, Gmail and its display network) based on the conversions you tell it to chase. If it is chasing the wrong event, it will happily find cheap clicks that never book. This may be part of why your clicks cost 34 cents.
   - In Google Ads, go to Goals → Conversions → Summary. Look at the list of conversion actions and the "Primary" or "Secondary" label beside each one.
   - The GA4 `purchase` event should be the only Primary conversion. If it is missing, click "+ Create conversion action" → Import → Google Analytics 4 properties → Web, tick `purchase`, and import it. Set anything else (page views, engagement, contact clicks) to Secondary.
   - Once Step 4 is done, import `private_inquiry` the same way and set it to Secondary, so it is reported but does not pull summer budget away from public tour bookings.

7. **Clean up the search terms and separate your brand searches.**
   - Go to Campaigns → Insights and reports → Search terms (in some accounts this is under Campaigns → Audiences, keywords, and content → Search keywords). Set the date range to the last 90 days.
   - Sort by cost. For each term, ask: is this person likely to book a boat cruise? On the call you found "Harrison Hot Springs Resort" and possibly "Harrison Hot Springs restaurants." Both describe a hungry person or a hotel guest, not a cruise booker.
   - Add terms like these as negative keywords. Go to Campaigns → Audiences, keywords, and content → Search keywords → Negative keywords → "+" → choose the Shoreline campaign. Start with: resort, hotel, restaurant, restaurants, menu, jobs, hiring, weather, hot springs pool, camping. Add new ones every time you check.
   - Keep terms that show booking intent, like "Harrison boat tour", "boat tours near me", "Harrison Lake cruise" and "attractions near me" (29 clicks for $11.92 last month, which looks fine).
   - Check how much of your spend comes from people searching "Shoreline Tours" by name. If it is a large share, those are guests who were already looking for you, and the 89 paid conversions are overstated. Bring the figure to the Week 12 review. Do not exclude your brand name yet: it is cheap traffic, and stopping it is a decision to make with full numbers.
   - Repeat this check for 15 minutes every two weeks during booking season.

8. **Rewrite the ads for the season that is actually sailing.**
   - Go to Campaigns → your campaign → Asset groups → Edit. You currently have three headlines ("Shoreline Tours, Public Tours and Private Events, Cruise Harrison Lake"; "Shoreline Tours, Boat Tours, Knowledgeable Onboard Staff"; "Relaxing Lake Cruises, Harrison Lake Boat Tours") and matching descriptions.
   - Add headlines for every season and switch them on and off as the seasons change. In river season, for example: "Harrison River Eagle Cruises", "See Bald Eagles From the Deck", "River Cruises Oct to Nov". In summer: "2-Hour Harrison Lake Cruise", "Lake Cruises Daily May to Sept", "Room for Up to 35 Guests". Use ChatGPT to draft 15 headlines (30 characters each) and 4 descriptions (90 characters each) per season, then edit them.
   - Remove any headline about a cruise that is not running. A searcher who clicks "lake cruise" in October lands on a page that cannot sell them one.
   - In the campaign settings, find Final URL expansion and turn it off. Otherwise Google may send people to any page on your site, not the homepage you chose.
   - Leave the location targeting as it is (BC, Alberta and Washington). Someone in Alberta searching "Harrison Lake cruise" is planning a trip, and that is worth paying for.

9. **Test a Search campaign that repeats the guest's own words.**
   - Performance Max does not let you choose the exact searches you appear for. A standard Search campaign does, and it supports keyword insertion, which places the searcher's own words into your headline. Someone searching "family boat tour Harrison" sees a headline that says exactly that.
   - Do this only after Steps 6 to 8 have been running for at least four weeks, so you have a clean Performance Max baseline to compare against.
   - Go to Campaigns → "+" → New campaign → Sales → Search. Set the location to BC, Alberta and Washington, with "Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations." Untick Search Partners and Display Network.
   - Create one ad group with 10 to 15 phrase-match keywords of booking intent, for example "harrison lake cruise", "harrison boat tour", "harrison hot springs boat tour" and "things to do in harrison hot springs".
   - In a responsive search ad headline, type `{KeyWord:Harrison Lake Cruises}`. Google replaces it with the keyword that matched, or shows "Harrison Lake Cruises" if the keyword is too long.
   - Fund it by splitting your existing $10 a day: $5 to Search and $5 to Performance Max for four weeks. Then compare cost per booking and keep whichever wins.

#### Part C: Rebuild Meta Ads Around Four Audiences

10. **Move from Boost to Ads Manager and check the foundations.**
    - All campaigns from here on are built at adsmanager.facebook.com, not with the Boost button. Boost uses limited objectives and audience options, and it cannot run retargeting or lookalike campaigns properly.
    - In Business Settings (business.facebook.com → Settings) → Brand safety and suitability → Domains, check that your website domain is listed as verified. If it is not, follow Meta's steps to verify it with a meta tag in Squarespace (Settings → Advanced → Code Injection → Header). [CONFIRM: website domain]
    - In Billing and payments, confirm the payment method is current and there are no account holds.
    - Pause any boosted posts that are still running.

11. **Build the warm audiences.**
    - In Ads Manager, open the menu → Audiences → Create audience → Custom audience. Build each of these and name them clearly:
    - "IG Engagers 365": source Instagram account, everyone who engaged with your profile in the past 365 days.
    - "FB Engagers 365": source Facebook Page, everyone who engaged with your Page in the past 365 days.
    - "Video Viewers 50%": source Video, people who watched at least 50 percent of any of your Reels or videos in the past 365 days.
    - "Site Visitors 180": source Website, all website visitors in the past 180 days, using your pixel.
    - "Bookers 180": source Website, people who triggered Purchase in the past 180 days. This is your exclusion list, so people who have just booked do not keep seeing "book now" ads. It is also the seed for Step 16. This one fills only after Step 3 is working.
    - Check each audience's estimated size on the Audiences page and write it in your baseline sheet.

12. **Launch the retargeting campaign and leave it running.**
    - Create a campaign with the Sales objective. Set the conversion location to Website and the conversion event to Purchase. If Purchase is not yet firing, use the Traffic objective optimizing for landing page views, and switch once Step 3 is confirmed.
    - Audience: the four warm audiences from Step 11 combined, excluding "Bookers 180". Location: the Lower Mainland and Washington State.
    - Creative: direct and specific. Name the cruise that is bookable now, the dates, the price ($79 and $70 as shown on your site, or the current prices), and a reason to book now. "Summer weekends sell out first" is the sort of line Conner described. These people already know you, so this is where you can be direct.
    - Budget: $5 a day off-season and $8 a day in summer. This campaign stays on all year. When you are closed, point it at the next season's dates if they are bookable, or pause it if nothing is.

13. **Build the four audience campaigns.**
    - Create four Sales campaigns, one per audience, each optimizing for Purchase (or Private Inquiry for the groups campaign). Each one excludes "Bookers 180".
    - Families (summer public sailings): Lower Mainland, age 28 to 55 as a suggestion. Message: a two-hour lake cruise the whole family can do, from grandparents to toddlers. Destination: homepage for now.
    - Retirees and intentional travellers (spring and fall): Lower Mainland and the Fraser Valley, age 55 and up. Message: waterfall and eagle cruises, booked ahead, with commentary from an onboard team. Destination: homepage for now.
    - Washington State (summer and shoulder seasons): location Washington State only. Message: two nights in Harrison plus a cruise, with every price in Canadian dollars so a US dollar goes further. Check the exchange rate before you put a percentage in an ad, and update it when it moves. Destination: homepage for now.
    - Private groups (bachelorette and stag parties, staff parties, anniversaries, birthdays): Lower Mainland, age 25 to 45 for bachelorette and stag groups, or no age limit for corporate and family celebrations. Message: your own 62-foot yacht for up to 35 guests, with food and drinks on board. Destination: the private events inquiry page.
    - On the audience screen, Meta will offer Advantage+ audience. Location is always a firm limit. Age is treated as a suggestion unless you choose "Switch to original audience options", which makes it firm. Use the suggestion setting for Families and Groups and the firm setting for Retirees, matching the 35-and-up rule you already use for river tours.
    - Keep interests empty. Conner's experience across many accounts is that Meta finds the right people better when targeting is broad and the creative does the work.

14. **Load every format you have and let Meta choose.**
    - In each campaign, add six to ten ads with different formats: a Reel, a short 10-second clip, a longer day-on-the-boat video, a carousel of stills, a single photo, and any creator content you have permission to use (Step 17).
    - Write two or three versions of the main text for each campaign with ChatGPT, then edit them. Meta will mix and match.
    - Meta automatically shifts budget toward the ads that perform. You do not need to watch it daily.
    - After two weeks, open the campaign → Ads tab, sort by cost per result, and switch off any ad that has spent more than about $30 without a single booking or inquiry. Replace it with a winner from your organic posts (Tactic 3, Step 10).

15. **Run the four campaigns in two-week turns.**
    - At around $1,000 a month, four campaigns running at once would each get too little to learn anything. Run one at a time instead, for two weeks each, with retargeting always on underneath.
    - Budget split: in summer, about $25 a day on the active campaign and $8 on retargeting, which totals roughly $1,000 a month. Off-season, about $20 a day on the active campaign and $5 on retargeting, roughly $750 a month.
    - Choose the order by what is bookable next. If the next sellable dates are spring waterfall cruises, start with Retirees. If summer is already open, start with Families and Washington. Run Private Groups early in any order, because group bookings are made further ahead than summer public sailings, which are often booked the night before.
    - At the end of each block, record spend, bookings (or inquiries) and cost per booking in your sheet. After all four have had a turn, give the extra budget to the cheapest and keep the rest rotating.
    - Never switch off retargeting to fund a test. It is the campaign most likely to return the most per dollar.

16. **Add a lookalike campaign once you have enough bookers.**
    - A lookalike audience is a group of people Meta judges similar to an audience you give it. Built from your bookers, it is usually the best new-customer audience you can buy.
    - Once "Bookers 180" has at least 100 people (Meta's minimum for a lookalike source), go to Audiences → Create audience → Lookalike audience. Choose "Bookers 180" as the source, Canada as the location, and 10 percent as the size. Repeat with United States as the location for a Washington version.
    - In the ad set, narrow the location to the Lower Mainland (or Washington State for the US version). Meta builds lookalikes country-wide, so this step stops it reaching Quebec or Florida.
    - Run it as its own two-week block in the rotation from Step 15, using your best-performing creative from the four audience campaigns.

#### Part D: Turn Creator Visits Into Ads

17. **Get a simple release in writing before every hosted visit.**
    - You host creators often (from Tourism Harrison, SLAP and direct contacts), and their posts have drawn 106 and 138 comments. Today there is no agreement, so you cannot run their content as ads without asking after the fact.
    - Write a one-page agreement and send it before each visit. It should cover: what you provide (the cruise for two, drinks on board), what they provide (for example, one Reel and five stills), permission for Shoreline Tours to use the content in paid ads for 12 months, a link to your website in any blog or portfolio post, and collab-post tagging on Instagram.
    - Ask Tourism Harrison whether they already use a standard creator release you can adapt. If they do, use theirs.
    - Ask for raw clips as well as the finished post. Raw vertical clips are the most useful ad material you can get.

18. **Run creator posts as partnership ads.**
    - When a creator posts about you as a collab post, you can run that post as an ad from both accounts, which Meta calls a partnership ad. People engage more with a creator's face and voice than with a brand.
    - The creator has to give permission inside Instagram. Ask them to add Shoreline Tours as a brand partner on the post and allow you to run it as an ad (Instagram places this in the post's partnership label and ad settings), or to send you a partnership ad code. Instagram moves these menus often, so the creator may need to search its help pages for "partnership ads".
    - In Ads Manager, when creating the ad, switch on the partnership ad option in the ad setup and select the creator's post or enter the code. Put the permission request in the release from Step 17 so it is agreed before the visit.
    - Run it first in the retargeting campaign, where warm audiences will recognize you, and then in whichever audience campaign matches the creator. A family creator goes to Families. A couple goes to Retirees.

### Tips

- If you only do one thing in Part A, do Step 2's reality check. Comparing GA4's purchase count to FareHarbor's booking count for the same dates tells you in five minutes whether the reports can be trusted.
- Do not judge Google by cost per click. Thirty-four cents a click is only good news if those clicks book. Cost per booking is the number that matters.
- Change one thing at a time in each campaign, and write the date in your sheet. When something improves, you will know why.
- Use ChatGPT the way you already do, but give it the audience. "Write 10 Meta ad headlines for a retired couple from Langley who has never been on Harrison Lake" gets far better results than "write a boat tour ad."
- In July, when you have no time at all, the only Meta task that matters is checking that retargeting is running and the current campaign is on the right season.

## Tactic 2: Send Every Ad to the Page That Matches It
Phase: Conversion Path
Short Label: Landing Pages
Subtitle: Use the homepage as the ad destination today, fix what slows it down, and build one page per cruise so each season's ads land somewhere written for them.

### What We Heard From You

You told us many guests do not realize you run a different cruise each season, which is why the homepage leads with "Choose Your Adventure": lake cruise May to September, waterfall cruise February to April, river cruise October and November. Visitors see the month and click. Right now every Google ad sends people to the homepage. On the call you thought about pointing them at the River Tours page instead, and we agreed to take a second look before you change anything. You also said the original site was built for you about six years ago and that you update photos and text but leave the layout alone, because you are not confident with it. You told us you had not acted on the assessment's heading fixes because you were not sure what "Heading 1" meant.

### What This Is

Two stages of the same job. Right now, the homepage stays the destination for your ads, because it explains who you are and shows every season in one place. That makes it a better first page for a stranger than any single tour page on your site today. The first stage makes the homepage ready for paid traffic: correct headings, a title and description that name the seasons, a phone number last-minute callers can find, and tagged ad links so you can tell campaigns apart. The second stage builds one complete page for each cruise, plus one for private groups, in the same structure each time. Each season's ads then point at the page that matches them, and you switch them as the seasons change.

### Why It Matters

When someone clicks an ad about eagles and lands on a page that talks about eagles, uses the same picture and has a booking button for the eagle cruise, they are far more likely to book than if they land on a general page and have to find the cruise themselves. Conner estimated the right page could lift conversion by 30 to 40 percent, and the only way to know is to test it. The homepage is the right page today because the tour pages do not yet give a first-time visitor enough context about where you are and what the boat is. Building that context into each cruise page means you can stop routing every season through one page. It also gives Google a page per cruise to rank for searches like "Harrison eagle cruise" or "bachelorette boat party BC", which the homepage cannot do alone.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Make the Homepage Ready for Ad Traffic

1. **Point every ad at the homepage for now.**
   - In Google Ads, open your campaign → Asset groups → Edit and check that the Final URL is your homepage, with Final URL expansion off (Tactic 1, Step 8). [CONFIRM: website domain]
   - In Meta, set the website URL on every ad in the four audience campaigns and the retargeting campaign to the homepage. The exception is the private groups campaign, which goes to the inquiry page.
   - Why the homepage and not the River Tours page: the homepage opens with a large photo of the boat, explains the business, and shows all three seasons with their dates. A tour page assumes the visitor already knows who you are. Until each tour page carries that context (Part B), the homepage is the safer landing spot.
   - Make one change to the homepage each season: update the main heading or the line under it to name what is sailing now. In river season, for example, "River and Eagle Cruises, October and November." Guests who arrive from an eagle ad then see eagles named first.

2. **Fix the homepage headings.**
   - Headings tell Google what a page is about. Each page should have exactly one Heading 1 (the page's main topic), then Heading 2s for sections and Heading 3s for items inside a section. The assessment found four Heading 1s on your homepage: "Shoreline Tours | Harrison Hot Springs Tours", "CHOOSE YOUR ADVENTURE", "Come cruise with us", and one empty one.
   - In Squarespace, open Pages → Home → Edit. Click into the "CHOOSE YOUR ADVENTURE" text. A small toolbar appears. Open the dropdown that shows the text style (it will say "Heading 1") and choose "Heading 2".
   - Do the same for "Come cruise with us": change it to Heading 2.
   - Click into each of the season card titles (Lake Cruise, Waterfall Cruise, River Cruise) and set them to Heading 3.
   - The empty Heading 1 is usually a blank line inside a text block that still has the Heading 1 style. Click at the end of each text block near the top of the page and press Delete or Backspace to remove any empty lines. If you cannot find it, send Junction a screenshot of the page in edit mode.
   - Keep "Shoreline Tours | Harrison Hot Springs Tours" as the only Heading 1. Save.
   - Changing a style may change how big the text looks. If "CHOOSE YOUR ADVENTURE" looks too small as a Heading 2, adjust its size in the same toolbar rather than switching it back.

3. **Rewrite the homepage title and description.**
   - The title tag is the blue headline Google shows in search results. Yours is 72 characters and gets cut off. The meta description is the grey text underneath. Yours is 119 characters and does not mention the seasons.
   - In Squarespace, go to Pages → hover over Home → click the gear icon → SEO tab.
   - SEO title (59 characters): `Shoreline Tours | Harrison Hot Springs Lake & River Cruises`
   - SEO description (146 characters): `Harrison Lake and River cruises on a 62-foot yacht. Waterfall cruises Feb to Apr, lake cruises May to Sept, eagle cruises Oct to Nov. Book online.`
   - Save. Google can take a few weeks to show the new text.

4. **Put the phone number where last-minute callers will see it.**
   - Many of your guests call before booking, especially older guests and same-weekend summer bookers. The assessment found your number only in the footer.
   - In Squarespace, open Edit on any page, click the site header → Edit Site Header → Elements. If the header button is free, turn it on, set the label to "Call (604) 796-3100", and set the link to `tel:6047963100`.
   - If the header button is already used for Book Now (keep it if so), add the number to an announcement bar instead. In the Squarespace menu, open Website Tools → Announcement Bar (on older layouts, Design → Announcement Bar). Use text like "Questions? Call us at (604) 796-3100" and link it to `tel:6047963100`.
   - Check it on your phone. Tapping the number should open the dialler.

5. **Tag every ad link so you can tell campaigns apart.**
   - Without tags, GA4 lumps all Facebook and Instagram visits together. With tags, you can see which campaign brought each booking.
   - Google: in Google Ads, go to Admin → Account settings → Auto-tagging and check it is on. Google then tags its own links.
   - Meta: in each ad, scroll to Tracking → URL parameters → Build a URL parameter, and enter: `utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign={{campaign.name}}&utm_content={{ad.name}}`. Meta fills in the campaign and ad names automatically.
   - Name your campaigns plainly ("Families Summer", "Retirees Spring Fall", "Washington", "Groups", "Retargeting") so the reports make sense later.
   - In GA4, you will find these under Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition. Change the table's first column to "Session campaign".

#### Part B: Build One Page per Cruise

6. **Decide the four pages.**
   - Lake Cruise (summer, families and Washington visitors), Waterfall Cruise (spring), River and Eagle Cruise (fall), and Private Charters and Groups (bachelorette and stag parties, staff parties, anniversaries, birthdays).
   - Where a tour page already exists, rebuild it rather than creating a second page about the same cruise. Two pages competing for the same search confuse Google. [CONFIRM: URLs of the existing lake, waterfall, river and private events pages]
   - Build them in this order: Lake Cruise first, because summer is the season this plan exists for, then Private Charters and Groups, then whichever season is next after summer.

7. **Back up each existing page before you change it.**
   - In Squarespace, go to Pages → hover over the tour page → gear icon → General → Duplicate Page. The copy appears in the Not Linked section with "(Copy)" in its name.
   - Rename the copy, for example "Lake Cruise BACKUP". In its gear icon → SEO tab, turn on "Hide page from search results" so Google never indexes it.
   - Now edit the original page, which keeps its address and any search ranking it already has. If something goes wrong, the backup has the old version.
   - For a page that does not exist yet (possibly Private Charters and Groups), duplicate the finished Lake Cruise page once it is done, move the copy into your menu, and rewrite it. This keeps the layout consistent and means you never have to build a layout from scratch.

8. **Write each page to the same structure.**
   - Top of the page, visible without scrolling on a phone: a headline that matches the ad (for example, "Two-Hour Harrison Lake Cruise"), one line on where and when ("Departs the Harrison Hot Springs dock at 100 Esplanade Avenue, May to September"), the price, and a Book Now button.
   - The Book Now button must open the booking calendar for that specific cruise, not the general one. In edit mode, click the Book button on your current tour page to see which FareHarbor link it uses, or ask FareHarbor Support for the item link for each cruise.
   - Then, in this order: what you will see (the falls, the eagles, the river mouth), what is on board (indoor and outdoor decks, food and drinks), who it suits (families, grandparents, anyone who does not want a strenuous outing), accessibility details, what to bring, the weather and cancellation policy, three recent Google reviews that mention this cruise, a short FAQ, and the phone number.
   - The FAQ answers the questions you get on the phone. Start with these: Do I need to be staying at the resort? Is it suitable for young children and grandparents? What happens if the weather is bad? Where do I park? Are prices in Canadian dollars? Use ChatGPT to tidy your answers, but write the facts yourself.
   - End with a short "Other seasons" strip linking to the other cruise pages.
   - The Private Charters and Groups page swaps the Book button for the inquiry form, names the occasions you host, gives the guest capacity (up to 35), and gives an idea of what food and drink can be arranged, even if the full catering menu stays private. Use the form redirect from Tactic 1, Step 4 so inquiries are tracked.

9. **Set the headings, title and address for each page.**
   - One Heading 1 per page, matching what people search: "Harrison Lake Cruise", "Harrison Lake Waterfall Cruise", "Harrison River Eagle Cruise", "Private Boat Charters on Harrison Lake". Sections are Heading 2s.
   - In the gear icon → SEO tab, write a title under 60 characters and a description of 140 to 155 characters for each page. For example: `Harrison Lake Cruise | 2-Hour Boat Tour | Shoreline Tours`.
   - In the gear icon → General, check the URL slug is short and plain, such as `/lake-cruise`. If you change the slug of an existing page, add a redirect from the old address (Settings → Developer tools → URL mappings) so old links and search results still work.
   - Add alt text to every image: click the image → Edit → find the alt text field, and describe what is in the photo ("Guests on the upper deck passing Rainbow Falls on Harrison Lake").

10. **Test every page on your phone before any ad uses it.**
    - Open each page on your phone, not your computer. Most ad clicks come from phones.
    - Check that the headline, price and Book Now button are visible without scrolling, the button opens the right cruise in FareHarbor, the phone number dials, and the page loads quickly.
    - Ask one person who has never booked with you to find the price and the departure point in under 30 seconds. If they cannot, move that information higher up the page.
    - Use Meta Pixel Helper (Tactic 1, Step 3) on your computer to confirm the pixel fires on each new page.

#### Part C: Switch the Ads Over

11. **Map each ad to its page and set the switching dates.**
    - In your tracking sheet, add a simple table with three columns: campaign, season, and destination page.
    - Families and Washington point at Lake Cruise from the date summer sailings open for booking until the end of September. Retirees points at Waterfall Cruise in late winter and spring, then River and Eagle Cruise from late summer onward, since fall eagle guests book weeks to a month ahead. Groups always points at Private Charters and Groups. Retargeting points at whichever cruise is bookable next.
    - Put the switching dates in your calendar with a reminder. The most common mistake, and the one you found on the call, is an ad still promoting a cruise that is not running.
    - Do the same for Google: when you switch season, update the headlines (Tactic 1, Step 8). Once the cruise pages are live, change the Final URL to the matching page.

12. **Move one campaign at a time and compare against the homepage.**
    - Start with the campaign whose season is bookable now and whose page is finished.
    - Change only the destination URL. Leave the budget, audience and ads the same, so the page is the only thing that changes.
    - Run it for two weeks and compare against the previous two weeks on the homepage: landing page views, bookings, and cost per booking. Find these in Ads Manager by adding the "Landing page views" and "Purchases" columns.
    - If the cruise page does as well or better, keep it and move the next campaign. If it does worse, check Step 10 again (usually the button or the price is too far down the page), fix it, and retest.
    - Season changes can make any two-week comparison noisy. If the numbers are close, keep the cruise page: it matches the ad, and its benefit grows as more guests see it.

## Tactic 3: A Clip-and-Post Habit That Feeds the Ads
Phase: Content Rhythm
Short Label: Social Content
Subtitle: Capture short clips on every sailing, post three times a week in a few repeatable formats, and move whatever performs organically into the ad account.

### What We Heard From You

You told us it is "really hard for me to even, like, post an Instagram video, like, 3 times a week" in summer, because you run events, hiring, scheduling and social media yourself. You also told us what changed your mind: a two-minute clip of fish over the side of the boat with "POV" text drew 2,000 views. "I need to stop thinking like, oh, I need to make this big fancy reel," you said. "It can just take five minutes." You have noticed that simple scenery posts beat "buy a ticket" posts, and that you already ask creators to invite you as a collaborator. You have a full camera roll, new photography coming from Tourism Harrison, and an Instagram audience that engages at 2.13 percent when you do post. The assessment found you posting 1.1 times a week.

### What This Is

A small system that turns time already spent on the boat into posts, and posts into ads. You capture a few short clips on every sailing, keep them sorted, and turn them into posts using five formats you repeat rather than reinvent. You schedule posts a month at a time in Meta Business Suite so the account keeps going when you are busy. Once a week you check which posts did best, and the winners become ad creative for Tactic 1. Organic posting is the free testing ground. Paid ads are where the winners earn their keep.

### Why It Matters

Meta's ads work best with plenty of creative, and the best creative is the kind your followers already responded to for free. Without a steady supply of clips, the four audience campaigns in Tactic 1 run on the same few ads all season and tire out. Your own data shows what works: Reels make up 67 percent of your content, and your FLUXX Summer Series post drew 8.62 percent engagement, more than three times your average. Simple formats posted consistently beat polished ones posted rarely. Three posts a week is realistic if the clips are already on your phone and the posts are scheduled ahead.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Build the Clip Library

1. **Shoot five clips on every sailing.**
   - Vertical, 5 to 15 seconds each, no talking needed. One of each: the view off the bow, something over the side (fish, eagles, the falls), guests reacting, the crew at work, and the boat from the dock or another angle.
   - Do it during moments you are already standing still, such as boarding, the turnaround and docking. It takes about two minutes per sailing.
   - Hold the phone steady and let each clip run a few seconds longer than feels necessary. Clips can be trimmed, but not stretched.
   - Ask guests before filming them up close, and only post faces of people who have said yes.
   - This is a task you can hand to any crew member who is on board. Tell them the five shots and they can do it.

2. **Sort the clips the same day.**
   - On your phone, create albums: Lake, Waterfall, River, Guests, Crew, Events and Groups. Before the end of the day, move that day's clips into the right album and delete the bad ones.
   - Favorite (heart) the two or three best of each day. Those are the first ones you will reach for.
   - Once a month, back the albums up to Google Drive or another cloud folder so nothing is lost if the phone breaks.

3. **Collect the content other people are making about you.**
   - Ask Tourism Harrison for the photos and video from the photographers you hosted, along with written confirmation of how you can use them, including in ads.
   - Save every creator's raw clips and finished posts (with the release from Tactic 1, Step 17) into a "Creators" album.
   - When guests tag you, reply and ask whether you can share it. If they say yes, screenshot the permission and save the post.
   - When the Vancouver Is Awesome eagles piece publishes, save it and share it as a post.

#### Part B: Five Formats You Repeat

4. **Pick five formats and stop inventing new ones.**
   - POV: one clip, text over the top. "POV: looking over the side on a Harrison River cruise." This is the format that drew 2,000 views.
   - Day on the boat: five or six clips cut together with music, 15 to 20 seconds, from boarding to docking. The kind of montage Conner pointed to from Nimmo Bay and Pacific Sands.
   - Guest moment: a birthday, an anniversary, grandparents with grandchildren, a bachelorette group toast. These show the private and group side of the business without a hard sell.
   - Behind the scenes: your dad at the helm, the crew prepping food, setting up for a charter. A family business is a story people follow.
   - Season and events: what is sailing now and what is coming next, plus special events like the FLUXX Summer Series, which is your best-performing post on record.
   - Rotate them in order, so a week might be POV, Guest moment, Season. No single post has to carry the whole account.

5. **Write captions in batches with ChatGPT.**
   - Once a month, give ChatGPT a short brief: "I run a family boat cruise company on Harrison Lake and River in BC. Write 12 short Instagram captions, one or two sentences each, plain and friendly, no hashtag lists, for these posts:" and then list the 12 clips in a line each.
   - Edit what it gives you so it sounds like you. Keep the first line short, because that is all most people read.
   - Put booking information in the caption only for Season posts. Your own experience is that "buy a ticket" posts do worse than simple scenery.
   - Keep three to five relevant hashtags and a location tag (Harrison Hot Springs, Harrison Lake or Harrison River) on every post.

#### Part C: A Posting Rhythm You Can Keep in July

6. **Set the cadence to three posts a week, and decide who posts.**
   - Three posts a week, on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, all year. The assessment suggests five a week in peak season. Try it only if three is already running easily.
   - In the closed months, still post three times a week from the library: highlights from the past season, what is coming next, and behind-the-scenes preparation for spring.
   - You own this. If someone on your team takes on capturing clips or scheduling posts for the summer, write down exactly which tasks are theirs (for example, "shoot the five clips on every afternoon sailing" or "schedule Monday and Wednesday posts") and keep approval of anything posted under your name.

7. **Schedule a month at a time in Meta Business Suite.**
   - Go to business.facebook.com → Planner (or Content → Planner). Click Create post or Create reel, and choose both Facebook and Instagram so one upload covers both accounts.
   - Add the clip, paste the caption, then click the arrow next to Publish → Schedule, and pick the date and time. Evenings between 6 and 8 p.m. are a reasonable start. Check your own Insights after a month and adjust.
   - Block out one two-hour session at the start of each month to schedule all twelve or thirteen posts. In summer, do it before the season starts and schedule six weeks at once.
   - Leave one slot a week empty for something that happens that week, such as a sighting, a great sunset or a creator visit.

8. **Make every collaboration a collab post.**
   - You already ask creators to invite you as a collaborator, so the post appears on both accounts. Keep doing it, and make it a line in the release from Tactic 1, Step 17.
   - Do the same with local businesses you have worked with on Instagram collabs, and with Tourism Harrison when they feature you.
   - When you post a creator's content yourself, invite them as a collaborator so it reaches their audience too.

#### Part D: Let Organic Choose the Ads

9. **Check the week's posts every Friday.**
   - Ten minutes. In Meta Business Suite → Insights → Content, look at the posts from the past week. Note views, shares and saves for each.
   - Mark any post that did clearly better than your usual (for example, twice your normal views, or any post with unusually high shares). Those are your candidates.
   - Write the winners in a "Winners" column in your tracking sheet with the format, season and date. After a couple of months you will see which formats win for which season.

10. **Move the winners into the ad account.**
    - Each fortnight, add the best one or two winners as new ads in the campaign that matches them: a family post to Families, an eagle POV to Retirees, a group toast to Groups.
    - In Ads Manager, create the ad and choose "Use existing post" so the likes and comments come with it.
    - Replace the weakest ad in that campaign at the same time (Tactic 1, Step 14), so each campaign keeps six to ten ads and always has fresh ones.
    - A post that did poorly organically is not banned from ads, but start with the proven ones.

### Tips

- If you have not posted by Friday, post a POV clip. It takes two minutes and it is your best-performing format so far.
- Text on screen beats a caption. Many people watch without sound and never open the caption.
- Shoot more of the boring things than feels right: the dock in the morning, the rope, the coffee. They become the in-between shots of every montage.
- Do not chase followers. The job of these posts is to warm people up for the ads and find the next winning creative. Follower growth is a side effect.
- Keep a list of every creator who has visited and when. A thank-you message and a share of their post when they publish is what gets them back.

## GBP Quick Wins

These are small jobs, most under an hour, that improve how you show up on Google. None of them depend on anything else in the plan. The heading, title and description fixes from the assessment are covered in Tactic 2, so they are not repeated here.

### On the Google Business Profile

- **Reply to every review within 48 hours.** You have 263 reviews at 4.8 stars, and many mention staff by name and celebrations on board. Thank the guest, mention the specific thing they said, and name the cruise they took. Future guests read the replies, and Google treats them as a sign the business is active.
- **Set your closed-season hours.** Before you close in December, add special hours (or mark the business as temporarily closed with a reopening date) so Google does not show you as open when you are not. Change them back before spring sailings start.
- **Post once per season.** Add a Google post when each season opens: what is sailing, the dates, and a Book button linked to the homepage or the matching cruise page.
- **Refresh the photos with each season.** Add five new photos per season from the Tourism Harrison shoot and your clip library: the boat, guests on deck, the falls, the eagles, and a group celebration.
- **Check the booking link.** Confirm that the profile's website and booking links go to your current homepage or FareHarbor, not an old page.

### On the Website

- **Add alt text to the four adventure images.** The assessment found four images on the site without alt text, in the adventure category section. Click each image → Edit → alt text, and describe it plainly, for example "Harrison Lake waterfall cruise boat tour passing Rainbow Falls."
- **Ask Vancouver Is Awesome for a link.** When the eagles interview publishes, email the reporter, thank them, and ask that the article link to your river cruise page. A link from a well-read Vancouver site is the kind that lifts your search rankings.
- **Check your Tourism Harrison and Destination BC listings.** Make sure each listing links to your website (not just your social pages), shows current season dates, and uses the same name, address and phone number as your Google profile.
- **Ask every creator with a blog or portfolio page to link to you.** Instagram posts do not help your search ranking. A link from their website does. Add this request to the creator release in Tactic 1, Step 17.
