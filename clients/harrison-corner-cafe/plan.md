## Goal

Harrison Corner Cafe already has the reputation most restaurants spend years trying to build. Ten years on Hot Springs Road, 563 Google reviews at 4.5 stars, 76 percent of them five-star, and 80 percent of them written in the last eighteen months. You rank first on Google for your own name and third for "breakfast harrison hot springs." Hotels and the resort send their guests to you. Your regulars keep coming back. None of that came from marketing. It came from the food and the room.

The gap is everything you put online yourself. Instagram has 989 followers and gets a new post roughly once every five weeks. The photos on your Google profile are all from customers. The website loads fast but does not show your hours, has no main heading for Google to read, and has no analytics, so nobody can see who visits or where they came from. Your reputation works well on people who are already in town. It does much less for the people you said you want next: day trippers from Chilliwack, thirty minutes away, through to Abbotsford, who come for the lake, the hot springs or the new pool in Agassiz and decide on breakfast the night before or that morning, on their phone. They look in two places, Google and Instagram, and right now you are visible in one and close to silent in the other.

This plan is built around how you actually work. You have a few hours a week, mostly yours, and you do not want a daily commitment. You and your husband will not be on camera. You prefer fewer, better posts. And you told us the real bottleneck is not posting, it is having the photos and clips ready when you sit down. So the plan runs on one monthly content session that feeds everything else: the Instagram and Facebook posts, the Google profile photos and the new website pages. Two posts a week go out on a schedule you set in one sitting. Reviews get fifteen minutes every Monday. The website work is written as a task list your husband could take over.

The order matters. Month 1 sets up the measurement, tidies the Google profile and runs the first content session, so the first scheduled posts go out by Week 4. Month 2 settles the posting rhythm and adds the first new website pages. Month 3 holds the rhythm, finishes the pages, including a Work Here page for kitchen hiring, and gets the cafe ready for the winter break.

To be honest about timing: these ninety days run through the quietest part of your year and into your winter closure, so you will not see the full payoff inside them. What you will have by the end is a Google profile that looks cared for, a library of your own photos and video, a website that tells Google what you are, a posting habit that survives a busy week, and a head start on the pizza dinner service and on hiring before next season. The real test is the spring reopening, when the day-trip traffic comes back and finds a cafe that is easy to discover.

## Strategic Positioning

When we asked what your best regulars tell their friends, your answer was quality and made to order. Everything is cooked fresh, with good ingredients and real technique. You gave the example of a guest who asked what you add to the scrambled eggs to make them so creamy, and the answer was nothing: freshly cracked eggs, whipped, cooked properly. That story is your positioning. Hotel breakfast buffets and the other cafes on the tourist strip are not competing with you on price. A visitor scrolling at 8am is choosing whichever place looks worth the walk in the ten seconds they give it. Your reviews already say food, atmosphere and service. The job now is to show it.

You do not need to be a personality-led brand to do that. The food, the room and Harrison itself can be the face of the business. That fits the second idea you raised on the call: nobody drives to Harrison just for breakfast, they come for the whole day. So pair the day with the dish. The hiker, the camper, the fisher and the family at the lake each have a breakfast here that fits their morning. That gives Fraser Valley day trippers a reason to plan you into the trip before they leave home, and it gives you a content idea that does not run out.

> **Your Strategic Positioning**
>
> Harrison Corner Cafe is where a Harrison day starts properly: everything made to order, with fresh ingredients and real technique, by a family that has cooked on Hot Springs Road for ten years. Whatever brought you to Harrison, the lake, the trails or the hot springs, there is a breakfast here that fits it.

## Tactic 1: Run Your Google Business Profile Like a Front Window
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Google Profile
Subtitle: Keep your Google listing current with your own photos, regular posts and a weekly review routine, so the people searching "breakfast near me" pick you.

### What We Heard From You

You told us that this year you typed your full menu into your Google profile because going to a website to find a menu is annoying, and that you have noticed more people, and more takeout, since. You also said you have never added photos yourself, so everything on the profile came from customers, and you had not come across Google Posts until the call. You already reply to every review, good and bad, which very few businesses do. You also have a love-hate relationship with old reviews about staff who have left and dishes you no longer serve. We agreed a weekly routine is more realistic than chasing a 48-hour reply, and that a review QR code at the till would help.

### What This Is

Active management of the profile you already have, in three parts. First, record where the profile stands today and tidy the details a first-time visitor reads. Second, add your own photos every month and publish a Google Post every two weeks so the listing looks active. Third, turn review replies into a fifteen-minute Monday job and make it easy for happy guests to leave one.

### Why It Matters

For a restaurant, Google is the main marketing tool. It is where "breakfast near me" and "best breakfast Harrison Hot Springs" are answered, and you are already third for that second search, which gets around 320 searches a month. The first ranking signal is a steady flow of good reviews, which you have. The next is evidence that the business is active: fresh owner photos, recent posts and replies. Your menu experiment showed what happens when you make Google one step easier for a visitor. Photos and posts are the same idea applied to the rest of the listing, and they cost nothing but time.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Know Where You Start

1. **Record your starting numbers.**
   - On a computer, sign in to the Google account that manages the cafe, then search "Harrison Corner Cafe" on Google. Your profile management panel appears at the top of the results.
   - Click Performance. Set the date range to the last three months and write down profile views, calls, direction requests and website clicks.
   - Click Read reviews and note the total (563 at the time of the assessment) and the star rating (4.5).
   - Open Photos and check the "By owner" tab. It should be at or near zero. Write that down too.
   - Keep these numbers in one place, a note on your phone or a simple spreadsheet. Every target in this tactic is measured against them, and you will compare again in Week 12.

2. **Tidy the details a visitor reads first.**
   - From the same panel, click Edit profile.
   - Business description: rewrite it so the first sentence says what you are and where. For example: "Made-to-order breakfast and brunch on Hot Springs Road in Harrison Hot Springs, cooked fresh by a family-run kitchen for ten years." Then add a line on walk-ins: "Walk-ins welcome. For large groups and private functions, please call or email ahead."
   - Hours: confirm 9am to 2pm, closed Monday and Tuesday. Under Special hours, add any holiday closures as soon as you know them.
   - When your winter break dates are set, add them under Special hours too, along with the reopening date. A visitor who drives in from Chilliwack to a locked door leaves a one-star review, and one line on Google prevents it.
   - Menu: check that every item still exists and the prices are current. You mentioned complaints about dishes you no longer serve. A menu that matches the kitchen stops new ones.

#### Part B: Photos and Posts

3. **Add your own photos every month.**
   - After each monthly content session in Tactic 2, choose 8 to 10 of the best images and upload them to Google the same week.
   - On a computer, search your business name and click Add photo from the management panel, or go to Edit profile → Photos.
   - Cover four kinds of photo over the three months: signature dishes, the dining room with guests (hands and tables, no faces needed), the outside of the cafe from Hot Springs Road so people recognise it when they arrive, and any seasonal touches such as winter dishes or the patio in its last warm weeks.
   - Take dish photos in daylight near a window and keep them natural. Heavily edited or AI-generated food images put people off, which matches your instinct.
   - Do not delete customer photos. Owner photos add to them; they do not replace them.

4. **Publish a Google Post every two weeks.**
   - From the management panel, click Add update (it may sit under a Promote or Posts button, depending on your screen). Choose Add update, Add offer or Add event.
   - Use Posts for practical, decision-making information, not behind-the-scenes content. People reading your Google profile are already deciding where to eat. Good topics for these ninety days:
     - a signature dish with one line about how it is made;
     - "Walk-ins welcome, open 9 to 2, Wednesday to Sunday";
     - a seasonal dish;
     - your last weekend before the winter break and your reopening date;
     - a preview that pizza nights are coming next season.
   - Add one photo from the content library and keep the text to two or three sentences.
   - Add a button. Call now works for most posts. Once the Plan Your Visit page from Tactic 3 is live, use Learn more and link to it.
   - If you see a "Schedule this post" option at the bottom of the form, use it to set the next two posts in one sitting. If not, publish on the day.

#### Part C: Reviews

5. **Run a fifteen-minute review routine every Monday.**
   - Most of your reviews arrive over the weekend, so a Monday reply still reaches most guests within about 48 hours. The rule is simple: no review waits more than a week.
   - Search your business name on Google, click Read reviews, and reply to everything new since last Monday.
   - For good reviews, thank them and mention one specific thing they said, such as the dish, their server or their kids. A reply that names the eggs Benedict reads as real; "Thanks for the kind words!" does not.
   - For negative reviews, thank them, address the specific point in one sentence, and invite them to email you at harrisoncorner@outlook.com. Keep it short and calm. Future guests read the reply more than the complaint.
   - For complaints about a dish you no longer serve or staff who have since left, it is fair to say so: "That item is no longer on our menu" or "Our team has changed since your visit, and we would love to have you back." That turns an outdated review into a sign the business listens.

6. **Put a review QR code at the till and on the tables.**
   - The QR code can only be created on a computer, not a phone. Search your business name on Google, click Read reviews, then Get more reviews.
   - Copy the review link and save it somewhere you can find it. Then right-click the QR code and choose Save image as.
   - In Canva, which you already use, make a small card or table tent with the QR code and one line, for example: "Enjoyed breakfast? A quick Google review helps other visitors find us." Print enough for the till and every table.
   - Ask servers to mention it at payment when a table has clearly had a good time. People leave reviews when they are asked, and most simply never think to.
   - Never offer a discount, free item or anything else in exchange for a review. Google prohibits it and can remove reviews or restrict the profile.

7. **Check the profile's numbers once a month.**
   - On the first Monday of each month, after your review replies, open Performance and compare calls, direction requests, website clicks and profile views with your Step 1 baseline.
   - Business slows through this part of the year, so compare each month with the same month last year where Google shows it, rather than with the busy summer months.
   - Note which posts went out that month. Over time you will see which kinds of posts line up with more calls and direction requests.

### Tips

- Set a recurring Monday reminder on your phone called "Reviews, 15 minutes." The routine works because it is small. If it grows past fifteen minutes, you are writing replies that are too long.
- Upload Google photos in the same sitting you sort the content library in Tactic 2. Doing it later is how it stops happening.
- Your Google menu is already doing work. Whenever a dish changes, update Google the same day, before the website.
- When a review mentions a server by name, show it to them. It costs nothing and it is the kind of workplace the Work Here page in Tactic 3 will describe.
- If a review breaks Google's rules, for example it is about a different business or is plainly spam, you can report it from the review's three-dot menu. Reviews that are simply unfair usually stay, and a calm reply works better than a removal request.

## Tactic 2: Build a Content System That Runs on a Few Hours a Week
Phase: Content
Short Label: Social Content
Subtitle: One monthly content session and one scheduling sitting produce two Instagram and Facebook posts a week, with nobody on camera.

### What We Heard From You

You were clear that the hard part is not posting, it is having the material: "We have to take a photo of the specials, and then the specials run out." You described your current posting as journalistic, mostly updates like hours and new items, and said you are not interested in soup-of-the-day posts from restaurants you will never visit. Neither you nor your husband wants to be on camera, and you try to avoid AI-generated posts. You asked whether posting at 10pm matters, whether hashtags still work and how often is too often. You liked the idea of pairing Harrison moments with dishes and came up with the characters yourself: the camper, the hiker, the fisher. And you told us that going from zero to two posts would already make a difference.

### What This Is

A content system with three parts. First, a one-time setup: a short written profile of the cafe for ChatGPT, four content types, and the right free apps. Second, a two-hour content session once a month, where you plate four or five dishes for the camera, invite a few friends to eat them, and collect a month of photos and clips. Third, a scheduling sitting after each session, where you turn that material into Reels and carousels with search-friendly captions and schedule two a week to Instagram and Facebook at once. Stories are used separately, for news, specials and the pizza oven build.

### Why It Matters

Instagram is now a search engine. People type "best breakfast Harrison" into it the same way they type it into Google, and most of your views will come from people who do not follow you, because the platform shows each post to whoever it thinks is interested. That answers your over-posting worry: your followers are not shown every post, so two good posts a week will not annoy anyone. It also means follower count matters much less than captions that match what people search. The account already shows it can perform. Your average engagement when you post is 1.72 percent, and your best post reached 6.77 percent. The problem is volume, at 0.2 posts a week, and volume is exactly what a monthly session and one scheduling sitting fix.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Set It Up Once

1. **Write a one-page cafe profile for ChatGPT.**
   - Open a document in Notes or Word and write it the way you would describe the cafe to a new server. It does not need to be polished.
   - Include:
     - who you are (a husband-and-wife team, ten years on Hot Springs Road);
     - what you are known for (made to order, fresh ingredients, technique, with the scrambled eggs as your example);
     - your hours and the walk-in policy;
     - your four or five most popular dishes;
     - who you want to reach (Fraser Valley day trippers and weekend visitors);
     - the four content types from Step 2;
     - the rules: no faces of the owners, no AI-generated images, nothing that sounds like an advertisement.
   - When you sit down to plan, start a new ChatGPT chat, attach or paste the file, and ask something specific. For example: "Here are my clips from this month's session. Give me four Reel ideas and four carousel ideas I can make on my iPhone in under two hours." Or: "My brain is empty after the lunch rush. Based on this profile, give me three post ideas for this week."
   - If it suggests something too ambitious, like a tripod and actors, tell it to keep it simple and doable in thirty minutes with a phone. It adjusts well.
   - Use ChatGPT for ideas, shot lists and first drafts of captions only. Rewrite captions in your own words before posting, and never use it to generate food or cafe images.

2. **Choose four content types and stick to them.**
   - **Made to order.** Short clips or carousels showing how a dish is made: cracking the eggs, the whisk, the pan, the plate, the table. Tell one technique story per post. The scrambled eggs are the first one.
   - **The Harrison pairing.** A Harrison moment paired with a dish: the hiker's breakfast after the trail, the lake-day brunch, the warm-up after the hot springs, the fisher's early start. Two or three clips of the place, then the dish.
   - **What your order says about you.** A carousel with one popular dish per slide and a playful line on the kind of person who orders it. This was Conner's idea on the call and fits the way you already talk about guests.
   - **The people behind the pass.** Appreciation posts for the team: hands plating, a server carrying four plates, a thank-you caption. No one has to show their face. Over time this tells the people who see it that the cafe is a good place to work, which supports the hiring work in Tactic 3.
   - Aim for roughly equal amounts of the first two types, with the third and fourth once or twice a month each.

3. **Set up the free tools.**
   - Download **Edits** (made by Instagram) from the App Store or Google Play and sign in with the cafe's Instagram account. It is where you will cut clips together, add text over video and pick music.
   - Download **Meta Business Suite** and sign in. This is where you will schedule posts to Instagram and Facebook together. Instagram's own scheduler does not cross-post to Facebook, so Business Suite saves you doing everything twice.
   - In Business Suite, confirm that both the Instagram account and the Facebook page show at the top. If only one appears, connect the other through the app's settings before your first scheduling sitting.
   - Check that Instagram is set as a professional (business) account: Settings → Account type and tools. Scheduling and Insights need it.
   - In Canva, build one carousel template with your fonts and colours so every carousel looks like it came from the same place. Use it every time.

4. **Name your content helper.**
   - Choose the person who will help with photos and clips, for example the server who has already offered. Agree on an hour or two a month and what they will do: hold the camera at the content session and grab short clips of the room and the pass when it is safe to do so during service.
   - Show them the four content types and the shooting basics in Step 6, so they know what to capture without asking.
   - Ask staff and friends before posting anything that shows their face, and respect a no. Hands, backs and plates work perfectly well.

#### Part B: The Monthly Content Session

5. **Plan each session a week ahead.**
   - Pick a two-hour slot when the kitchen is quiet. Mondays and Tuesdays, when you are closed, are ideal.
   - Choose four or five dishes: two or three signature dishes, one seasonal dish, and one for the month's Harrison pairing.
   - Invite two to four friends for a free meal. The food gets eaten rather than wasted, and you get natural shots of a full table, hands reaching for plates and coffee being poured.
   - Write a one-line shot list per dish, or ask ChatGPT with your profile file to draft it: ingredients, prep, cooking, plating, the pass, landing on the table, first bite.

6. **Run the session.**
   - Plate each dish exactly as you want it photographed. These plates are made for the camera first and eaten second.
   - Shoot near a window or at the front of the room in daylight. Turn off overhead lights that give a yellow cast if the natural light is good enough.
   - Film video vertically (phone upright) for Reels and Stories. Take 5 to 10 second clips: one wide, one close, one moving shot of the plate being set down. Hold the phone still and let the action happen in the frame.
   - Take photos both upright and sideways. Upright works for Instagram, sideways for Google and the website.
   - Record the sounds too: sizzling, the whisk, a cup set down. Real sound often beats music.
   - Before your friends eat, get a wide shot of the full table and a few shots of hands sharing plates. Those are your carousel covers.
   - Aim for 40 to 60 photos and 20 to 30 short clips per session. That is enough for a month of posts plus Google and website photos.

7. **Collect Harrison moments as you go.**
   - Whenever you or your helper are at the lake, on a trail, at the hot springs or walking the beach, take two or three 5-second vertical clips. No people needed.
   - These are the first half of every Harrison pairing post. Without them, that content type cannot be made.
   - Save them straight into a "Harrison" album on your phone so they are ready at the scheduling sitting.

8. **Sort the library the same day.**
   - In your phone's Photos app, create an album for each month, for example "Content: Month 1". Inside it, use favourites or sub-albums for Dishes, Room, People and Harrison.
   - Delete blurry and duplicate shots straight away while you remember which were good.
   - Mark the 8 to 10 strongest sideways photos for the Google profile (Tactic 1, Step 3) and the website pages (Tactic 3).
   - Back the album up to Google Photos or iCloud so a lost phone does not mean a lost month.

#### Part C: Make, Caption and Schedule

9. **Build a batch of posts in one sitting.**
   - Set aside about two hours in the week after each session. Each sitting produces eight posts: four Reels and four carousels, which covers four weeks at two a week.
   - For a Reel: open Edits, start a new project, import four to six clips from the month's album, trim each to 1 to 3 seconds, and add one short line of text over the top, such as "No cream. Just eggs and technique." Add music from the Edits library or keep the real kitchen sound. Keep it to 7 to 15 seconds and export it to your camera roll.
   - For a carousel: choose 4 to 8 photos, open your Canva template, and add short text to the first slide so it works as a cover. For "What your order says about you," put one dish and one line on each slide.
   - Save the finished posts into a "Ready to schedule" album.

10. **Write captions people can find.**
    - Put the words people search for in the first sentence. Use phrases like "breakfast in Harrison Hot Springs," "brunch near Harrison Lake" and "family breakfast in Harrison," and name the dish. For example: "The best breakfast in Harrison Hot Springs starts with eggs cracked to order. No cream, no shortcuts, just technique."
    - Keep captions to two to four sentences. End with the practical line: "Walk-ins welcome, Wednesday to Sunday, 9 to 2, 310 Hot Springs Road."
    - Skip the long block of hashtags. As Conner mentioned on the call, the head of Instagram said recently that hashtags no longer help and can show a post to fewer people. The caption does the work now.
    - Write all eight captions in the same sitting while the ideas are fresh. ChatGPT with your profile file can give you a first draft, but the final version should sound like you.

11. **Schedule two posts a week to Instagram and Facebook at once.**
    - Open Meta Business Suite, tap Create, then Post or Reel. Select both the Instagram account and the Facebook page as places to publish.
    - Add the post from your "Ready to schedule" album and paste the caption.
    - Under the publishing options, choose Schedule and pick the date and time. Business Suite lets you schedule up to 29 days ahead, so one sitting covers the whole month.
    - Pick two fixed days, for example Wednesday and Saturday mornings. Exact times matter much less than they used to, because posts keep getting seen for weeks, but fixed days make it easy to see if one has been missed.
    - Check the Planner view when you finish. Eight posts lined up across four weeks means you are done until the next session.

12. **Use Stories for news and the pizza oven.**
    - Stories mostly reach your existing followers, so use them for the updates you already post: specials, hours changes, the winter break and reopening dates, and new menu items. One Story when there is news is enough. Nothing here needs to be daily.
    - Use them to let people in on the pizza oven: the oven arriving, test crusts, a flame shot, a "which topping should we try?" poll. No faces needed. When dinner service launches, followers will feel they were there from the start.
    - Share a Story whenever a scheduled post goes live, using the share button on the post. It takes ten seconds and gives the post a second push.
    - Stories cannot be scheduled with Instagram's built-in tools, so keep them for moments that happen on the day.

13. **Look at what worked once a month.**
    - At the start of each scheduling sitting, open Insights in the Instagram app (or the Insights tab in Edits for Reels) and check the last month's posts for views, shares and saves. Shares and saves are the strongest signs that a post is reaching new people.
    - Note which content type did best and make one extra post of that type in the next batch.
    - Paste the results into ChatGPT with your profile file and ask what to try next. Even if you ignore the answer, writing out the question usually makes the next idea obvious.

### Tips

- Protect the content session the way you would protect a delivery. If it slips, everything downstream slips with it. Put all three sessions in the calendar now.
- When you are mid-service and something looks good, a five-second clip is enough. Ask your helper to grab it so you do not have to leave the line.
- Do not wait for perfect. A clear, well-lit phone clip of eggs being folded will outperform a perfect photo that never gets posted.
- Reuse what works. A Reel that does well can be cut differently or posted again two months later with a new caption. Most of the people who see it will be seeing it for the first time.
- If a week goes wrong and nothing is scheduled, post one strong photo from the library with a search-friendly caption. One post keeps the rhythm alive; skipping the week tends to become skipping the month.
- Facebook is where older locals spend time, often in community groups. Once in a while, share a seasonal post to a Harrison community group if the group's rules allow it. It reaches people who will never follow the page.

## Tactic 3: Give Google More to Read on Your Website
Phase: Visibility
Short Label: Website & SEO
Subtitle: Turn a fast but thin website into one that tells Google what you are, answers a visitor's questions and helps you hire for the kitchen.

### What We Heard From You

You said you can edit the website yourself but that updating it is your least favourite job, and that it is "not the most reliable one," which is part of why you put the menu on Google. Conner's read on the call was that the site works and loads quickly, but there is very little text on it for Google to match to a search. You also told us that in ten years you have never found a cook who could keep up with the weekend rush: people say they can cook, and then the rush hits and they decide it is too much. You remembered that Boulevard in Vancouver offered a reward for kitchen referrals when your husband worked there, and you liked the idea of doing something similar publicly.

### What This Is

A short list of website jobs in three parts. First, install analytics, connect the site to Google Search Console and fix the homepage basics, including a main heading, a better page title and business details Google can read. Second, add three pages with real text on them: Plan Your Visit, Reviews and Our Story. Third, add a small Work Here page with a public referral reward for your next kitchen hire. Everything is done in the Wix editor and dashboard, and none of it needs a developer. The steps are written so your husband can take any of them over.

### Why It Matters

Google ranks pages by matching the words on them to the words people search. Right now your homepage has no main heading, your hours are not shown anywhere on the site, and most of the content is images, so Google has little to work with beyond your name. You are third for "breakfast harrison hot springs" largely on the strength of your reviews. More text on more pages, written around the searches visitors actually make, is how you move up for that and similar searches. Each page also answers a question that currently costs you a phone call during service or a wasted trip. Without analytics, nobody can tell which of this is working, which is why measurement goes in first.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Measurement and the Homepage

1. **Install Google Analytics 4.**
   - Go to analytics.google.com and sign in with the cafe's Google account. Click Start measuring, name the account "Harrison Corner Cafe," set the time zone to Pacific, and create a Web data stream for www.harrisoncornercafe.net.
   - Copy the Measurement ID. It starts with "G-".
   - In your Wix dashboard, go to Marketing Integrations (under Marketing & SEO), find Google Tag and click Connect, then paste the Measurement ID. This needs a paid Wix plan and a connected domain, which your site should already have.
   - Publish the site. Then open it on your phone and, in Google Analytics, go to Reports → Realtime. You should see yourself as a live visitor within a few minutes.
   - If the site shows a cookie banner, Analytics only records visitors who accept it, so expect the numbers to be a little lower than real traffic.

2. **Connect the site to Google Search Console.**
   - In your Wix dashboard, go to Marketing & SEO → SEO, open the SEO Setup Checklist, and click Connect to Google. Sign in with the cafe's Google account. Wix verifies the site and submits your sitemap for you.
   - Search Console is where you will see which searches show your site, such as "breakfast harrison hot springs," and your average position for each. That is how the ranking target in this tactic gets measured.
   - Wait a week for data to appear, then note the average position and clicks for your top five searches. That is your baseline.

3. **Add a main heading and rewrite the page title.**
   - In the Wix Editor, open the homepage. Click the main headline text near the top, click Edit Text, then SEO & accessibility, and set the HTML tag to Heading 1 (H1). If there is no headline, add one from Add Elements → Text → Heading 1.
   - Use one H1 on the page, for example: "Made-to-Order Breakfast and Brunch in Harrison Hot Springs." Every other heading on the page should be H2 or lower.
   - Open Pages & Menu, click the three dots next to Home, choose SEO basics, and set the title tag to something like "Harrison Corner Cafe | Breakfast in Harrison Hot Springs" (56 characters, under the 60 that Google shows).
   - In the same panel, write a meta description of about 150 characters, for example: "Made-to-order breakfast and brunch on Hot Springs Road in Harrison Hot Springs, BC. Fresh ingredients, real technique, walk-ins welcome. Open 9am to 2pm."
   - Publish, and repeat the title and description step for every new page in Part B.

4. **Fill in your Business Info so Google can read your details.**
   - In the Wix dashboard, go to Settings → Business Info. Enter the business name, category (restaurant or cafe), a short description, the address at 310 Hot Springs Road, phone and email.
   - When a name and location are saved, Wix automatically adds Local Business structured data to your homepage. This is the "schema" the assessment found missing. It helps Google show your details correctly in search results.
   - Make sure the name, address and phone match your Google profile exactly, down to the abbreviations.
   - To check it worked, go to Google's Rich Results Test (search.google.com/test/rich-results), enter your homepage address, and confirm a Local Business item appears.

#### Part B: Pages With Real Words on Them

5. **Build a Plan Your Visit page.**
   - In the Wix Editor, open Pages & Menu → Add Page and choose a blank page. Name it "Plan Your Visit."
   - Give it an H1 such as "Plan Your Visit to Harrison Corner Cafe in Harrison Hot Springs."
   - Cover the questions visitors ask by phone, each under its own short H2:
     - **Hours:** 9am to 2pm, closed Monday and Tuesday, and your winter break.
     - **Walk-ins:** walk-ins only, with large groups and private functions asked to call or email ahead.
     - **When it is busy:** weekend mornings are busiest, and arriving at opening is easiest.
     - **Where to park:** street, nearby lot, any time limits, whatever is accurate.
     - **Getting here:** the address, a map, and a line on how long the drive is from Chilliwack.
     - **Accessibility:** the accessibility details already on your site, moved here so visitors find them.
   - Add a short section called "Make a Day of It" with two or three Harrison ideas, such as the lake, the trails and the hot springs, each paired with a breakfast suggestion. It mirrors the Harrison pairing posts in Tactic 2 and gives Google more local words to match.
   - Use two or three sideways photos from the content library. For each image, click Settings and fill in the "What's in the image? Tell Google" field with a plain description, such as "Eggs Benedict on the patio at Harrison Corner Cafe."

6. **Build a Reviews page.**
   - Add a page named "What Our Guests Say" with an H1 such as "Why Guests Call Us the Best Breakfast in Harrison Hot Springs."
   - Copy 10 to 15 recent Google reviews into the page as text, with the reviewer's first name and last initial. Pick ones that mention specific dishes, the service or Harrison itself. Typed text is readable by Google; a review widget usually is not.
   - Open the page with a line about your rating and review count, for example "4.5 stars from more than 560 Google reviews," and update it every few months.
   - Add a button at the bottom linking to your Google review link from Tactic 1, Step 6, so happy guests can add their own.
   - Refresh four or five of the quotes every couple of months with new reviews from your Monday routine.

7. **Build an Our Story page.**
   - Add a page named "Our Story." This does not need photos of you or your husband. Photos of hands at work, the kitchen, the pass and the room tell the story well.
   - Write 300 to 500 words in your own voice, covering:
     - how and when you opened;
     - what ten years on Hot Springs Road has been like;
     - why everything is made to order;
     - the scrambled eggs story;
     - what you love about Harrison.
   - If writing it feels like a chore, paste your ChatGPT profile file into a chat and ask it to interview you one question at a time. Then turn your answers into the page in your own words.
   - Give the page an H1 such as "Our Story: Ten Years of Made-to-Order Breakfast in Harrison Hot Springs."

8. **Link the new pages so people and Google find them.**
   - In Pages & Menu, make sure Plan Your Visit, What Our Guests Say and Our Story all appear in the main menu. Put Plan Your Visit near the front.
   - On the homepage, add a short line under the H1 with links to Plan Your Visit and the Reviews page.
   - Set the title and meta description for each new page using SEO basics, as in Step 3.
   - Point the Learn more button on your Google Posts (Tactic 1, Step 4) to Plan Your Visit, and add the Plan Your Visit link to your Instagram bio.

#### Part C: Help the Kitchen Hire

9. **Add a Work Here page with a public referral reward.**
   - Add a page named "Work Here" and link it in the footer.
   - Keep it short. In three or four paragraphs, say what it is like to work with you: a small, family-run business, working directly with the owners, a fast-paced kitchen, regulars who come back for years, and room to learn and grow. Be honest that the weekend rush is intense. The right cook will see that as a reason to apply.
   - Say what you are looking for in a cook and how to apply: email harrisoncorner@outlook.com with a short note about kitchen experience.
   - Add the referral reward in one clear line, for example: "Send us our next line cook. If someone you refer is hired and stays [CONFIRM: qualifying period], you get [CONFIRM: referral reward amount] in credit at the cafe."
   - Use two or three photos from the "people behind the pass" content in Tactic 2.
   - When the page is live, share it once as an Instagram and Facebook post, and again as a Story ahead of spring hiring.

### Tips

- Every step in this tactic is a good one to hand to your husband with this page open. None of them need marketing judgment, just following the instructions.
- Do the website work in short sittings, one step at a time. Each step is designed to fit inside an hour, so it never becomes a whole day lost to the website.
- Write the way you talk to guests at the counter. A page that sounds like a real person ranks and reads better than one stuffed with keywords.
- When your hours or winter dates change, update the website, the Google profile and the Instagram bio on the same day. Matching details across all three is one of the things Google checks.
- Check Search Console once a month, not daily. Rankings move slowly, and a new page usually takes several weeks to settle into a position.

## GBP Quick Wins

These are small jobs, each under an hour, that improve how you appear on Google and on your website. None of them depend on anything else in the plan, so do them whenever you have a spare half hour.

### On the Google Business Profile

- **Check your logo and cover photo:** In Edit profile → Photos, make sure the logo is your actual logo and the cover photo is one of your best dishes or the cafe front. The cover is the first image many people see, and a random customer photo may be filling the slot.
- **Fill in the attributes:** In Edit profile, look for the More or Attributes section and tick everything that is true, such as takeout, good for kids, outdoor seating and the parking options that apply. Google uses these to match searches like "family breakfast."
- **Link your Instagram and Facebook:** In Edit profile → Contact, add your Instagram and Facebook links under social profiles. Visitors checking Google can then see the content you are about to start posting.

### On the Website

- **Show your hours on the homepage:** Add "Open Wednesday to Sunday, 9am to 2pm. Closed Monday and Tuesday" near the top of the homepage. The assessment found hours shown nowhere on the site, which is the most common reason for a wasted trip.
- **Make the phone number tappable in the header:** Add your phone number to the site header and link it using Link → Phone number in the Wix Editor, so a visitor on their phone can call in one tap.
- **Put the full name, address and phone in the footer:** "Harrison Corner Cafe, 310 Hot Springs Road, Harrison Hot Springs, BC," followed by your postal code and phone number, written exactly as they appear on Google.
- **Add alt text to the three homepage images:** Click each image, then Settings, and describe it in the "What's in the image? Tell Google" field, for example "Breakfast plate with eggs and toast at Harrison Corner Cafe." The assessment found all three images missing it.
- **Replace any reservation wording with your walk-in policy:** Wherever the site suggests booking or reservations, replace it with "Walk-ins welcome. For large groups and private functions, call or email us," and link to your phone number and harrisoncorner@outlook.com.
