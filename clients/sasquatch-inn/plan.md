## Goal

The Sasquatch Inn has been on the Lougheed Highway for 72 years, and the last three years of renovation have turned it into a place people plan around, not just pull over for. The proof is in the numbers: 1,735 Google reviews at 4.3 stars that Nancy never had to chase, a Facebook following of 6,000, and events like Season of the Wild that sell out every year. The gap is not the business. The gap is that Google shows three businesses with three different sets of hours as one listing, the website is missing the basic search signals the digital assessment flagged, and social posting happens about once a week when the platforms reward a lot more than that. The next ninety days fix those three things in order. Nancy leaves on November 16 for three weeks, so the plan front-loads the heavy setup into the first two weeks and uses scheduled content and a helper to keep everything moving while she is away. By the end, the pub, the hotel, and the liquor store each have their own accurate Google listing, the website answers the questions travellers and AI tools are asking, and more hotel guests are booking direct instead of through Booking.com.

## Strategic Positioning

The Sasquatch Inn sits in a spot with no grocery store or corner store nearby, which makes it a destination by default for campers, anglers, motorcyclists, golfers, paragliders, and ski-hill traffic, and a favourite for locals from Agassiz and Mission. Most of those visitors find the Inn the moment they need it: a Google Maps search from the highway, or a quick question to an AI tool about where to eat or sleep between Mission and Agassiz. That is the moment this plan is built for. The positioning is simple and honest: a family-owned pub, hotel, and liquor store with 72 years behind it, a patio that welcomes dogs, and eagles and salmon close enough to make a day trip worth turning into a night away.

## Tactic 1: Separate Google Business Profiles for the Pub, Hotel, and Liquor Store
Phase: Foundation
Short Label: Google Listings
Subtitle: Split the single Google listing into three accurate ones so the pub, hotel, and liquor store each show the right hours, the right buttons, and the right customers.

### What We Heard From You

Right now one Google listing represents three businesses that open at two different times. The pub opens at 11 AM while the liquor store and hotel open at 9 AM, so customers misread the hours, show up to locked doors, and sometimes confuse what Google says with what the website says. In your questionnaire you asked for one listing that shows all three entities, and on the call we landed on the opposite approach: three separate listings. That is the right call. It is also the one that makes the hotel and liquor store findable, because today nobody searching "liquor store near me" or "hotel Harrison Mills" is likely to see a listing that is mostly about the pub.

### What This Is

Three Google Business Profiles at the same address, each with its own name, category, hours, description, photos, and link to its own page on your website. The existing listing, with its 1,735 reviews, stays with whichever business most of those reviews are about, and the other two are created fresh. Google allows this for departments inside a larger business when each one has a different name, a different primary category, and different hours. You meet all three tests, which is why this works.

### Why It Matters

Google's search and AI results now separate business types much more cleanly than they used to. When someone asks for a liquor store, a pub, or a place to stay, the answer comes from a listing built for exactly that, and a single blended listing loses to specialists in every one of those searches. Three listings also give you three places to post offers and events. The EAGLE special belongs on the hotel listing, happy hour belongs on the pub listing, and Sasquatch merchandise belongs on the liquor store listing, each in front of the people already searching for that thing.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Decide and Create

1. **Audit the existing listing and decide which business keeps it.**
   - Open business.google.com and write down exactly what the current listing says: name, primary category, hours, phone, website link, and review count. Take screenshots of all of it so you can show Google the before and after if you ever need to appeal.
   - Skim the most recent 50 reviews and tally how many are about the food and pub, the rooms, or the liquor store. Reviews cannot be moved or split between listings, so the existing listing stays with the business most of the 1,735 reviews describe. Based on what the assessment found (steak, beef dip, pizza, the patio, staff by name), that is almost certainly the pub.
   - For each of the three businesses, write down the name exactly as it appears on your signage, the opening hours, the phone number, the primary category, and which page on your website it will link to. Anywhere you see square brackets in this plan, such as [INSERT PHONE NUMBER], fill in the real detail before anything gets published.
   - Check that each business has a different primary category and different hours. A pub, a hotel, and a liquor store do, and that difference is what Google looks for when deciding whether separate listings at one address are legitimate.

2. **Reset the existing listing so it describes only one business.**
   - Assuming the existing listing becomes the pub, change the name to match your pub signage (for example "Sasquatch Inn Pub"), set the primary category to Pub, and add secondary categories such as Restaurant or Bar & Grill only if they truly describe how you operate.
   - Remove the hotel and liquor store categories from this listing. They move to the new listings in Step 3.
   - Set the hours to the pub's real hours, starting at 11 AM. If Google offers a Happy Hour option under "More hours," add it so the listing shows it directly.
   - Make all of these edits in one sitting. Google sometimes asks for re-verification after a name or category change, and it is much easier to deal with once than three times.

3. **Create the hotel and liquor store listings and get them verified.**
   - In Business Profile Manager, choose Add business profile, then Add single business. Create one for the hotel (primary category Hotel, with Inn as a secondary category) and one for the liquor store (primary category Liquor Store). Use the same street address for both, 46001 Lougheed Hwy, with the names matching your signage.
   - Verification usually happens by video. Google asks for a continuous recording that shows your signage, the entrance for that business, the inside, and something that proves you operate there. Have whoever is on site film it, because this may land while you are travelling.
   - Keep all three listings under the same owner Google account. If Google flags a listing as a duplicate, do not delete it. Appeal with photos of the separate signage and a note about the different hours and categories, since those are exactly the criteria Google's own guidelines use.
   - Do not give two listings the same primary category. That is the most common reason department listings get filtered out of results.

#### Part B: Complete Each Listing

4. **Set the hours, phone, and website link for each business.**
   - Enter the real hours for each: pub from 11 AM [INSERT CLOSING TIME], liquor store from 9 AM [INSERT CLOSING TIME], hotel front desk from 9 AM [INSERT CLOSING TIME]. Use the same format on all three, the same format you will use on the website.
   - Add the phone number for each business, [INSERT PHONE NUMBER]. If all three share one line, use that number on all three, and if any business has its own line, use it.
   - Point each listing to its own page on the website once those pages exist (Tactic 2, Step 6): pub to the pub page, hotel to the hotel page, liquor store to the liquor store page. Until then, use the homepage and update the link as soon as the pages are live.
   - Add special hours for holidays as they approach. Christmas Day, New Year's Day, and any closure should be set at least a week ahead so nobody drives out to a dark building.

5. **Write the description, attributes, and services for each listing.**
   - Pub, 750 characters or fewer: "The Sasquatch Inn Pub is the family-owned pub on the Lougheed Highway in Harrison Mills, serving home-cooked comfort food, cold drinks, and a pet-friendly patio. Steak, beef dip, pizza, and wraps are regulars' favourites. Open from 11 AM. Part of the Sasquatch Inn, serving travellers between Mission and Agassiz for 72 years."
   - Hotel: "Comfortable rooms in a renovated, family-owned inn on the Lougheed Highway in Harrison Mills, between Mission and Agassiz. Book direct on our website for the best rates. Stay near eagle viewing and salmon spawning in the fall and winter, the golf course next door, and the ski hill 20 to 25 minutes away. Pub and liquor store on site. [CONFIRM PET POLICY]."
   - Liquor store: "The Sasquatch Inn Liquor Store is the closest stop for campers, anglers, golfers, and ski-hill travellers on the Lougheed Highway near Harrison Mills, with no grocery or corner store nearby. Beer, wine, spirits, and Sasquatch merchandise. Open from 9 AM."
   - Under attributes, turn on only what is true for each business. For the pub that likely includes outdoor seating and, if Google offers it for your category, dogs allowed. For the hotel, parking and pet policy. Accurate attributes get you into filtered searches like "pet-friendly patio near me" without any extra work.
   - On the pub listing, add a link to the menu and list the popular dishes by name. On the liquor store listing, use the Products section to add the Sasquatch merchandise with photos and prices.

6. **Upload photos to each listing.**
   - Aim for at least 10 photos per listing before you call it done. Pub: the patio, the bar, and the dishes people mention in reviews. Hotel: the rooms, the exterior, and anything that shows the setting. Liquor store: the shelves, the local and BC picks, and the Sasquatch merchandise.
   - Use real photos from Season of the Wild and from the content capture in Tactic 3. Phone photos in good light are fine. A listing with real, recent photos beats a polished stock-looking one every time.
   - Upload from the business account rather than as a customer, so the photos are labelled as owner-uploaded and show higher up in the photo section.

#### Part C: Posts, Reviews, and the Weekly Routine

7. **Start posting on each listing, including the first offers.**
   - Google Posts appear right on the listing in search and Maps. Post at least weekly on each listing, with content that matches that business: happy hour and specials on the pub, rooms and the winter special on the hotel, new arrivals and merchandise on the liquor store.
   - Use the Offer post type for the EAGLE special on the hotel listing. Include the start and end dates and the code, and link to the hotel page or the promo link from Tactic 2, Step 9.
   - Reuse the same content you are producing for Facebook and Instagram. One photo and two sentences per post is plenty, and the posts take about five minutes each once you have a rhythm.

8. **Build the review system for each listing.**
   - Each listing has its own review link, found in Business Profile Manager under Get more reviews. Copy all three. The 1,735 existing reviews stay on the original listing, and the hotel and liquor store start from zero, which means they need a deliberate ask.
   - For the hotel, add a review request to the post-stay email in Little Hotelier (Direct booking, then Guest communications) with the hotel listing's review link. Pair it with a thank-you and a reminder that booking direct next time gets the best rate.
   - For the pub, put a small QR code on the receipt holder or the table, and for the liquor store, a QR code at the counter. Print a laminated card with "Enjoyed your visit? A quick Google review helps a family business like ours."
   - Never offer a discount, a free drink, or any incentive in exchange for a review. It breaks Google's policies and can get reviews removed.

9. **Reply to reviews and set up a weekly 30-minute routine.**
   - Reply to every review, positive and negative, within 48 hours, using the guest's name when they gave one. Your reviews mention staff members by name, so thank them for that and tell the team. Responses are read by future guests and are indexed by search, so keep the voice warm and specific.
   - For positive reviews, mention the thing they mentioned: "Glad the beef dip hit the spot." For negative ones, acknowledge the specific issue, apologise without arguing, and offer a direct way to follow up.
   - Block 30 minutes every week, same day and time, to check all three listings for new reviews, check that hours are still right, and publish the week's posts. If a helper does the replying while you are away, give them two or three template responses to start from.

### Tips
- The most common reason department listings get flagged is two of them sharing the same primary category. Check that Pub, Hotel, and Liquor Store are the three primary categories before you click publish.
- Do not try to hide or merge the three listings later if one of them gets a slow start. The hotel listing will take longer to build reviews than the pub because there are simply fewer stays than meals, and that is normal.
- The pub listing carries the weight of 1,735 reviews, which is a real asset. Do not rename it dramatically or change the category in a way that makes Google treat it as a new business. Keep the name close to the existing one and match your signage.
- Use the hotel listing as the place where you tell the eagle and salmon story. Day visitors who come for the spawn and the eagles are your best conversion opportunity for overnight stays, and an Offer post with a clear code sits right in front of them when they search for a place to stay.
- If Google asks for re-verification on the original listing, do it right away. A listing in limbo can drop out of search results for days.

## Tactic 2: WordPress SEO Fixes and Direct Booking Pages
Phase: Search and Booking
Short Label: Website & Booking
Subtitle: Fix the issues the assessment found, give each business its own page, and make booking direct the easiest thing a guest can do.

### What We Heard From You

The assessment gave the website a B-minus, and the reasons are almost all fixable in an afternoon. The homepage has no meta description, the title tag is only 13 characters, there are three H1 tags and one of them is an outdated September closure notice, there is no schema markup, no Facebook Pixel, and the Instagram account is not linked from the site. There is also no parking information and no FAQ, even though your guests include RV travellers, motorcyclists, and people with dogs who all have specific questions before they arrive. Your biggest goal in the questionnaire was more direct hotel bookings so you rely less on Booking.com, and you recently learned you can add a discount code on your own booking site. That is exactly the right lever.

### What This Is

Two jobs. The first is the technical cleanup, using the SEO plugin you already have on WordPress: the title tag, the meta description, the H1s, the social sharing tags, and the schema. The second is building the pages that do the work: one page each for the pub, hotel, and liquor store, a parking and accessibility section, an FAQ page, and a clear Book Direct button connected to promo codes in Little Hotelier. These pages are also what each Google listing from Tactic 1 points to, so the two tactics reinforce each other.

### Why It Matters

Your website already dominates branded searches, with a position-one ranking for "sasquatch inn" and roughly 1,338 estimated visits a month. The people you are missing are the ones who do not know your name yet and are searching for "hotel Harrison Mills," "pub near Harrison Hot Springs," or asking an AI tool where to stay for eagle watching. Those searches are answered from pages with clear headings, direct answers, and accurate details. Fixing the architecture and building those pages is how you show up for them. The Book Direct work matters for a more practical reason: every room booked on your own site instead of Booking.com keeps the commission in the business.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Fix the Architecture

1. **Fix the title tag and meta description on the homepage.**
   - Open your SEO plugin (Yoast SEO or Rank Math) on the homepage and find the SEO title field. The current title is just "Sasquatch Inn," which is 13 characters. Replace it with "Sasquatch Inn: Pub, Hotel & Liquor Store | Harrison Mills BC," which is about 60 characters and tells Google and travellers what you are and where.
   - In the meta description field, enter: "Family-owned pub, hotel and liquor store in Harrison Mills. Pet-friendly patio, home-cooked meals, 72 years serving travelers on the Lougheed Highway." That is about 150 characters, which stays under the point where Google cuts descriptions off.
   - Save, then search Google for your own site name a day or two later to check that the new title shows. Google sometimes takes a few days to refresh.

2. **Fix the duplicate H1 tags and remove the outdated closure notice.**
   - A page should have exactly one H1, the main headline. The homepage currently has three, including a notice about a September 14 closure that is long out of date. Open the homepage in the WordPress editor, remove the closure notice entirely, and set the headline "The Sasquatch Inn" as the only H1.
   - Change the other two headings to H2 or H3. In the block editor, click the heading block and use the dropdown in the toolbar to change the level.
   - Use this same rule on every page you build in Part B: one H1, with H2s for main sections and H3s beneath them.

3. **Add the social sharing tags and install the Meta Pixel.**
   - In Yoast, open the Social settings, or in Rank Math open Titles & Meta, then Social, and add a default sharing image and description. These Open Graph tags control how your pages look when someone shares them on Facebook, so a link to the Inn shows a real photo and a real title instead of a blank card.
   - Install the Meta Pixel using Meta's official plugin for WordPress, or by pasting the pixel code into the header with a plugin like WPCode. The pixel records visits from Facebook and Instagram and builds an audience of website visitors you can use for ads later.
   - Check that it is working by opening Meta Events Manager, visiting your own site on your phone, and confirming that a PageView event appears.

4. **Add the Instagram link, a tap-to-call phone number, and the full address to the footer.**
   - Your footer links to Facebook and Twitter but not to Instagram (@sasquatchinnpub). Add it, and add TikTok when the account is live.
   - Make the phone number a tap-to-call link so mobile visitors can call with one tap. In the block editor, select the number, click the link icon, and enter tel: followed by the number with no spaces, for example tel:[INSERT PHONE NUMBER WITH NO SPACES].
   - Add the full street address, 46001 Lougheed Hwy, Harrison Mills, BC, to the footer on every page. Consistent address information across the site and your three Google listings is one of the strongest local search signals you can send.

5. **Add schema markup so Google understands the three businesses.**
   - Schema is a small piece of code that tells search engines what a page is about. The assessment recommends LocalBusiness schema with your address, phone, hours, price range, and review rating.
   - If your SEO plugin has a local business or schema module, use it to add a Hotel schema on the hotel page, a BarOrPub schema on the pub page, and a LiquorStore schema on the liquor store page. If your plugin does not include this, the assessment estimates a developer will do it for about $200 to $300 CAD, which is money well spent.
   - Test each page with Google's Rich Results Test (search.google.com/test/rich-results). It will show whether the markup is valid and flag any errors to fix.

#### Part B: Build the Pages That Do the Work

6. **Build one page for each business.**
   - Create three pages if they do not already exist: a pub page, a hotel page, and a liquor store page. If any already exist, upgrade them using the structure below.
   - Give each page one clear H1, for example "Sasquatch Inn Pub in Harrison Mills, BC," "Hotel Rooms at the Sasquatch Inn, Harrison Mills BC," and "Sasquatch Inn Liquor Store, Harrison Mills BC." Put the first answer in the first sentence: what it is, where it is, and when it opens.
   - Include the hours exactly as they appear on the matching Google listing, the phone number, an embedded map, and three to five real photos. The hotel page leads with the Book Direct button, the pub page with the menu, and the liquor store page with the merchandise.
   - Add a short line on each page linking to the other two, something like "The Sasquatch Inn is a pub, a hotel, and a liquor store at one address." That clears up the confusion customers have between the three and helps search engines connect them as one brand.
   - Point each Google listing to its matching page (Tactic 1, Step 4) as soon as these are published.

7. **Add parking and accessibility information.**
   - The assessment found no parking information on the site, even though your reviews and customer mix point to RVs, trailers, and motorcycles. On the hotel page and in the FAQ, describe what vehicles you can accommodate, whether parking is free, how much space there is for trailers, and whether hotel guests can park overnight. [CONFIRM DETAILS].
   - Add a short accessibility statement covering which rooms, if any, are wheelchair accessible, whether the pub has step-free access, the washroom situation, and your service animal policy.
   - Keep it plain and specific. Visitors planning a trip with a trailer or a mobility need are looking for a clear yes or no, and the businesses that answer first win the booking.

8. **Build an FAQ page that answers real questions in the first sentence.**
   - Create a page called "Frequently Asked Questions." Write each question as an H2 or H3 heading, then answer it directly in the first sentence of the paragraph below. AI tools like ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews extract answers in exactly this format.
   - Start with these questions and open each answer with a direct reply:
     - "What time is check-in and check-out at the Sasquatch Inn?" Check-in is [INSERT TIME] and check-out is [INSERT TIME].
     - "Is the Sasquatch Inn pet friendly?" The pub patio welcomes dogs, and [CONFIRM ROOM PET POLICY AND FEES].
     - "What are the hours for the pub, liquor store, and hotel?" The pub opens at 11 AM, and the liquor store and hotel open at 9 AM [INSERT CLOSING TIMES].
     - "Does the Sasquatch Inn serve breakfast?" [CONFIRM].
     - "Is there Wi-Fi at the Sasquatch Inn?" [CONFIRM].
     - "Where is the Sasquatch Inn?" We are at 46001 Lougheed Hwy in Harrison Mills, between Mission and Agassiz, about an hour and a half from Vancouver.
     - "Is there parking for RVs, trailers, and motorcycles?" [CONFIRM from Step 7].
     - "Can I see eagles near the Sasquatch Inn?" Eagles gather along the river in the fall and winter, and the Inn is minutes away. [ADD BEST MONTHS AND VIEWING SPOTS FROM YOUR LOCAL KNOWLEDGE].
     - "Can I see salmon spawning near Harrison Mills?" [ADD SEASON AND VIEWING SPOTS].
     - "What is there to do near the Sasquatch Inn?" The golf course is right behind the Inn, the ski hill is 20 to 25 minutes away, and Sasquatch Provincial Park is nearby.
     - "Do you sell Sasquatch merchandise at the liquor store?" Yes, [DESCRIBE WHAT YOU SELL AND WHERE TO FIND IT].
   - If your SEO plugin supports FAQ schema, enable it on this page. Then add a link to the FAQ page in the footer and in the main navigation.

#### Part C: Make Direct Booking the Obvious Choice

9. **Add a Book Direct button and set up promo codes in Little Hotelier.**
   - Add a Book Direct or Check Availability button above the fold on the homepage and the hotel page, in a colour that stands apart from the navigation and linking straight to your Little Hotelier booking engine. The assessment flagged this as a simple conversion win.
   - In Little Hotelier, go to Direct booking and open Promotion codes (menu labels shift slightly between versions of the platform). Create a code for the eagle special, using EAGLE as the code, a discount of [INSERT DISCOUNT], and stay dates that cover the eagle season. Create separate codes for any partner you work with, such as a Parkbus code at the 15 percent you have agreed.
   - Little Hotelier lets you build a custom link that has the promo code applied automatically. The format is your booking engine address followed by ?promocode=EAGLE. You will find your booking engine address under Direct booking, then Setup, then Property, then General: [INSERT BOOKING ENGINE URL]. Use these links in Google Offer posts, social bios, captions, and emails so a guest lands on the discount with one tap.
   - Package the winter special so it can be booked online. A queen room dropping from $159 to $139 or $129 with the $20 pub gift card is a good offer, and Little Hotelier lets you attach inclusions and extras to a rate plan.
   - Keep your Booking.com cap of two rooms at a time. That forces the rest of your inventory toward your own site. Check your Booking.com agreement before advertising a publicly lower rate, since a code that only works when guests enter it on your site is generally treated differently from a lower public price, and it is worth confirming how Booking.com sees it.

10. **Track where bookings come from.**
   - Add UTM tags to every link you control so you can see which channel sent each visitor, for example ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=gbp&utm_campaign=hotel for the hotel listing. Google's Campaign URL Builder makes this a copy-and-paste job.
   - Connect Google Search Console to the site if it is not already, so you can see which searches bring visitors to each page. It is free and takes about 20 minutes.
   - Once a month, open Google Analytics and look at the Traffic acquisition report, and open Little Hotelier's reports to see how many direct bookings used each promo code. Compare direct bookings to Booking.com bookings and write the ratio down. That ratio is the number that tells you the plan is working.
   - Ask guests at check-in how they found you or booked. A five-second question gives you information the analytics never will.

### Tips
- Do the technical fixes in a single sitting. Title tag, meta description, H1s, footer link, tap-to-call, and address are about 90 minutes in total, and they are the quickest way to make the site better for both Google and visitors.
- Write the three business pages like you are answering a guest standing at the counter. A first-time visitor wants to know three things: are you open, what do you have, and how do I get there. If the first sentence of the page answers those, you have done it right.
- The FAQ page is where AI tools will pull answers from. When you are unsure about an answer, do not skip the question. Write the true answer, even if it is "no," because a clear no still wins over a missing answer.
- Test the Book Direct button from your phone before you promote it. Open the website, tap the button, enter the EAGLE code, and confirm the discount shows. A broken booking path costs more than having no promotion at all.
- Review the promo codes monthly. A code that is still active after the season ends will keep discounting rooms you could have sold at full price.

## Tactic 3: Social Media Content System with TikTok
Phase: Content and Audience
Short Label: Social & Content
Subtitle: Build five content pillars, a batching routine, and a TikTok presence on top of Facebook and Instagram, so the Inn stays visible even when Nancy is running the business or away.

### What We Heard From You

You post about once a week, mostly on Facebook where you have built a real audience of 6,000 followers, and you do collab posts through Tourism Harrison and the Chamber. Instagram (@sasquatchinnpub) has 1,804 followers and posts less than once a week, with about 0.72 percent engagement, and only 5 of the last 12 posts were Reels. You already use Canva and ChatGPT heavily, which means the tools are not the problem. The gap is a system: what to post, how often, and a way to batch it so it keeps going during a busy week or a three-week trip. The call also surfaced the best opportunity you have, which is the day visitors who come for the salmon spawn and the eagles and could be turned into overnight guests.

### What This Is

A content system built on five pillars, a weekly or twice-weekly batching session where you write captions with ChatGPT, design in Canva, and schedule everything in one go, and an added TikTok presence using the same video you are already making. It also includes a specific series for the eagle and salmon season that sends viewers straight to the direct booking link, and a simple email list for events that sell out.

### Why It Matters

Most posts now reach people who do not follow you, so the goal is volume and consistency across several platforms, not a perfect post on one. Facebook is your strongest platform and should stay the anchor. Adding TikTok costs almost nothing extra because the same video works on Instagram Reels and TikTok, and the 30-plus audience that books overnight stays is on both. 2. **Capture content at Season of the Wild and on the river this week.**

Take your phone and capture 20 or more photos and 5 or more short clips: the room set up, the salmon dinner being plated, guests arriving, the atmosphere. Ask the creator to tag the Inn in their posts so you can share them.

   - Over the next two weeks, film short clips of eagles and the salmon run from public viewing spots, staying a respectful distance from the wildlife. Aim for 10 to 15 clips of 10 to 20 seconds each. This is the raw material for the Stay pillar for the rest of the winter.
   - Do not wait for perfect footage. Phone video in natural light is what performs, and the first ten clips will teach you more than any plan.

3. **Set up the content library and the Canva brand kit.**
   - Create a Google Drive folder called "Sasquatch Inn Content Library" with a sub-folder for each pillar. Upload everything you capture on the day you capture it, and name files with the pillar and date, for example stay-eagles-20261108.mp4.

   - Share both with your helper. A library that only one person can find is a bottleneck waiting to happen.

#### Part B: Batching, Scheduling, and TikTok

4. **Run a weekly batching session with ChatGPT and Canva.**
   - Block 90 minutes once a week, always at the same time. In that session you choose the next week's posts from the library, write the captions, design anything that needs Canva, and schedule it all in Meta Business Suite at business.facebook.com. That tool posts to Facebook and Instagram at the same time and is free.
   - Use a standing prompt in ChatGPT so the voice stays consistent: "You are writing captions for the Sasquatch Inn, a family-owned pub, hotel and liquor store on the Lougheed Highway in Harrison Mills, BC. The voice is friendly, a little cheeky, and local, never corporate. I will give you a photo description and the pillar. Write three caption options under 40 words, each with one clear call to action and no more than three hashtags."
   - Aim for a minimum of four feed posts a week across Facebook and Instagram, with at least two as Reels. Add a Story whenever something is happening, because Stories take about 10 seconds and keep the account active in between.
   - Always review the caption before it goes live. ChatGPT is a fast first draft, and your edits are what make it sound like the Inn.

5. **Launch TikTok and post the same videos everywhere.**
   - Create a TikTok Business account at business.tiktok.com with a handle that matches your Instagram. Use the same profile photo and a bio that matches the Google listing description.
   - Every Reel you make for Instagram also goes to TikTok. Download the video from Canva or your phone before it is uploaded to Instagram, so it does not carry the watermark, and post it to TikTok the same day.
   - Start with one to two videos a week and build from there. TikTok does not need followers to show your video to new people, so a clip of eagles at Harrison Mills can reach people who have never heard of the Inn.
   - Add a link to the hotel page or the EAGLE promo link in the bio, and update the Instagram bio and Facebook About section to use it too.

6. **Schedule content for the three weeks you are away.**
   - Before you leave on November 16, run an extended batching session and schedule enough content for three weeks. For four posts a week, that is about 12 posts for Facebook and Instagram, scheduled in Meta Business Suite with the dates set.
   - Use the Season of the Wild content and the eagle and salmon clips from this week. They are fresh, timely, and the most likely to perform.
   - Leave your helper a one-page note: where the content calendar lives, who answers comments and messages, who posts the Google Posts for each listing, and who to call if something needs a decision. A post that goes out with nobody watching the comments is a missed chance.
   - Check in once during the trip, for about 10 minutes, to confirm that posts are going out and nothing needs a reply.

#### Part C: Turn Viewers into Guests

7. **Run a weekly Stay series for eagle and salmon season.**
   - Make it a recurring post, like a weekly eagle report: one short clip or photo, one line on what is happening on the river, and a call to action to stay the night. Put the EAGLE promo link in the post and in the first comment.
   - Write every Stay post to move a day visitor toward an overnight: "Eagles by day, a warm room and a hot meal by night." Tie in the winter special when it applies, such as the queen room with the $20 pub gift card.
   - Use the same content on the hotel's Google listing as an Offer post (Tactic 1, Step 7) so it reaches people searching Google as well as people scrolling social.
   - Track every promo redemption in Little Hotelier so you can see which posts brought bookings.

8. **Use the partners you already have.**
   - Tourism Harrison and the Chamber already do collab posts with you, so send them the content, not just a request. A short email with two or three clips, a caption, and the tag you want is easy to say yes to.
   - Use Instagram's Collab feature on posts where a partner is involved, so the post appears on both accounts and doubles the reach.
   - For the Parkbus trip, make a short post for their audience and your own that shows the overnight and the food, and use the Parkbus promo code at the 15 percent you agreed.
   - Hold the ski and stay package content until the mountain contact is reachable, then add it to the Out and About pillar.

9. **Start a simple events email list.**
   - Your events sell out and bring people from Agassiz and Mission, which is exactly what an email list is for. Set up a free MailerLite account and create one sign-up form.
   - Put a QR code to the form on the pub tables, at the liquor store counter, and at the hotel desk, and link it from the website footer. Canada's anti-spam law requires clear consent, so use a sign-up form with a clear opt-in rather than collecting names on a card.
   - Send one email a month with upcoming events, the winter special, and one story. The tulip festival package is a good first reason to write.
   - Frame events around food, the experience, and staying the night. Given the rural location and no public transit, a room after the tasting is both the responsible option and an upsell.

### Tips
- Facebook is your strongest platform, so do not neglect it while building TikTok. Treat TikTok as a free add-on to content you are already producing, not a second job.
- The best-performing post in the assessment hit 2.88 percent engagement, which shows your audience responds when the content is right. When a post beats your average, write down the pillar, the format, and the caption style, and make five more like it.
- Make a habit of replying to every comment within a few hours of posting. It tells the algorithm the post is worth showing to more people and it makes your followers feel seen.
- Winter is the quiet season for social, so keep the volume steady and lean on the Stay pillar. The assessment suggests more posting from March to October, but the eagle season is your winter opportunity, and it deserves the effort.
- Do not boost every post. When you do spend on promotion, boost the Stay posts with the EAGLE link first, since those are the ones tied to a booking you can measure.
