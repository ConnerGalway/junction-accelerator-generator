“
Your 90-Day Goal

The 2026 Harrison Festival of the Arts was one of the best-selling in its forty-plus years, and when we asked what drove it, the honest answer was that you did not change much. The hall lineup had a few more recognizable names, more people stayed close to home, and the poster was strong. Email is the one channel you know works, because ticket sales jump the moment a send goes out. Everything else is a guess. Rogers tells you the campaign did well, Black Press will tell you the same, and right now there is no way to check either of them against a single ticket sale. The plan runs for twelve weeks, from January 4 to March 28, 2027, and it is shaped by one fact: nothing can be sold in earnest until the lineup is announced in mid-April. Branding arrives at the start of April, print deadlines hit at the same time, and from then until July you are running marketing alongside artists, volunteers, artisans, signage and production. So the work of the next ninety days is to get everything in place before that crunch. By the end of March you should be able to see which channel sold every ticket, have an audience on Facebook and Instagram that has been hearing from the festival every week since January, have a larger email list that opens your emails because they are not always asking for money, and have Google reviews arriving again after an eighteen-month gap. The order matters. Weeks 1 to 3 connect Google Analytics and the Meta pixel to your Ecwid store, because every ad and every email after that depends on being able to see what sold. Weeks 2 to 12 build a social rhythm of two posts a week that takes about three hours a fortnight. Weeks 3 to 12 grow the email list with a sign-up offer that fits your calendar, "hear the lineup before anyone else," and add a review request after every Season of Performing Arts show. Meta ads start in Week 6 as a low-cost warm-up and finish with a retargeting campaign tested on a Season show and built in draft, ready to switch on the day the lineup goes public. What this produces, stated honestly: very few festival tickets inside the ninety days, and that is by design. The Season shows between January and March are where the tracking, the review emails and the retargeting get tested on real sales at low stakes. The return comes from mid-April to July. The measure of success for this plan is readiness: purchase tracking live with three months of channel data behind it, 150 new subscribers who have not yet bought a ticket, Google reviews up from 13 to 25 or more, Instagram engagement up from 0.49 percent to around 1.5 percent, and a retargeting audience of more than 1,000 people waiting for the announcement.

Strategic Positioning

The hardest thing about marketing Harrison is the thing that makes it good. Every day of the festival is different: a blues show one night, an African group the next, a Celtic band after that. The ten days are spread out rather than packed into a weekend, so, as you put it, people come for very specific reasons at specific times. When you aim an ad at one audience, you risk losing the one you wanted for a different day. That is why picking "the right demographic" for each show has felt impossible. Your two closest comparisons show the choice clearly. Mission Folk has a defined folk and roots crowd and markets to it. The Langley festival the weekend after yours has gone toward corporate sponsors and activations. Harrison is neither, and you have no reason to become either. It is the open, inclusive world roots festival by the lake, run by a nonprofit that has been doing it for more than four decades. It is also two festivals in one. You can spend a day on the beach, walk the artisan market and bring the kids to Children's Day for $6 without buying a hall ticket, or you can spend about $30 on a night in Memorial Hall. That range is the answer to the younger audience you want to reach. Families and people under 40 are not being priced out. They simply do not know the free and low-cost half of the festival exists, which is the same thing your audience tells you after the fact: "I had no idea this was here." So the positioning leads with the feeling and lets the lineup be the payoff. Your own experience already proved this. The near-sellout show post reached about 200 people and the video of leaves falling off a tree reached about 3,000. Your audience responds to the place and the moment first. From January to March the festival should be telling that story of the lake, the beach stage, the market and the hall, so that by mid-April the lineup lands on people who already want to come and are waiting to find out who is playing. > **Your Strategic Positioning** > > The Harrison Festival of the Arts is ten days of world roots music by the lake, where you can spend a whole day on the beach without spending a dime or a night in Memorial Hall for about $30. Every day sounds different, and everyone is welcome. Come for the moment you will remember, then find out who is playing.

Tell the Festival's Story Before the Lineup Exists

A clip-and-carousel routine that builds the festival's atmosphere from January to March, so the April lineup announcement reaches people who are already paying attention.

What This Is

A fixed weekly pattern of two posts, one Reel and one carousel, built from material you already have and from short clips you collect on your phone at Season shows. You make the Reels in Instagram's free Edits app by stitching three-second clips together, and the carousels from archive photos with a line of text on each slide. You batch four posts every second Monday and schedule them in Meta Business Suite so they publish to Facebook and Instagram without you. By the end of March you will also have an announcement kit ready, so the April posts are a matter of dropping in the new branding and artist names.

What This Breaks Into
Build the Raw Material
Gather 60-100 archive photos, type up 20-30 member quotes, and cut the promo video into short clips ready for Reels.
Set the Posting Rhythm
Two posts a week: Tuesday Reel and Friday carousel, batched every second Monday and scheduled in Meta Business Suite.
April Announcement Kit
Build the Reel template, carousel layout and captions for the lineup announcement, so April posts are ready in minutes.
What We Heard From You

You told us you know the festival needs more storytelling and more fun posts, and that you "just don't have the creativity and the brainpower" to produce them while managing everything else. You said your picture of success is a social campaign that is close to automatic and that you don't have to think about. You remembered the post about a near-sellout show that reached about 200 people, and the video of leaves falling the next day that reached about 3,000. You also told us the students on Canada Summer Jobs made the behind-the-scenes posts you liked, that your photography team gives you strong stills but video shot under the hall's LED lighting looks grungy, that your big promo video tells the festival's story well without being about any one artist, and that you have a pile of member quotes from the family membership forms that you have never used.

Why It Matters

Instagram engagement is 0.49 percent, which means about nine of your 1,825 followers interact with a typical post, and the account posts 1.3 times a week, with only a quarter of posts as Reels. The platforms now show photos mostly to people who already follow you, while video is how you reach new people. In Junction's own accounts, roughly 80 to 85 percent of the people who watch a Reel are not followers. The festival has no artists to announce until April, which is exactly why this window matters: posts about the atmosphere are the only thing you can publish now, and they are also the type of post your audience has already shown it prefers. If you skip this, the lineup announcement goes out to a quiet account and the Meta ads in Tactic 2 have no proven posts to run.

Time Commitment
40 min to fix analytics, this week
    Build the Raw Material
  1. 1
    Pull the archive into one place.
    • Create one folder on your phone or in Google Drive called "Festival 2027 Content," with three subfolders: Photos, Clips and Quotes.
    • Copy in the best 60 to 100 photos from the last two festivals from your photography team. Choose by moment rather than by artist: kids at Children's Day, the beach stage crowd, the artisan market, people dancing in Memorial Hall, volunteers, the lake.
    • Sort the photos into four groups that will become carousel series: Children's Day, the Beach Stage, the Artisan Market and a Night in the Hall.
    • Leave out anything where a single artist is the whole point of the photo. You will want those in April, and they date the post to a specific year.
  2. 2
    Turn the membership forms into a quote bank.
    • Go through the family membership forms and type every comment that answers "why are you a member" or "what is your favourite part of the festival" into one document in the Quotes folder.
    • Aim for 20 to 30 quotes. Note the first name and town only where the member wrote them on the form, for example "Jim, Langley."
    • Put a star next to any quote that mentions family, kids, the beach, the lake or a specific memory. Those are the ones that speak to the younger audience you want.
    • If you write a caption in the voice of a family on the beach, that is fine as a caption. Just never put a real person's name on a line they did not say.
  3. 3
    Cut the promo video into a clip library.
    • Your promo video already has the drone shots, the dancing crowd and the artisans waving. Instead of posting it once, cut it into 8 to 12 separate clips of 2 to 5 seconds each.
    • In Edits, open the video, tap the clip on the timeline and use Split to cut it into pieces, then save each one to the Clips folder.
    • Name each clip by what is in it ("drone lake," "crowd dancing," "market wave") so you can find them during batching.
    • These clips are the base of most Reels between January and March, mixed with new clips from Step 7.
  4. 4
    Install Edits and make one practice Reel.
    • Download Edits, Instagram's free video editing app, from the App Store or Google Play and sign in with the festival's Instagram account.
    • Tap the + button, select four or five clips from your camera roll, and let the app line them up. Trim each to two or three seconds by dragging the clip edges.
    • Add one line of text on screen, such as "Somewhere in BC, in July," and choose a track from the app's audio library. Using Edits' own library avoids music rights problems.
    • Save it as a draft without posting. The aim of this step is to learn the app in 20 minutes so batching is quick from now on.
    5. Set a Rhythm That Survives a Busy Week
  5. 5
    Fix the posting pattern at two posts a week.
    • Tuesday is a Reel and Friday is a carousel. The assessment's target of four posts a week belongs to April to July. For January to March, two that actually go out beat four that don't.
    • Rotate the Reels through a series called Little Moments: four to six clips of two to three seconds each, one line of text, and music from Edits. Examples: the lake in winter, the hall lights coming up before a Season show, an artisan's hands, last summer's beach crowd.
    • Rotate the carousels through two series. The first is A Day at the Festival, with six to eight archive photos and a short line of text on each slide telling one day, such as a Children's Day morning or a beach stage afternoon. The second is Why We Come Back, with a member quote on a photo.
    • Once a month, swap the Friday carousel for a teaser: "The 2027 lineup drops in April. Hear it first," linking to the email sign-up in Tactic 3.
  6. 6
    Batch and schedule every second Monday.
    • Block 90 minutes every second Monday. In that time, make two Reels in Edits and two carousels, then schedule all four.
    • To schedule, go to business.facebook.com, open Planner from the left menu, and choose Create post (for carousels) or Create reel. Tick both the Facebook Page and the Instagram account so one post publishes to both.
    • Write the caption in two or three short lines. Mention Harrison Hot Springs by name at least once a week, since many people scrolling past do not know where the festival is.
    • If a batching Monday gets away from you, schedule one Reel and one carousel at minimum. A short week is fine, but a gap of three weeks is what the algorithm punishes.
  7. 7
    Start the clip habit at every Season show.
    • At each Season of Performing Arts show, spend five minutes shooting 10 to 15 clips of three to five seconds each on your phone. Hold the phone vertically.
    • Shoot the things that look good in any light: the lobby filling up, the bar, people laughing at intermission, chairs being set out, the marquee or sign outside, a sound check in daylight, the lake on your way in.
    • Skip filming the performance under the LED lighting, since you already know that footage looks grungy. A wide shot from the back for two seconds, with the crowd silhouetted, is the exception worth trying.
    • Move the clips into the Clips folder the next morning. You will use only one in five, and that is normal.
    8. Learn From It, Then Hand Off to April
  8. 8
    Check what worked once a month.
    • On the last Friday of each month, open Meta Business Suite, go to Insights, then Content, and sort the month's posts by Reach and then by Shares.
    • Note the top two posts and whether most of their reach came from non-followers. In the Instagram app, tap View insights under any post to see the split between followers and non-followers.
    • Write one line in your content folder: what the top post was about and what format it was. After three months this list tells you what your audience actually responds to.
    • The top two posts each month become the warm-up ads in Tactic 2, Step 11.
  9. 9
    Build the April announcement kit.
    • In the last two weeks of March, set up everything the announcement needs, so that when branding arrives in early April the only work is dropping it in.
    • In Edits, build a Reel template with placeholder slots for three to five artist photos and the announcement date, using your best-performing Little Moments clip as the opener.
    • Draft an artist carousel layout with one artist per slide and a line on what kind of night it will be ("A Celtic night in Memorial Hall"), so each show speaks to its own crowd without the whole festival being narrowed to one audience.
    • Write the captions for the announcement post, the early-access email teaser and the first two follow-up posts now, while you have the time.

Tips
  • Shoot more than feels sensible. Nearly all of what you collect will never be used, and that is the system working. You are building a pile to choose from, not producing finished videos.
  • Never post a hall performance clip that you would not be proud to show an artist. The lobby, the lake and the setup are always safe, and they carry the feeling just as well.
  • Post the same Reel to Facebook and Instagram through Planner. Your older audience is more likely on Facebook, and it costs you nothing extra.
  • The falling leaves video is your proof. When you are unsure whether a post is good enough, ask whether it shows a moment someone would want to be in. If it does, post it.
  • If a student or volunteer helps out before April, give them this pattern and the content folder rather than a general instruction to post more. They will not know the festival, but the folder will.

Measure Every Ticket, Then Retarget the People Who Already Care

Connect Google Analytics and the Meta pixel to your Ecwid store so every sale has a source, then run a warm-up campaign and a retargeting campaign that is ready to sell the moment the lineup goes public.

What This Is

Two connected pieces of work. The first is measurement: connecting Google Analytics 4 and the Meta pixel to Ecwid so that every completed ticket purchase is recorded with its value and its source, and tagging every link you control so that email, Instagram, Black Press and Rogers each show up separately in the reports. The second is the advertising structure Conner described on the call: a warm-up campaign that runs your best organic posts to a broad Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley audience with no hard sell, and a retargeting campaign aimed only at people who have visited the site, watched your videos or engaged with your accounts, with a direct message to buy. The retargeting campaign gets tested on a Season show in March and is then built in draft for the April announcement.

What This Breaks Into
Make Every Ticket Traceable
Connect GA4 and the Meta pixel to Ecwid, tag every link with UTM parameters, and run a test purchase to confirm it works.
Run a Warm-Up Campaign
Boost your top organic posts to a broad Lower Mainland audience with no hard sell, building a retargeting audience as you go.
Retargeting Campaign
Test retargeting on a Season show, then build the April lineup campaign in draft, ready to switch on the day the lineup goes public.
What We Heard From You

You told us you have limited experience with Google Analytics and do not know how to track who is buying tickets or which channel they came from, and that figuring this out would be one of the most useful things you could get from the Accelerator. You sell through Ecwid and process tickets yourself. You thought a Meta pixel should already be on the site, because Rogers asked you to install one for their campaigns, but you were not sure it was active, and our tracker did not find one. On ads, you have boosted posts that did well, run separate ads for particular shows, and found that broad targeting got more results than narrow targeting. You already buy most ads through Meta Business Manager on desktop. You also run Google Ad Grants as a charity, and you are moving some of your Black Press budget from print to digital.

Why It Matters

The good news from the call is that your checkout stays on harrisonfestival.com, which makes Harrison one of the easier festivals to track properly. Ecwid has built-in settings for both Google Analytics and the Meta pixel, so you can see actual completed purchases and revenue, not just checkout clicks. Without this, you will go into April the same way you went into every other year, unable to say whether Facebook, email, the Black Press buy or the Rogers campaign sold the tickets. The retargeting audience also takes time to build. If you start building it in April, it will be small when you most need it. If you start in February, it will be in the thousands by the announcement.

Time Commitment
3 hrs content & social, per week
    Make Every Ticket Traceable
  1. 1
    Check what is already installed.
    • Log in to your Ecwid admin and go to Settings → General → Tracking & Analytics. Write down whatever is in the Google Analytics ID field and the Facebook (Meta) Pixel ID field, even if they are empty.
    • Go to analytics.google.com and check whether a Google Analytics 4 property already exists for harrisonfestival.com. If you are not sure whose Google account set it up, check the account you use for Google Ad Grants first.
    • Install the free Meta Pixel Helper and Google Tag Assistant extensions in Chrome, then visit your homepage and your ticket page. Each extension shows which tags are firing on the page.
    • Send Junction a screenshot of what you find. It decides whether Steps 2 and 8 are a setup or a repair.
  2. 2
    Connect Google Analytics 4 to Ecwid.
    • If there is no GA4 property, create one at analytics.google.com: Admin → Create → Property, name it "Harrison Festival," set the time zone to Pacific and the currency to Canadian dollars, then create a Web data stream for harrisonfestival.com.
    • In GA4, go to Admin → Data collection and modification → Data streams, click the harrisonfestival.com stream and copy the Measurement ID (it starts with G-).
    • In Ecwid, go to Settings → General → Tracking & Analytics, paste the ID into the Google Analytics ID field and save.
    • Back in the GA4 data stream, scroll down to Measurement Protocol API secrets, click Create, name it "Ecwid," and copy the secret value. Paste it into the Measurement Protocol API key field that now appears in Ecwid's Tracking & Analytics page and save. This sends purchases to Google directly from Ecwid's servers, so ad blockers cannot hide them.
    • Once connected, Ecwid sends ticket views, add to cart, begin checkout and completed purchases to GA4, including the order value. Purchase is marked as a key event automatically.
  3. 3
    Make sure the rest of the website is tracked too.
    • Ecwid's connection tracks the store pages. Your homepage, FAQ, festival information and artist pages also need the GA4 tag, or GA4 will miss where people came in.
    • Add the same Measurement ID to the website itself through its platform settings or plugin. [CONFIRM: website platform, for example WordPress on Bluehost, and who can edit it]
    • After adding it, use Google Tag Assistant to check that each page has exactly one GA4 tag. Two tags on the same page double-count visits, which is a common problem when both a website plugin and Ecwid add one.
  4. 4
    Run a test purchase.
    • Buy the cheapest available Season show ticket yourself, on your phone, using a normal payment method. Then refund it in Ecwid.
    • In GA4, open Reports → Realtime while you buy. You should see a begin_checkout event and then a purchase event appear within a minute or two.
    • In GA4, go to Admin → Data display → Key events and confirm purchase is listed and switched on.
    • Note the date. From this day on, every ticket sale has a source attached. Purchase data starts appearing in standard reports after about 24 hours.
  5. 5
    Tag every link you control.
    • Use Google's free Campaign URL Builder to create tracked versions of your ticket page link. Each one records where the click came from.
    • Use one naming pattern throughout: source is the channel (email, instagram, facebook, blackpress, rogers), medium is the type (email, social, display, audio), and campaign is the season or show ("season-feb-2027," "festival-2027").
    • Give the Black Press and Rogers reps their own tagged links and ask them to use those links in every digital ad they run for you. This is what lets you check their claims against your own sales.
    • Put a tagged link in your Instagram and Facebook bios and in Linktree. If your email platform has a setting to add UTM tags automatically, switch it on; otherwise use tagged links in each email.
  6. 6
    Connect GA4 to your Google Ad Grants account.
    • In GA4, go to Admin → Product links → Google Ads links → Link, and choose your Ad Grants account.
    • In Google Ads, go to Goals → Conversions → Summary, click + New conversion action, choose Import, then Google Analytics 4 properties, then Web, and select purchase.
    • Ad Grants accounts must have valid conversion tracking and record at least one conversion a month, so this protects the grant as well as measuring it.
    • Before April, open Keywords and add the kinds of searches you described on the call, such as "things to do with kids in the Fraser Valley," "live music Fraser Valley" and "Harrison Hot Springs events July." Ad Grants does not allow most single-word keywords and requires the account to keep a 5 percent click-through rate, so pause any keyword that drags it below that.
  7. 7

Grow the List and Turn Ticket Buyers into Reviewers

Give people a reason to join the list before April, send one story email a month alongside the sales emails, and ask every Season show audience for a Google review.

What This Is

Three small changes to email that work together. First, a sign-up offer that suits your calendar exactly: subscribers hear the 2027 lineup before it is announced publicly, which costs you nothing and is the most valuable thing you can offer anyone between January and April. Second, one story email a month, made from the same material as that month's social posts, so the list gets used to opening your emails without being asked to buy. Third, a short thank-you email after every Season show with a direct link to leave a Google review, plus a QR card at the exit.

What This Breaks Into
Lineup Sign-Up Offer
Launch "Hear the lineup first" as the sign-up incentive, with forms on the website, bios and at Season show venues.
One Story Email a Month
Send one non-sales email monthly using the same material as that month's social posts, so the list opens emails without expecting a pitch.
Post-Show Review Emails
Send a thank-you email within 48 hours of every Season show with a direct link to leave a Google review.
Reply & Reuse Reviews
Reply to every review within a week and copy the best lines back into carousels and retargeting creative.
What We Heard From You

You told us email is your best channel by a distance: when people buy online, Ecwid captures their address, you keep reaching them, and they come back because the shows are good. You also told us that almost nobody signs up any other way, and that the contests you ran on social media brought in only a handful of sign-ups and did not seem worth it. Every email you send is about a show, two per show, with a note about the next one at the bottom, and you said you would be open to changing that pattern. On reviews, you said the assessment's point about Google reviews was something you had not been thinking about and liked straight away, and that you want reviews for the Season of Performing Arts as well as the festival. You also told us the feedback survey you sent a few years ago mostly brought back criticism.

Why It Matters

On the call, Conner put a rough value on a new subscriber of $10 to $15, based on a $30 average ticket and the share of subscribers who buy and then come back. At that rate, 150 new sign-ups before April is worth $1,500 to $2,250 in future ticket sales, from a form and an offer that cost nothing. The story emails matter for a practical reason: lists that only ever receive sales emails open less over time, and the emails start landing in promotions tabs and spam folders. On reviews, the festival has 13 Google reviews at 4.4 stars and none in eighteen months. Google weighs recent reviews heavily when it decides which business to show for searches like "things to do in Harrison Hot Springs," and a profile with no recent reviews looks inactive to visitors as well. The Season shows give you a reason to ask every month instead of once a year, and the post-show email is a request for praise rather than a survey that invites complaints.

    • Save it as a template. After the festival in July, the same template goes to every festival ticket buyer, which is when the largest batch of reviews will come in.

    Ask in the room.

    • Add one line to the end-of-show thank-you from the stage: "If you enjoyed tonight, a Google review helps more people find us."

    • Keep the signs and the volunteer line for the festival in July, as the assessment recommended.
  9. 9
    Reply to every new review within a week.
    • Turn on review notifications in your Business Profile so new reviews reach your email.
    • Reply to every review by name, mention something specific they said, and invite them back to the next show or the festival.
    • For any critical review, thank the person, acknowledge the issue in one sentence, say what has changed if something has, and offer an email address to continue the conversation. Never argue in public.
    • Google treats replies as a sign the business is active, and future visitors read them.
  10. 10
    Put the best review lines back to work.
    • Each month, copy the best lines from new reviews into the Quotes folder alongside the member quotes.
    • Use them in the Why We Come Back carousels in Tactic 1 and in the retargeting creative in Tactic 2, credited as "Google review" with the reviewer's first name.
    • When the festival website is next updated, swap any old testimonials for recent Google reviews.

So the work of the next ninety days is to get everything in place before that crunch. By the end of March you should be able to see which channel sold every ticket, have an audience on Facebook and Instagram that has been hearing from the festival every week since January, have a larger email list that opens your emails because they are not always asking for money, and have Google reviews arriving again after an eighteen-month gap. The order matters. Weeks 1 to 3 connect Google Analytics and the Meta pixel to your Ecwid store, because every ad and every email after that depends on being able to see what sold. Weeks 2 to 12 build a social rhythm of two posts a week that takes about three hours a fortnight. Weeks 3 to 12 grow the email list with a sign-up offer that fits your calendar, "hear the lineup before anyone else," and add a review request after every Season of Performing Arts show. Meta ads start in Week 6 as a low-cost warm-up and finish with a retargeting campaign tested on a Season show and built in draft, ready to switch on the day the lineup goes public. What this produces, stated honestly: very few festival tickets inside the ninety days, and that is by design. The Season shows between January and March are where the tracking, the review emails and the retargeting get tested on real sales at low stakes. The return comes from mid-April to July. The measure of success for this plan is readiness: purchase tracking live with three months of channel data behind it, 150 new subscribers who have not yet bought a ticket, Google reviews up from 13 to 25 or more, Instagram engagement up from 0.49 percent to around 1.5 percent, and a retargeting audience of more than 1,000 people waiting for the announcement.
