# Harrison Beach Hotel: Accelerator Implementation Plan

## Meta
Client Slug: harrison-beach-hotel
Has GBP Quick Wins: yes

## Goal

Harrison Beach Hotel is already ahead of most of the properties we work with. The schema is in place, the FAQ page is live, the landing pages have been refreshed, and ChatGPT puts the hotel in its top three places to stay in Harrison and at number one for meetings. The assessment scored the booking experience at 99 out of 100 and the reputation at 4.4 stars across 895 Google reviews. What the numbers also show is where the money is leaking: OTA bookings crept from a historic 20 percent to about 30 percent in August and September, and a social following of 23,206 is producing 0.34 percent engagement and almost no comments. The next ninety days are about closing both gaps. The first is a topic cluster content hub that makes Harrison Beach Hotel the most specific, most citable answer to the questions travellers put to AI tools, ahead of Expedia and Booking.com. The second is a high-volume social system, built on a bank of pillar content that your coordinator, Nicole, and Megan can keep full, that turns an audience you already have into direct bookings. The third is the conversion layer that connects the two: direct-booking perks that are impossible to miss, packages with your partners, midweek and shoulder season campaigns, and a proper push on the meetings and events business. The target is simple to state and measurable every month: direct bookings back toward 80 percent, fewer empty midweek nights, and more meeting enquiries.

## Strategic Positioning

Harrison Hot Springs is a destination people already want to visit, which means the competition for a booking is decided by who gives the clearest, most trustworthy answer at the moment someone is planning. OTAs win that moment by default because they are everywhere, but they cannot tell a traveller that the team remembers loyal guests by name, that the rooms have just been renovated, that the dog can come, or that a group of 30 can meet steps from the lake. Harrison Beach Hotel can say all of that, and AI search tools are now looking for exactly that kind of specific, first-hand answer. The positioning is a renovated lakefront hotel run by a team guests rave about, with direct-booking benefits that the OTAs cannot match: flexible cancellation, a best rate guarantee, and a stay planned around you.

## Tactic 1: FAQ Topic Clusters and AI Search Visibility
Phase: Search and AI Visibility
Short Label: AI Visibility & FAQ
Subtitle: Build five question-led topic clusters on WordPress so Harrison Beach Hotel becomes the most specific answer AI tools and search engines can find, then keep that visibility accurate and measured.

### What We Heard From You

You are already doing the hard parts of GEO: schema markup is implemented, an FAQ page went live this year, and the landing pages are fresh. The result is visible, with ChatGPT listing the hotel in its top three places to stay and ranking it first for the best place to hold a meeting in Harrison. The concern is the OTAs. Booking.com and Expedia are a double-edged sword in AI search, they send traffic but they also show up ahead of the hotel, and you flagged that losing visibility could push commission costs up in 2027. There is also a specific error to fix: ChatGPT is pulling a photo from harrisonresort.com under the Harrison Beach Hotel listing.

### What This Is

A content hub built from five topic clusters, each with one pillar page and five question posts, published as a blog section on WordPress and linked from a new item in the main navigation. Every post is a real question a guest asks, with a direct answer in the first sentence and details only the hotel can provide. The tactic also covers the entity housekeeping that makes AI answers accurate, including the wrong photo, and a simple monthly test that shows whether the hotel is being recommended and cited.

### Why It Matters

AI tools do not rank pages, they assemble answers, and they favour sources that answer a specific question completely. A traveller who asks "where can I stay in Harrison with a dog" or "where can I hold a 25-person meeting near Vancouver" is having a conversation, and the property that has already published the clearest answer gets named. This is where Harrison Beach Hotel can beat the OTAs: they have breadth, you have first-hand detail. It is also the foundation for the rest of this plan, because every post written here becomes social content in Tactic 2 and every cluster feeds the direct booking pages in Tactic 3.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Plan the Clusters

1. **Choose the five clusters and map the questions behind each one.**
   - The call identified five clusters built around the adventures and experiences the hotel offers. Based on your guest mix and goals, these five cover the ground:

   | Cluster | Who It Serves | Example Questions |
   |---------|---------------|-------------------|
   | Winter and Shoulder Season in Harrison | Visitors from Saskatchewan and Manitoba, couples, ski and lights visitors | "What is there to do in Harrison Hot Springs in winter?" "Can I stay at Harrison Beach Hotel and ski at Sasquatch Mountain Resort?" "What is Lights on the Lake?" |
   | Pet-Friendly Stays | Lower Mainland travellers with dogs | "Is Harrison Beach Hotel pet friendly?" "What dog-friendly beaches and trails are near Harrison Hot Springs?" "What is the pet fee?" |
   | Families and Multigenerational Getaways | Families, grandparents, larger groups | "What is the best hotel in Harrison Hot Springs for families?" "Do the rooms have kitchenettes?" "What does the family adventure package include?" |
   | Adventures and Experiences | Weekend and 2-3 night visitors planning itineraries | "What are the best things to do in Harrison Hot Springs in summer?" "How far is Sasquatch Provincial Park from the hotel?" "How do I spend a weekend in Harrison?" |
   | Meetings and Small Gatherings | Corporate planners, small receptions | "Where can I hold a meeting in Harrison Hot Springs?" "How many people fit in the meeting space?" "Can I host a small reception at the hotel?" |

   - For each cluster, collect 15 to 20 candidate questions from four sources: your last 100 Google reviews, the questions Megan and the front desk hear most, People Also Ask results and AnswerThePublic, and ChatGPT and Perplexity themselves (ask "what do people ask about [topic] in Harrison Hot Springs?").
   - Pick the five questions per cluster that are most specific and most likely to lead to a booking or an enquiry. Specific beats broad. "Is Harrison Beach Hotel pet friendly?" is a better target than "pet-friendly travel."
   - Save the full list in a Google Sheet with columns for cluster, question, status, publish date, and the person responsible. The remaining questions become the next 90 days.

2. **Build the content calendar for the next three to four months.**
   - Thirty pieces cover the first cycle: five pillar pages and 25 question posts, published at about three a week from Week 3 through Week 12. Schedule the winter cluster first because it is the most timely, then pets, families, adventures, and meetings.
   - Publish each cluster over a two-week window, with the question posts first and the pillar page last, so the pillar can link to posts that already exist. In each two-week window, that is three posts in the first week and two posts plus the pillar in the second.
   - Assign one writer per cluster, and assign one person on the hotel side to confirm facts. Prices, policies, distances, and hours change, and a wrong answer in an AI result is worse than no answer.

#### Part B: Build and Publish

3. **Create the blog section and add it to the main navigation.**
   - On WordPress, create a blog section with one category per cluster. The assessment and the call both point to a blog format, because it is easy for AI bots to crawl and easy for your team to publish into.
   - Add a new item to the main navigation, something like "Explore Harrison," and consider replacing the existing Activities link with it. If you replace Activities, set up 301 redirects from the old page to the new section so you keep the search equity the page has earned.
   - Link the navigation item to a hub page that lists the five clusters with a one-line description of each, then link each cluster to its pillar page.

4. **Write the five pillar pages.**
   - A pillar page is the central page for a cluster: 600 to 900 words, a single H1, a direct overview in the first paragraph, and one section per question that links to the full post. Add a Book Direct call to action with a UTM-tagged link near the top and the bottom.
   - Use the same facts-first approach as your existing FAQ page, with schema that matches what you have already implemented. Your existing Hotel and LodgingBusiness schema stays as is, and the pillar and question pages add Article and FAQPage markup.
   - Link each pillar to the other four, so the hub reads as one connected body of work. Internal linking is how the crawlers understand the cluster as a single authority.

5. **Write and publish the question posts.**
   - Each post is 250 to 500 words. The H1 is the question. The first sentence is a complete, direct answer, with the specific details after it. Add one real photo, a link to the pillar page and two sibling posts, and a Book Direct link with a UTM tag.
   - A pet-friendly example opens like this: "Yes, Harrison Beach Hotel welcomes dogs in [CONFIRM WHICH ROOMS], with a pet fee of [CONFIRM FEE] per stay and [CONFIRM ANY SIZE OR NUMBER LIMITS]." Then give the details a guest actually wants: where to walk the dog, what to bring, and what the team provides.
   - Use Claude to draft, but give it the facts. A reliable prompt: "Write a 300-word answer for the Harrison Beach Hotel blog to the question below. The first sentence must answer the question directly. Use only the facts I provide, and do not invent policies, prices, or distances. Keep the tone warm, specific, and unfussy. Facts: [PASTE]." Then have the team member who knows the topic check every claim before publishing.
   - Add one detail only your team would know to every post, such as the room that gets the best sunset or what Megan tells dog owners at check-in. That is what separates your answer from an OTA summary.

6. **Turn every post into social content the day it publishes.**
   - Each question post becomes at least three pieces: a Reel that answers the question in 15 to 30 seconds, a carousel with the key points, and a Google Business Profile post linking back to the page. This is the bridge to Tactic 2, and it means the content hub and the social calendar are fed by the same work.
   - Capture the Reel footage when you capture the post photos. One shoot, several outputs.
   - Log the social post links in the content calendar so you can see which written answers earn the most engagement. Those are the questions to expand next.

#### Part C: Entity Accuracy and Monitoring

7. **Fix the wrong photo and make the hotel's details consistent across every source AI tools read.**
   - Start with the photo. In ChatGPT, open the answer that shows the harrisonresort.com image, click through to its cited source, and find where the image originates. It is usually a listing, an aggregator, or an OTA page. Fix it at the source, and use ChatGPT's feedback option on the answer to report the incorrect image.
   - Make sure the correct image is the one every source offers: the Google Business Profile primary photo, the website's Open Graph image, the `image` property in your Hotel schema, and the lead image on TripAdvisor, Expedia, and Booking.com.
   - Claim and complete a Bing Places listing and an Apple Business Connect listing if you have not, since AI search tools draw on Bing and other sources as well as Google, and match the name, address, phone, and description exactly to the Google Business Profile.
   - Retest the same prompt two weeks later. Corrections can take a few weeks to propagate, so keep a note of what you changed and when.

8. **Test your AI visibility every month with a fixed set of prompts.**
   - Build a list of 15 prompts and use the same wording every time: "best place to hold a meeting in Harrison," "best pet-friendly hotel in Harrison Hot Springs," "where to stay in Harrison Hot Springs in winter," "family hotel Harrison Hot Springs," "things to do near Harrison Hot Springs," and so on across the five clusters.
   - Run them in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews. In a Google Sheet, record whether Harrison Beach Hotel is named, its position, which URL is cited, whether the details are accurate, and which OTAs appear.
   - Run the baseline in Week 2, before any new content publishes, then again in Weeks 6, 10, and 12. The change between those tests is the evidence that the hub is working.
   - Protect the number-one meetings ranking. That page and its supporting content are an asset, so check that prompt every month and do not change the page without testing the effect.
   - In Google Analytics, check referrals from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, and similar sources each month. AI referrals are small but growing, and they tend to convert well.

### Tips
- Specific questions beat broad topics every time. "Is Harrison Beach Hotel pet friendly?" will be cited far more often than a general article on pet travel, because it matches how a person asks an AI tool.
- Do not let a draft publish with a placeholder or a guess. Policies, fees, and distances are the first things an AI tool repeats, and a wrong figure travels fast. One owner for fact-checking is worth the time.
- The OTAs are not going away, and they are not the enemy in AI search. The goal is to be the most specific answer, with a direct booking path from every page, so that the traveller who reads about the hotel books on your site.
- If you restart ChatGPT Ads, send the traffic to the cluster pages that match the question being asked, not to the homepage. The content is built for that moment.
- Review the oldest posts every quarter. A post that says the pool is open until 9 PM and then the hours change is a liability, and updating a page is much cheaper than publishing a new one.

## Tactic 2: High-Volume Social Content System
Phase: Content and Audience
Short Label: Social Content
Subtitle: Build a bank of pillar content and a repeatable posting system across Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts so the 23,000-follower audience finally works for the hotel.

### What We Heard From You

You have a social and marketing coordinator who joined in the summer and handles content, and Nicole and Megan contribute photos and videos, including the pet-friendly Bernese video that is exactly the kind of content that works. Katie manages the ads. The assessment found 23,206 followers across Instagram and Facebook, a 0.34 percent engagement rate, 2.1 posts a week, Reels at only a third of the mix, and almost no comments. You named Instagram as the biggest opportunity, and on the call the point was clear: algorithms now show people content from accounts they do not follow, so reach comes from volume and consistency across many platforms, not from perfecting individual posts.

### What This Is

A system with four parts: eight brand pillars with ten content ideas each, a brand voice brief and set of templates that let Claude and Canva produce on-brand drafts quickly, a weekly capture and batching routine that fills the content bank, and a publishing and engagement rhythm that runs across Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. The same video goes everywhere, and winning posts are handed to your paid campaigns.

### Why It Matters

The average person in North America uses seven platforms and sees thousands of ads a day, so a hotel that posts twice a week is invisible by default. Big brands win through repetition, and the same logic applies at your scale: hit the audience with the same message many times over a short period, in many places, until the brand is familiar. The assessment's recommendation of five to seven posts a week with 60 percent Reels and daily Stories is the right target, and it is only sustainable if the content is produced in batches from a bank rather than invented each morning. The engagement routine matters just as much, because the algorithm reads early comments and saves as the signal to show a post to more people.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Pillars and the Content Bank

1. **Run the 45-minute pillar session and fill the bank.**
   - Get Katie, the coordinator, Nicole, and Megan in a room for 45 minutes. Agree on eight pillars, then write ten content ideas under each. That is 80 ideas, which is about three months of content at the volume below, and it can be done in a single sitting if you keep it fast.
   - The eight pillars that fit your guests and goals:

   | Pillar | What It Covers | Example Idea |
   |--------|----------------|--------------|
   | The Room and the View | Renovated rooms, lake views, kitchenettes, room tours | 20-second room tour ending on the lake view |
   | Pet-Friendly Stays | Dogs at the hotel, welcome touches, nearby trails | The Bernese video, plus "what we give every dog at check-in" |
   | Pool, Hot Tub, and Cozy | Pool, hot tub, winter evenings, relaxation | Steam rising off the hot tub on a cold night |
   | Lake and Local Adventures | Beach, trails, Sasquatch Provincial Park, day trips | "Three things to do within 15 minutes of the hotel" |
   | Winter in Harrison | Lights on the Lake, ski packages, cozy escapes | Ski day to hot tub night, in one Reel |
   | Guests and Team | Reviews, Nicole and Megan, loyal guests, behind the scenes | A real guest review over a photo of the room they loved |
   | Meetings and Gatherings | The meeting space, small receptions, corporate retreats | A set-up timelapse of the room from empty to ready |
   | Packages and Partners | Ski and stay, the family adventure package, the cafe B&B offer | What a stay with the water park passes actually looks like |

   - Write the ideas as a headline and a shot, not as finished posts. "Room 214 sunset, 15-second pan" is enough. The finished version happens in the weekly batch.
   - Keep the list in one shared Google Sheet with a column for status, so anyone on the team can pick up an idea and know what has already been shot or posted.

2. **Build the brand voice brief and the templates.**
   - Write a one-page brand voice brief that Claude and anyone on the team can use. Include who the hotel is, the three main guest types, the tone in three adjectives, words you use and words you avoid, a short library of verified facts (check-in time, pet policy, parking, pool hours, the direct-booking perks), and five example captions you consider perfectly on-brand.
   - Load the brief into a Claude project so every caption draft starts from it. The brief is what keeps the content from sounding like generic AI copy, and it is the reason the team can scale volume without losing authenticity.
   - Add an authenticity rule: every post includes one real detail that only someone at the hotel would know. It could be a guest's reaction, a team member's tip, or the exact view from a specific room.
   - Build the visual templates once, in Canva or in the design system you saw on the call: a Reel cover, a carousel layout, a Story template, and a quote card for guest reviews. Save them in a shared brand kit. The coordinator and Megan should be able to produce a post without asking for a design.

3. **Set up the weekly capture routine.**
   - Block 30 minutes a week for Nicole or Megan to capture raw material using the shot list from the content bank: room details, the pool, guests who have agreed to be featured, pet arrivals, sunsets, and team moments. Phone video in natural light is fine.
   - Get consent for guests and their pets before posting, ideally with a simple line at check-in or a verbal yes recorded in the notes. Never post an identifiable guest without it.
   - Upload every clip the same day to a shared drive with one folder per pillar. File names should include the pillar and date.
   - Capture Lights on the Lake content this week, since it is your current focus and will be at its best only for a limited time.

#### Part B: Publish at Volume Across Platforms

4. **Set the cadence and a weekly batching rhythm.**
   - The target from the assessment is five to seven feed posts a week on Instagram with 60 percent Reels, and daily Stories of seven to ten a week. Facebook gets the same content, and Google Business Profile gets a weekly post from the same pool.
   - Batch it in one session a week, ideally Monday. The coordinator pulls the week's ideas from the bank, drafts captions with Claude using the brand voice brief, builds the visuals from the templates, and schedules everything. Katie or Nicole reviews it in five minutes.
   - Use Meta Business Suite (free) to schedule Instagram and Facebook together. For TikTok and YouTube Shorts, use Buffer or Later, or the native schedulers in each app, so the same video goes everywhere without manual posting.
   - Stories are the exception to batching. They should be spontaneous and take 10 to 15 minutes a day: weather, the view, a dog arriving, something happening in the lobby.

5. **Launch TikTok and YouTube Shorts using the same content.**
   - Create business accounts on both, with a bio that matches the Google Business Profile and a link to the hotel's direct booking page with a UTM tag.
   - Post the same Reels to TikTok and YouTube Shorts, with a native caption written for each platform and the first two seconds reworked as a stronger hook if needed. Download the original file rather than re-recording the Instagram version so there is no watermark.
   - Start at three to five posts a week on each. Both platforms are growing fastest among older audiences, which is the group that books multi-night stays.
   - Do not wait for perfect footage. The first 20 videos are how the team learns what works.

6. **Create Story Highlights and tidy the profile.**
   - Build Instagram Highlights for Rooms, Pool and Hot Tub, Location, Guest Reviews, and Pets. The assessment flagged this as a quick win, since it acts as a permanent visual FAQ for visitors arriving from a Reel.
   - Update the Instagram and Facebook bios to match the positioning statement, with a link to the direct booking page. Use the same profile photo and handle style on all platforms.
   - Pin three posts that do the most work: the best room tour, the best guest review, and the direct-booking offer.

#### Part C: Engagement and Reputation

7. **Run a daily 30-minute engagement routine.**
   - In the first hour after posting, reply to every comment. The assessment found the average post receives zero comments, so every reply and every question you ask in a caption is a signal the algorithm notices. End every caption with a question, and use polls and question stickers in Stories.
   - Answer every DM within the same day. A message is a hot lead for a hotel.
   - Respond to Google reviews within 48 hours, which moves the response rate from about 60 percent to 90 percent or more. Prioritise one- to three-star reviews first, then recent five-star ones. Write three templates for common themes (staff praise, renovation compliments, a slow check-in) and edit each one so it sounds personal.
   - Pass the slow check-in feedback to operations. A reply that acknowledges it is good, and a fix that reduces it is better, and Tactic 3 includes a pre-arrival message that helps.

8. **Use performance data and hand the winners to ads.**
   - Every week, look at the top three posts by reach and by saves, and note the pillar, the format, the hook, and the caption style. Make five more like each one.
   - Every month, hand the best-performing organic Reels to Katie for the existing Meta campaigns, with a Book Direct call to action and a UTM-tagged link. Posts that have already earned engagement almost always cost less to promote than new creative.
   - Keep a simple log of results by pillar. After 90 days, you will know which two or three pillars earn the most attention and which ideas to drop.

### Success Metrics
| Metric | 30 Days | 60 Days | 90 Days |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| Posting frequency | 5 feed posts a week on Instagram and Facebook; Stories most days | 5-7 posts a week sustained; 7+ Stories a week | 5-7 posts a week with no missed weeks; coordinator running the batch independently |
| Reels share | 50% of feed posts are Reels | 60% | 60% sustained |
| Instagram engagement | 0.34% baseline recorded; trending up | 0.7%-1% | 1.5% or higher |
| Comments and replies | Replying to every comment within 1 hour of posting | Comments per post rising from near zero | Comments and saves are the main growth metric reviewed weekly |
| TikTok and YouTube Shorts | Both accounts live with 8+ videos each | 30+ videos each | At least one video above 5,000 views |
| Google review response rate | 80% | 90% | 90%+ maintained, all reviews answered within 48 hours |
| Content bank | 80 ideas written and 30+ captured clips | 4 weeks of content ahead | 6 weeks ahead; Nicole and Megan capture weekly |

### Tips
- Volume is the strategy. A decent post that goes out is worth more than a perfect post that does not, and with the bank and templates in place the coordinator is making fast decisions, not creative ones.
- The Bernese video is the model. It is specific, real, and visibly loved, and it came from the team doing their normal job. Make a point of celebrating the next ones to encourage Nicole and Megan to keep capturing.
- Write captions with Claude, but keep the final edit human. Read each one out loud. If it could have been written by any hotel, add the detail only your team knows.
- Winter content should shift to cozy: the hot tub on a cold night, the lights, ski days that end at the hotel. The assessment points out the summer lake and beach lifestyle is not what sells in January.
- Do not post more than the team can respond to. If comments and messages are piling up, slow the posting before the replies slip, since replies are a large part of why the algorithm shows the post to others.

## Tactic 3: Direct Booking, Midweek Demand, and Meetings
Phase: Conversion and Revenue
Short Label: Direct Bookings
Subtitle: Make the direct-booking advantage impossible to miss, build packages and campaigns that fill midweek and shoulder season, and turn the number-one meetings ranking into enquiries.

### What We Heard From You

Your direct to OTA split has historically been 80/20, but it crept to about 30 percent OTA in August and September, and you want it back. You already have strong direct perks that are not being fully used: flexible cancellation where the OTAs are non-refundable, a best rate guarantee that is not prominently displayed on the website, and more personal pre-arrival communication. You also have partnerships worth packaging, with Sasquatch Mountain Resort for ski, a local water park for a family adventure package, and Harrison Lillooet Cafe for a voucher and B&B offer, since the hotel has no on-site restaurant. Midweek and shoulder occupancy are the other priority, and you want to grow the meetings and events business, where a space of about 30 people works for small receptions and corporate meetings.

### What This Is

The conversion layer that sits between the content in Tactics 1 and 2 and the booking engine. It surfaces the direct-booking benefits where guests decide, packages the partnerships into offers with their own pages and tracking links, uses those pages as the destination for targeted midweek and shoulder season campaigns, and adds a proper meetings page with an enquiry path. It ends with a simple monthly measure of direct versus OTA share so you can see the effect.

### Why It Matters

The assessment scored the booking experience at 99 out of 100, so the mechanics are not the problem. The problem is that guests comparing options on an OTA never see why booking direct is better, and the direct perks that are real are not being said at the point of decision. Every OTA booking costs commission, and every point of share you move back to direct goes straight to the owners' revenue pool. Packages and landing pages give your paid campaigns somewhere specific to send traffic, and a dedicated meetings page converts the ranking ChatGPT already gives you into business that is less seasonal than leisure travel.

### Implementation Steps

#### Part A: Make the Direct Advantage Impossible to Miss

1. **Show the direct perks next to every Book Now button.**
   - Add a short block near the Book Now button on the homepage, room pages, and the booking confirmation: "Book direct: flexible cancellation, our best rate guarantee, and a personal pre-arrival touch from our team." The assessment recommends direct booking incentive messaging near the call to action, and your best rate guarantee is not currently prominent.
   - Add a "Why book direct" section to the FAQ page and as one question post in the winter cluster, comparing the direct terms with the typical OTA terms, in plain language and without naming competitors.
   - Test two versions of the wording for two weeks each and compare the booking rate. The assessment notes the booking infrastructure is strong, so the copy is the variable worth testing.
   - Make sure the same line appears in your Google Business Profile description, the website's meta description for the booking pages, and the footer of every confirmation email.

2. **Build packages with your partners and give each one its own page and link.**
   - Create three package pages: Ski and Stay with Sasquatch Mountain Resort, the Family Adventure package with the water park passes, and the Stay and Eat offer with Harrison Lillooet Cafe, framed as B&B-style vouchers. Each page has one H1, a plain description of what is included, the price or the savings, the booking link, and a short FAQ. [INSERT PACKAGE TERMS, INCLUSIONS, AND VALIDITY DATES FOR EACH]
   - Give every package a unique UTM-tagged booking link and a short code the front desk can use, so you can track bookings by package and by channel.
   - Hold off on promoting the hot springs partnership. It is paused because of inconsistent hours and slow payments, and a package that depends on it risks the guest experience. Revisit it once the vendor is reliable.
   - Add a midweek offer for January and February, since this is when occupancy is thinnest. Keep it direct-only, with the same flexible cancellation, so it reinforces the direct advantage.

#### Part B: Fill Midweek and Shoulder Season

3. **Build segment campaigns that send traffic to the package and cluster pages.**
   - The Prairie winter escape is the clearest segment: visitors from Saskatchewan and Manitoba who come after harvest, and whose stays are longer and more flexible. Build one landing page for them, with the winter cluster content, the ski package, and a midweek rate, and run Meta and Google campaigns targeting adults 40 and older in those two provinces as a starting audience to test.
   - Use the landing page and the winning organic Reels from Tactic 2 as the creative. Content that has already earned engagement is cheaper to promote and has a stronger message.
   - Keep your existing Facebook retargeting running and add the package pages to the audiences, so someone who read the ski page sees the ski offer. Landing pages come first and ads second, because ads sent to a general page lose the message.
   - Review each campaign every week for the first month and every two weeks after that. Cut what does not produce enquiries and move the budget to what does.

4. **Improve the pre-arrival and post-stay emails.**
   - Pre-arrival is a direct-booking perk and the place to fix the slow check-in feedback. ### Tips
- The perks are already real, so this tactic is about saying them. The quickest gain is putting the best rate guarantee and flexible cancellation next to the Book Now button.
- Do not discount on public rates. The offers that work against the OTAs are the ones that add value or flexibility for direct guests: a package, a perk, an extra night, a late check-out. A package is also harder to compare to an OTA listing.
- Packages depend on partners being reliable. Confirm the terms and the contact at the ski resort, the water park, and the cafe, and put the details in writing, before the page goes live.
- When a guest books by phone or email after reading a page, ask how they found you. A five-second question gives you information the analytics will not.
- Meetings are a slower sale than a room night, so measure the outreach by enquiries and conversations. A single recurring corporate client can be worth more than many leisure nights.
